Fear of failure and success

Staying out of the attempt because you dread falling short, or dread what succeeding would bring.

Philosophical lens

Effects on life

Aristotle describes what undue humility costs. Nicomachean Ethics 4.3: "the unduly humble man, being worthy of good things, robs himself of what he deserves".

He adds that such a person "seems also not to know himself".

Epictetus describes what follows when we put our weight on things outside our control. Enchiridion 1: "then you will be hindered. You will lament, you will be disturbed, and you will find fault both with gods and men."

This is related to fear of failure and success and not identical to it. Aristotle is speaking of a person who rates themselves too low, and Epictetus of disturbance in general; neither is a study of what fear of failure does. Reading them together is ours.
