Fear of failure and success

Staying out of the attempt because you dread falling short, or dread what succeeding would bring.

Effects on life

The parable of the talents shows what the fear costs. The two servants who traded with their money are told: "Well done, good and trustworthy slave; you have been trustworthy in a few things; I will put you in charge of many things; enter into the joy of your master" (Matthew 25:21, 23).

The servant who buried his talent returns it unchanged, and the master says: "So take the talent from him, and give it to the one with the ten talents" (25:28).

This is related to fear of failure and success and not identical to it. The parable's judgement on the third servant is severe, and it should not be read as a threat to anyone who is afraid. It shows a talent that gained nothing because it was hidden, and that reading is ours.
