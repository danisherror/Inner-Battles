The Stoics' advice is to take on contests you can win by your own effort, and to make the obstacle part of the work.

1. **Choose contests you control.** Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 19: "You may be unconquerable, if you enter into no combat in which it is not in your own control to conquer."
2. **Wish for what happens.** *Enchiridion* 8: "Don't demand that things happen as you wish, but wish that they happen as they do happen, and you will go on well."
3. **Turn the hindrance into help.** Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 5: "the mind converts and changes every hindrance to its activity into an aid; and so that which is a hindrance is made a furtherance to an act; and that which is an obstacle on the road helps us on this road."

This is related to fear of failure and success and not identical to it. These are practices for effort and obstacles in general, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for constant anxiety or low mood. Epictetus's "unconquerable" is about not entering contests you cannot win, and it is not advice to avoid every risk.
