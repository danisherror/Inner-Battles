The Buddhist texts teach looking at gain and loss as passing, and not siding with either.

1. **Reflect.** AN 8.6: "They reflect: 'I've encountered this gain. It's impermanent, suffering, and perishable.' They truly understand it." The sutta says the same of loss, fame, disgrace, blame, praise, pleasure and pain.
2. **Do not favour or oppose.** "They don't favor gain or oppose loss. They don't favor fame or oppose disgrace. They don't favor praise or oppose blame."
3. **Stay steady.** The verses of AN 8.5 say of the wise: "Desirable things don't disturb their mind, nor are they repelled by the undesirable."

This is related to fear of failure and success and not identical to it. The suttas are about meeting gain and loss in general, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for constant anxiety or low mood. Reading them as advice for the dread of an attempt is ours.
