The research read here is about what goes with fear of failure, and it does not test a treatment, so what follows is mostly common advice.

1. **Name what you fear will happen.** "I will look foolish" and "I will be noticed and then fall" are different fears.
2. **Shrink the attempt.** Pick a version small enough to finish, and finish it.
3. **Write down what success and failure would each mean.** Include what you would learn from the second.
4. **For fear of success, list the feared costs.** Check each one: is it likely, and what would you do about it?
5. **Separate your worth from the result.** Boileau and colleagues argue that fear of failure can also push people to try. It can be treated as information about what matters to you, and it does not have to be a verdict.
6. **Look at who is judging.** Moreno-Murcia and colleagues found that "coach autonomy support was associated with reduced fear of failure". That is an association, but it suggests asking for feedback that helps you decide, and not only for judgement.
7. **Build resilience.** Yıldırım and colleagues suggest the harm "can be mitigated by cultivating resilience and hindering extrinsic motivation". That is the authors' suggestion from a correlational study.
8. **Get help if it is constant.** A doctor or therapist can help when the fear comes with constant anxiety, low mood or a pattern you cannot break.

**Sources**

- Boileau et al. (2025), *Journal of Personality Assessment* 108(2), 235-249. [doi:10.1080/00223891.2025.2555358](https://doi.org/10.1080/00223891.2025.2555358)
- Moreno-Murcia et al. (2019), *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health* 16(9), 1563. [doi:10.3390/ijerph16091563](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16091563)
- Yıldırım et al. (2023), *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health* 20(10), 5895. [doi:10.3390/ijerph20105895](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph20105895)

All three were read in the abstracts (Europe PMC, 2026-11-14), not in full. None tested a way of reducing fear of failure. Steps 1 to 4 and 8 are common advice, not tied to a study read here; step 5 is our reading of Boileau et al.
