A story

Nachiketas and the offered riches

In the Katha Upanishad, Nachiketas has asked Death what lies after dying. Death tries to turn him aside with everything a person might want: "Choose sons and grandsons who shall live a hundred years, herds of cattle, elephants, gold, and horses. Choose the wide abode of the earth, and live thyself as many harvests as thou desirest" (1.1.23). He adds: "be waited on by them whom I give to thee, but do not ask me about dying" (1.1.25).

Nachiketas answers: "These things last till tomorrow, O Death, for they wear out this vigour of all the senses. Even the whole of life is short. Keep thou thy horses, keep dance and song for thyself. No man can be made happy by wealth" (1.1.26-27). He says again that he wants only the answer he asked for.

What it shows: Nachiketas is offered every kind of worldly success and turns it down for what he came to learn. It is a story about desire and knowledge, and not about fear of success; the link is ours, and it shows a person who is not carried off by what success would bring.
