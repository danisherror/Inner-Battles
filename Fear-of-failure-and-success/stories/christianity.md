**Elijah after the victory**

In 1 Kings 19 Elijah has just done what he set out to do. Ahab tells his queen "all that Elijah had done and how he had killed all the prophets with the sword." Jezebel sends a message: "So may the gods do to me and more also, if I do not make your life like the life of one of them by this time tomorrow" (19:1-2).

"Then he was afraid; he got up and fled for his life." He goes a day's journey into the wilderness and sits under a broom tree. "He asked that he might die, 'It is enough; now, O Lord, take away my life, for I am no better than my ancestors'" (19:3-4).

He lies down and sleeps. An angel touches him and says: "Get up and eat." There is "a cake baked on hot stones and a jar of water". The angel comes a second time: "Get up and eat, or the journey will be too much for you." He eats, and "went in the strength of that food forty days and forty nights to Horeb the mount of God" (19:5-8).

**What it shows:** Elijah's fear comes right after a victory, and what he is given first is food and sleep before any argument. The story is about a prophet and a threat and not about fear of success as such; the link is ours, in that success did not bring him peace.
