Fear of failure and success

Staying out of the attempt because you dread falling short, or dread what succeeding would bring.

A story

The Buddha changes course

In MN 36 the Buddha tells of the years before his awakening. After severe austerities he thinks: "this is as far as it goes, no-one has done more than this. But I have not achieved any superhuman distinction in knowledge and vision worthy of the noble ones by this severe, grueling work. Could there be another path to awakening?"

He remembers a pleasant meditation in childhood under a tree. Then the fear: "Why am I afraid of that pleasure, for it has nothing to do with sensual pleasures or unskillful qualities?" He answers himself: "I'm not afraid of that pleasure". He sees he will need strength: "I can't achieve that pleasure with a body so severely emaciated. Why don't I eat some solid food, some rice and porridge?"

The cost comes at once. The five mendicants who had been attending him "left disappointed in me, saying, 'The ascetic Gotama has become indulgent; he has strayed from the struggle and returned to indulgence.'"

What it shows: The Buddha counts the failure of one method, asks whether he fears the better one, and changes course although others think less of him for it. The sutta is about the path to awakening and not about fear of failure or success; the link is ours.
