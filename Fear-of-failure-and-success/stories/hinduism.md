**Arjuna lays down his bow**

At the start of the Gita Arjuna cannot decide what to do. He tells Krishna: "We do not even know which result of this war is preferable for us—conquering them or being conquered by them" (2.6). Then, "Having thus spoken," he says: "Govind, I shall not fight," and falls silent (2.9).

Krishna's reply runs through the chapter. One part is: "Fight for the sake of duty, treating alike happiness and distress, loss and gain, victory and defeat" (2.38). Another: "Be steadfast in the performance of your duty, O Arjun, abandoning attachment to success and failure. Such equanimity is called Yog" (2.48).

**What it shows:** Arjuna is stopped by not knowing which outcome would be better, and Krishna's answer is not to promise a result but to change what he is holding on to. The Gita is about duty in war and not about fear of failure as such; the story is read here for how the question of outcome is answered.
