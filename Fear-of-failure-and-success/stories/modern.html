Fear of failure and success

Staying out of the attempt because you dread falling short, or dread what succeeding would bring.

A story

Note: This is an invented composite story, not a real case. It is built from patterns people commonly describe, so you can see how these fears work and how people loosen their grip.

Jonas's story

Jonas, a freelance designer, had a portfolio people praised. When a large company offered him a six-month project, he said he would think about it, then let the deadline pass.

He told himself he was busy. In truth two thoughts were taking turns. One was "I will let them down and everyone will see". The other was less welcome: "If it goes well I will be expected to do this every time, and I will have to charge more, and some people will resent it."

What helped:

  1. He named both fears. Written side by side they looked different: one about falling short, one about what would follow success.
  2. He shrank the attempt. He wrote to the company and asked for a two-week trial phase.
  3. He listed what each outcome would mean. A failed trial would cost him two weeks and teach him what the company needed. A good one would mean a harder conversation about pricing, which he could prepare for.
  4. He asked for feedback that helped him decide. "What would make the first deliverable useful to you?" replaced "is it good enough?"
  5. He noticed it was not lifting. When the dread spread to every new client and cost him sleep, he spoke to his doctor, who referred him to a counsellor.

The fear did not vanish. It got smaller, and the trial phase went ahead.

What it shows: Jonas was afraid of two different things, and each needed its own answer. Shrinking the attempt and asking what each outcome would mean did more than waiting to feel ready.
