**Marcus Aurelius gets out of bed**

Marcus Aurelius is not telling a story in *Meditations* 5, but the opening is a scene. "In the morning when thou risest unwillingly, let this thought be present- I am rising to the work of a human being. Why then am I dissatisfied if I am going to do the things for which I exist and for which I was brought into the world?"

He argues with the voice that wants to stay under the covers. "Or have I been made for this, to lie in the bed-clothes and keep myself warm?- But this is more pleasant.- Dost thou exist then to take thy pleasure, and not at all for action or exertion?"

**What it shows:** Marcus does not wait to feel ready; he reminds himself what the work is for and gets up. The passage is about rising and duty and not about fear of failure or success; the link is ours, and it is that action begins before the feeling does.
