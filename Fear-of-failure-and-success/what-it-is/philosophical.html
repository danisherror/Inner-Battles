Fear of failure and success

Staying out of the attempt because you dread falling short, or dread what succeeding would bring.

Philosophical lens

What it is

Aristotle names a version of fear of success: claiming less than you deserve. In the Nicomachean Ethics (4.3): "The man who thinks himself worthy of worthy of less than he is really worthy of is unduly humble, whether his deserts be great or moderate".

He adds: "And the man whose deserts are great would seem most unduly humble; for what would he have done if they had been less?"

This is related to fear of failure and success and not identical to it. Aristotle is speaking of honour and of rating oneself rightly, and not of fear. Reading undue humility as one face of fear of success is ours.
