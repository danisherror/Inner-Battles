Fear of failure is dread of not succeeding, and of what that would cost you or say about you. Fear of success is less obvious: it means associating success with negative consequences, such as more pressure, envy or change. Both can lead to the same place, which is staying out of the attempt.

Researchers have studied both. Kosakowska-Berezecka and colleagues asked students to complete scales measuring "their associations with success (fear of success) and tendency for self-promotion", and note that avoiding self-promotion "is also potentially related to associating success with negative consequences".

Fear of failure is not only harmful. Boileau and colleagues developed a scale because existing measures "typically overlook" its motivational role "in favor of its negative effects". Teevan and McGhee's 1972 paper, "Childhood development of fear of failure motivation", is an early study of how it forms.

Everyday signs, as people commonly describe them and not as findings from these papers: putting off an application, starting many things and finishing few, turning down the promotion, or undermining your own work the night before it is due. See also [perfectionism](../../Perfectionism/what-it-is/modern.html) and [procrastination](../../Procrastination/what-it-is/modern.html).

This is not a diagnosis. If the fear comes with constant anxiety or low mood, a doctor or therapist can help.
