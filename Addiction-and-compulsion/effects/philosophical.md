Aristotle describes what follows the act. *Nicomachean Ethics* 7.8: "incontinent man is likely to repent." He draws a contrast with the self-indulgent man, who "stands by his choice".

He also says: "the incontinent man curable".

Plato's picture gives the cost of the struggle: "the driving of them of necessity gives a great deal of trouble to him."

This is related to addiction and not identical to it. Aristotle's "curable" is about weakness of will and not a medical claim, and the passage compares the two kinds of person in terms the site does not adopt. Reading these as effects of addiction is ours. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and a doctor can help with stopping safely.
