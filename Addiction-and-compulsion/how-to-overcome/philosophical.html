How to overcome it

The philosophers' counsel is to know which horse is pulling, and to decide before the pull.

  1. Name the pull. Plato, Phaedrus: "one of them is noble and of noble breed, and the other is ignoble and of ignoble breed".
  2. Decide before the moment. Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics 7.7: the impetuous "do not await the argument, because they are apt to follow their imagination." So the argument is best made early.
  3. Let regret lead somewhere. Nicomachean Ethics 7.8: "incontinent man is likely to repent."

This is related to addiction and not identical to it. Aristotle is writing about weakness of will, and step 2 is the site's own reading of his remark about those who do not wait for the argument. Plato's "ignoble" horse is an image in the text, and neither should be read as calling a person ignoble. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and a doctor can help with stopping safely.
