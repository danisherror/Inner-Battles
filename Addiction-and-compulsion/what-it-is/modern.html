What it is

Addiction is a repeated pull toward a substance or an activity, such as alcohol, drugs, gambling, a phone or pornography, that goes on despite a wish to stop and despite the harm. Compulsion is the sense that the act is driven and not chosen. Those are standard descriptions and not taken from the papers below.

Researchers disagree about what to call it. Leshner writes that "drug addiction is a chronic, relapsing disease that results from the prolonged effects of drugs on the brain", with "behavioral and social-context aspects" that "are important parts of the disorder itself". Lewis describes Heyman's view differently: "Rather than view addiction as a disease, Heyman sees it as a choice-one that works like other choices, whereby immediate rewards outshine long-term gains." Lewis himself argues that "the disease-versus-choice debate creates a false dichotomy". These abstracts are about drugs and alcohol, and do not cover phones or gambling.

Everyday signs, as people commonly describe them and not as findings from these papers: doing more than you meant, failed attempts to cut down, hiding it, and craving when a cue appears. It overlaps with impulsivity and distraction.

Struggling to stop is common, and it is nothing to be ashamed of. If you are finding it hard, please talk to a doctor, therapist or a local support service. Stopping some substances suddenly, including alcohol and certain medicines, can be dangerous, so ask a doctor before stopping on your own (a standard safety point, not drawn from the papers below). If you are in danger, or thinking of harming yourself, contact your local emergency services now; the International Association for Suicide Prevention points to a directory of verified helplines in over 150 countries (external link).
