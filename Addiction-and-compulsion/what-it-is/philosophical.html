What it is

Aristotle describes acting against what you know. Nicomachean Ethics 7.1: "the incontinent man, knowing that what he does is bad, does it as a result of passion, while the continent man, knowing that his appetites are bad, refuses on account of his rational principle to follow them".

Plato gives the picture. Phaedrus: "the human charioteer drives his in a pair; and one of them is noble and of noble breed, and the other is ignoble and of ignoble breed; and the driving of them of necessity gives a great deal of trouble to him."

This is related to addiction and not identical to it. Aristotle's "incontinence" (the Greek word is akrasia, weakness of will) is about acting against one's judgment and not about a medical condition, and Plato's image is of the soul in general. Reading them as an account of addiction is ours. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and a doctor can help with stopping safely.
