**The taste that remains**

Gita 2.59 speaks of someone who has restrained the senses: "Aspirants may restrain the senses from their objects of enjoyment, but the taste for the sense objects remains. However, even this taste ceases for those who realize the Supreme."

The commentary explains: "When one gives up eating, as in a fast, the desires of the senses become feeble. Similarly, in sickness one loses interest toward the objects of enjoyment. These states of dispassion are temporary, for the seed of desire remains within the mind."

**What it shows:** The text expects the taste to remain after the act is stopped, and does not treat that as failure. This is a picture drawn from one verse and its commentary and not a tale of a person, and linking it to addiction is ours. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and a doctor can help with stopping safely.
