Plato locates the trouble in the mixed nature of the soul. *Phaedrus*: "the human charioteer drives his in a pair; and one of them is noble and of noble breed, and the other is ignoble and of ignoble breed; and the driving of them of necessity gives a great deal of trouble to him."

Aristotle says who is most at risk of acting before thinking: "It is keen and excitable people that suffer especially from the impetuous form of incontinence; for the former by reason of their quickness and the latter by reason of the violence of their passions do not await the argument, because they are apt to follow their imagination."

This is related to addiction and not identical to it. Plato's image is of the soul and Aristotle's of weakness of will, and neither is about dependence on a substance. Reading them as roots of addiction is ours. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and a doctor can help with stopping safely.
