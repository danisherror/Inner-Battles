The sources read here offer accounts of why it happens, and they disagree.

- **The brain's response to repeated use.** Leshner: addiction "results from the prolonged effects of drugs on the brain". Lewis: "the salience of drug-related cues is enhanced by dopamine activity in the ventral striatum, orbitofrontal cortex, and amygdala, due to the intense hedonic impact of repeated drug experiences", and "dopamine-based craving peaks when drug (or alcohol or gambling) rewards become available, in the moment, and this rapid increase in attractiveness preempts rational judgment."
- **Choice, with the odds tilted.** Lewis calls addiction "a recurrent series of choices that permit negotiation, and sometimes cooperation, between immediate and long-range goals."
- **A challenge to the dopamine account.** Heyman writes that "there is more to the biology of addiction than dopamine", and cites an experiment in which "rats readily gave up cocaine for saccharin, even when they had been consuming massive amounts of the drug for weeks."

Common causes, as people describe them and not findings from these abstracts: stress, trauma, loneliness, easy access, and family history. None of this makes anyone to blame. Struggling to stop is common, and it is nothing to be ashamed of. If you are finding it hard, please talk to a doctor, therapist or a local support service. Stopping some substances suddenly, including alcohol and certain medicines, can be dangerous, so ask a doctor before stopping on your own (a standard safety point, not drawn from the papers below). If you are in danger, or thinking of harming yourself, contact your local emergency services now; [the International Association for Suicide Prevention points to a directory of verified helplines in over 150 countries](https://www.iasp.info/crisis-centres-helplines/).
