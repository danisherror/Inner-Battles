The philosophers' counsel is to examine yourself, and to be your own accuser first.

1. **Examine your life.** Plato's *Apology*: "the life which is unexamined is not worth living".
2. **Be your own accuser.** Seneca, *Letters* 28: "prove yourself guilty, hunt up charges against yourself; play the part, first of accuser, then of judge, last of intercessor. At times be harsh with yourself."
3. **Start with what you do not know.** Socrates: "I neither know nor think that I know."

This is related to denial and not identical to it. These are one school's disciplines, and Seneca's advice to be harsh with yourself should be read beside [Self-criticism](../../Self-criticism/what-it-is/modern.html), which describes how a harsh inner voice can do harm. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist.
