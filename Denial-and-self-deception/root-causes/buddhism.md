The Buddhist root is that we see others' faults more easily than our own. Dhp 252: "It's easy to see the faults of others, hard to see one's own. For the faults of others are tossed high like hay, while one's own are hidden, as a cheat hides a bad hand."

AN 4.49 puts the root in a deeper error: "Yoked by Māra's yoke, these people find no sanctuary from the yoke."

This is related to denial and not identical to it. Dhp 252 is about noticing faults in oneself and others, and AN 4.49 is about wrong view. Reading them as roots of self-deception is ours.
