**Rāhula and the water pot**

In MN 61 the Buddha visits his son Rāhula, who sets out water for his feet. The Buddha leaves "a little water in the pot" and asks: "Rāhula, do you see this little bit of water left in the pot?" "Yes, sir." "That's how little of the ascetic's nature is left in those who are not ashamed to tell a deliberate lie."

He throws the water away: "That's how the ascetic's nature is discarded in those who are not ashamed to tell a deliberate lie." He turns the pot upside down, then upright and empty: "That's how vacant and hollow the ascetic's nature is in those who are not ashamed to tell a deliberate lie."

Then he asks: "What is the purpose of a mirror?" "It's for checking your reflection, sir." "In the same way, deeds of body, speech, and mind should be done only after repeated checking."

**What it shows:** The Buddha moves from a pot to a mirror, from the cost of lying to the habit of looking at one's own acts. The sutta is about lying and the checking of deeds and not about self-deception as such; the link is ours.
