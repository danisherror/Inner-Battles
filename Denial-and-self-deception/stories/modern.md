> **Note:** This is an invented composite story, not a real case. It is built from patterns people commonly describe, so you can see how denial works and how people loosen its grip.

**Anil's story**

Anil opened a small shop with his savings. For two years it lost money. Each month he said it was a slow season, then that the street was being dug up, then that the next festival would fix it. When his sister asked how it was going he said "fine" and changed the subject.

He knew, when he looked at the accounts, and he often did not look. What helped:

1. **He noticed the discomfort.** The tightness in his chest when his sister asked was the sign that something was being pushed away.
2. **He wrote down the facts.** Rent, sales and what he had put in, for each month, with no commentary.
3. **He asked someone he trusted.** His sister said what she had seen, without blame, and he listened before answering.
4. **He looked ahead.** He asked what the story would sound like in a year if nothing changed.
5. **He went at his own pace.** He took a month to decide, and spoke to an adviser about the debts.
6. **He kept his dignity.** He closed the shop and kept the lessons, and told people honestly why.

The disappointment was real. The two years of "next month" had cost more than the truth did.

**What it shows:** Anil did not have to be harsh with himself. Looking at the accounts, and letting someone else look with him, did what a good story had not.
