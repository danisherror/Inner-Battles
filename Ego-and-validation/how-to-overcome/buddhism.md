The Buddhist texts counsel seeing every condition as passing.

1. **Reflect on each condition.** AN 8.6: "A learned noble disciple encounters gain. They reflect: 'I've encountered this gain. It's impermanent, suffering, and perishable.' They truly understand it." The same is said of praise and blame.
2. **Stop favouring and opposing.** AN 8.6: "They don't favor praise or oppose blame."
3. **Remember nobody is all one thing.** Dhp 228: "There never was, nor will be, nor is there today, someone who is wholly praised or wholly blamed."
4. **Be steady like a rock.** Dhp 81: "As the wind cannot stir a solid mass of rock, so too blame and praise do not affect the wise."
5. **Watch the "I am" rise and fall.** SN 22.89 says the lingering residue is removed as the noble disciple meditates "observing rise and fall in the five grasping aggregates."

This is related to the need for validation and not identical to it. These passages are about the eight worldly conditions and the grasping aggregates, and they sit within the Buddhist path. Reading them as a way through the need for approval is ours, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist.
