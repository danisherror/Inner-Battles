A story

Note: This is an invented composite story, not a real case. It is built from patterns people commonly describe, so you can see how the need for validation works and how people loosen its grip.

Tomas's story

Tomas designs posters and posts one each week. For a year he checked how it was doing every few minutes. A good number lifted his whole day, and a quiet one sent him back to the file to change things that were fine.

He noticed it when a friend said the poster looked great and he found himself waiting for more. What helped:

  1. He noticed what he was checking. He kept a tally for a week and was shocked by how long it ran.
  2. He named the domain. His worth was tied to being seen as talented, and he saw how exposed that made him.
  3. He did some work unseen. He made a poster each week that he did not post, only to see what he liked.
  4. He spoke to himself as to a friend. After a flat week he said what he would have said to a colleague, not what he would have said to himself.
  5. He kept some approval. He still liked a kind word. He stopped steering his week by the count.
  6. He asked for help. The urge to refresh stayed strong, and he talked to his doctor, who suggested a counsellor.

The wish to be liked did not vanish. It stopped deciding his mood.

What it shows: Tomas did not have to stop caring what anyone thought. Putting some of his worth somewhere that no count could reach did what checking never could.
