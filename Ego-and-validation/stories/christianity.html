A story

The Pharisee and the tax collector

In Luke 18 Jesus tells a parable "to some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous and regarded others with contempt". Two men go to the temple to pray. The Pharisee prays "standing by himself", thanking God "that I am not like other people: thieves, rogues, adulterers, or even like this tax collector. I fast twice a week; I give a tenth of all my income" (18:11-12).

The tax collector, "standing far off, would not even lift up his eyes to heaven but was beating his breast and saying, 'God, be merciful to me, a sinner!'" (18:13).

Jesus says: "I tell you, this man went down to his home justified rather than the other, for all who exalt themselves will be humbled, but all who humble themselves will be exalted" (18:14).

What it shows: The Pharisee needs a lesser man beside him to feel good about himself. The parable is about prayer and justification and not about approval as such; the link is ours, and it does not say that every Pharisee was like this one.
