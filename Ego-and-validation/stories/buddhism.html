A story

Khemaka and the scent of the flower

In SN 22.89 senior mendicants ask the sick Khemaka whether he regards anything in the five grasping aggregates as self. He answers: "I do not regard anything among these five grasping aggregates as self or as belonging to self." They say that makes him a perfected one, but he replies: "I am not a perfected one, with defilements ended. For when it comes to the five grasping aggregates I'm not rid of the conceit 'I am'. But I don't regard anything as 'I am this'."

When they press him on what "I am" refers to, he says: "It's like the scent of a blue water lily, or a pink or white lotus. Would it be right to say that the scent belongs to the petals or the stalk or the pistil?" "No, reverend." "It would be right to say that the scent belongs to the flower."

He tells them that a noble disciple, though they have given up the five lower fetters, "still have a lingering residue of the conceit 'I am'", and that it is removed as they meditate "observing rise and fall in the five grasping aggregates." At the end, "the minds of sixty senior mendicants and of Venerable Khemaka were freed from defilements by not grasping."

What it shows: A subtle sense of "I am" can remain after the coarser views are gone, and it fades by watching. The sutta is about the sense of self and not about validation as such; the link is ours.
