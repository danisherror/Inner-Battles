The Buddhist texts speak of the mind that will not stay put. Dhp 33: "The mind quivers and quakes, hard to guard, hard to curb. The discerning straighten it out, like a fletcher an arrow."

And Dhp 35: "Hard to hold back, flighty, alighting where it will; it's good to tame the mind; a tamed mind leads to bliss."

This is related to impulsivity and not identical to it. The verses are about the restless mind and its training, and do not describe acting on urges without thought as a pattern. Reading them as speaking to impulsivity is ours.
