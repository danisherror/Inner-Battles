Impulsivity

Acting on the urge of the moment and giving up a larger, later good for a smaller, sooner one.

What it is

The Upanishads do not name impulsivity, but the Katha Upanishad sets the pleasant against the good, which is the choice at its centre. Death says to Nachiketas (1.2.1): "The good is one thing, the pleasant another; these two, having different objects, chain a man. It is well with him who clings to the good; he who chooses the pleasant, misses his end."

And (1.2.2): "The good and the pleasant approach man: the wise goes round about them and distinguishes them. Yea, the wise prefers the good to the pleasant, but the fool chooses the pleasant through greed and avarice."

This is related to impulsivity and not identical to it. The passage is about choosing between what is good and what is pleasant, and does not describe acting without thought. Reading it as speaking to impulsivity is ours.
