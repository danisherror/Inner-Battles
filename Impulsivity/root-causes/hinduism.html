Impulsivity

Acting on the urge of the moment and giving up a larger, later good for a smaller, sooner one.

Root causes

The Gita places the root in how the senses meet their objects, and in the sweetness of the first taste.

Gita 3.34: "The senses naturally experience attachment and aversion to the sense objects, but do not be controlled by them, for they are way-layers and foes."

Gita 18.38 describes the pleasure: "Happiness is said to be in the mode of passion when it is derived from the contact of the senses with their objects. Such happiness is like nectar at first but poison at the end."

This is related to impulsivity and not identical to it. The verses are about attachment and the pleasure of the senses in general. Reading the sweetness of the first moment as what drives acting on impulse is ours.
