The Gita traces where a mind led by its senses ends. Gita 2.62: "While contemplating on the objects of the senses, one develops attachment to them. Attachment leads to desire, and from desire arises anger." And 2.63: "Anger leads to clouding of judgment, which results in bewilderment of memory. When memory is bewildered, the intellect gets destroyed; and when the intellect is destroyed, one is ruined."

On Gita 18.38 ("nectar at first but poison at the end") the translator's commentary says the joy "is as short-lived as the contact itself, and leaves in its wake greed, anxiety, guilt, and a thickening of the material illusion".

This is related to impulsivity and not identical to it. The verses describe attachment and its results in general. Reading them as the toll of acting on impulse is ours.
