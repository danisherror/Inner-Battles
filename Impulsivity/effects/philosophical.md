The philosophers name regret as the usual aftermath. Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* 7.8: "incontinent man is likely to repent."

Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 34, tells the reader to "bring to your mind both points of time: that in which you will enjoy the pleasure, and that in which you will repent and reproach yourself after you have enjoyed it".

This is related to impulsivity and not identical to it. Aristotle is speaking of the incontinent person in general, and Epictetus of a promised pleasure. Neither is a study of what acting on impulse costs, and the regret they describe is not the only effect.
