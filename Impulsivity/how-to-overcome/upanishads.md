The Katha Upanishad points to understanding, to holding the reins, and to holding back the senses.

1. **Drive with understanding.** Katha 1.3.6: "But he who has understanding and whose mind is always firmly held, his senses are under control, like good horses of a charioteer."
2. **Hold the senses back.** Katha 2.3.10-11: "When the five instruments of knowledge stand still together with the mind, and when the intellect does not move, that is called the highest state. This, the firm holding back of the senses, is what is called Yoga."
3. **Stay awake to it.** The same verse adds: "He must be free from thoughtlessness then, for Yoga comes and goes."

This is related to impulsivity and not identical to it. The verses are about yoga and self-mastery, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for impulsive behaviour that is causing harm. Reading them as advice for impulsivity is ours.
