Sloth

Philosophical lens

Root causes

Philosophers find the cause of sloth in weak will, the pull of immediate pleasure and lack of purpose.

  • Akrasia. Aristotle (NE 7.3) describes the person who knows the better course but is overcome by appetite or feeling.
  • Habit. Aristotle (NE 2.1) says we become what we repeatedly do, so idleness grows by repetition.
  • Pleasure of ease. Seneca (Letters 82) says that leisure without philosophy is a kind of death, and that comfort makes the mind soft.
  • Wasted time. Seneca (On the Shortness of Life 9) sees people postponing the start of living: "postponement is the greatest waste of life."
  • Fear of failure. Kant (Metaphysics of Morals, Doctrine of Virtue) treats neglect of one's natural powers as a failing in duty to oneself.
