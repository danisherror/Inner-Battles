Sloth

Neglecting what matters, from laziness, apathy or avoidance.

Root causes

Buddhism traces sloth and torpor to unwholesome conditions that can be changed.

  • Heavy body and mind. The suttas list causes of the hindrance: discontent, sloth, yawning, sleepiness after eating and mental sluggishness, which "fuel the arising of dullness and drowsiness" (SN 46.51, Sujato's translation).
  • Lack of energy and interest. Kosajja arises where there is no vision of the goal. The Buddha speaks of "unwise attention" feeding the hindrances.
  • Over-comfort. AN 3.39 (Sukhumala) describes the intoxication of health and ease making people careless.
  • Wrong view. Fatalist views that deny the point of effort were rejected by the Buddha (AN 3.61, the Sectarian Doctrines).
  • Aversion to effort. Avoiding the discomfort of practice.
