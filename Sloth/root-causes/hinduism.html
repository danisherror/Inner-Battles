Sloth

Neglecting what matters, from laziness, apathy or avoidance.

Hinduism lens

Root causes

Hindu thought traces sloth to tamas, the guna of inertia and ignorance.

  • Born of ignorance. Gita 14.8 says that tamas arises from ignorance and deludes all embodied beings through heedlessness, laziness and sleep.
  • Rising and falling gunas. Gita 14.10: sometimes sattva prevails over the other two, sometimes rajas, sometimes tamas. Habit and food, company and activity shift the balance.
  • Food and environment. Gita 17.10 describes stale, tasteless, rotten and impure food as the food of tamas.
  • Delusion about duty. Gita 18.32 says tamasic understanding sees wrong as right, covered in darkness. Gita 3.4-8 argues no one can avoid action, so refusing to act is itself self-deception.
  • Fear and attachment. In Gita 1-2, Arjuna's despondency before the battle shows sloth as a failure of nerve.
