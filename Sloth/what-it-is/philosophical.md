Philosophers discuss sloth as a failure to act on what one knows is good, and as waste of the life one has.

**Aristotle.** In the *Nicomachean Ethics* (7.1-10) he describes *akrasia*, weakness of will: knowing the better and doing otherwise. In the *Politics* and the *Ethics* he argues that happiness is an *activity* of the soul in line with virtue (*NE* 1.7), not a state of rest.

**Seneca.** In *On the Shortness of Life* (1) he writes: "It is not that we have a short space of time, but that we waste much of it."

**Marcus Aurelius.** In the *Meditations* (5.1) he scolds himself for lying in bed in the morning: "In the morning when thou risest unwillingly, let this thought be present: I am rising to the work of a human being" (Long translation).

**Kant.** In the *Groundwork* (4:423), he argues that a rational being cannot will universal neglect of talents, so cultivating them is a duty to oneself.
