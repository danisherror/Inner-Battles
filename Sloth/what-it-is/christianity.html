What it is

Christian tradition calls it sloth (Latin acedia, from Greek akedia, "lack of care"). It began as a spiritual condition described by the desert monk Evagrius Ponticus as the "noonday demon": restlessness, boredom and distaste for one's work and prayer. Later it broadened to include laziness and neglect of duty.

The Catechism (CCC 2733) describes acedia as a temptation to which presumption opens the gate: "a form of depression due to lax ascetical practice, decreasing vigilance, carelessness of heart." Aquinas (ST II-II q.35) defines it as sorrow about spiritual good: a sadness or weariness about doing what one ought.

The Bible has plenty on idleness. Proverbs 6:6-11 tells the sluggard to learn from the ant. Proverbs 26:14 says "As a door turns on its hinges, so does a lazy person in bed." Romans 12:11 says, "Do not lag in zeal." 2 Thessalonians 3:10 says, "Anyone unwilling to work should not eat."
