A story

Moggallana nods off

The Pacalayamana Sutta (AN 7.61) tells how the Buddha's chief disciple in psychic powers, Maha Moggallana, was sitting in meditation near the village of Kallavalamutta in Magadha, and kept nodding with drowsiness.

The Buddha saw this with his divine eye, appeared before him, and asked: "Are you nodding off, Moggallana? Are you nodding off?" "Yes, sir," said Moggallana.

The Buddha then gave him a series of remedies, in order. If drowsiness comes while you are attending to a particular perception, don't give attention to it. If it persists, think over and examine the Dhamma you have learned. If that fails, recite it aloud. If that fails, pull your earlobes and rub your limbs. If that fails, get up and wash your eyes with water and look at the sky and stars. If that fails, attend to the perception of light, by day and by night. If that fails, walk back and forth, with senses turned inward. And finally, if that fails, lie down on the right side, mindful, focused on the time of getting up.

On waking he should get up quickly, thinking that he will not live indulging in the pleasures of sleeping, lying down and drowsing.

What it shows: Even a great disciple gets sleepy. The Buddha's answer is practical, a ladder of remedies, with no shame attached.
