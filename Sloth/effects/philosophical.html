Philosophical lens

Effects on life

Philosophers describe the cost as a life unlived.

  • Wasted life. Seneca (On the Shortness of Life 1-3): life is long enough if you use it well, but it is wasted by those who put off and neglect it.
  • Lost happiness. Aristotle (NE 1.7-8) argues that happiness lies in virtuous activity. A life of idle ease achieves none of it.
  • Weakened will. Aristotle (NE 2.1): idleness repeated becomes character.
  • Unrealised talents. Kant (Groundwork 4:423) says a rational being cannot will that talents be left to rust.
  • Lost duty to others. Marcus Aurelius (Meditations 5.1) reminds himself that he is made for work, as bees are, and for the common good.
