A story

Note: This is an invented composite story, not a real case. It is built from patterns people commonly describe, so you can see how anxiety works and how people loosen its grip.

Amira's story

Amira was asked to present her team's results at a meeting of forty people. Her stomach dropped. Over the next week she rehearsed the ways it could go wrong: her voice shaking, a question she could not answer, everyone's faces.

She considered telling her manager she was ill. Each time she thought of backing out she felt a wave of relief, and then, an hour later, a heavier dread about the next time.

What helped:

  1. She named it. "This is anxiety. It is loud, and it is not a forecast."
  2. She stopped treating avoidance as the answer. The relief from backing out was short, and the dread came back bigger.
  3. She went in small steps. She presented to two colleagues, then to her team of eight, before the big meeting.
  4. She slowed her breathing for a minute before she began, and kept one page of notes.
  5. She noticed it was not lifting. Weeks of poor sleep and dread before any meeting led her to her doctor, who referred her to a therapist.

The anxiety did not vanish. It got smaller, and she presented.

What it shows: Avoiding the thing taught her alarm that it was right. Small, planned steps, and help when the dread would not ease, did more than waiting to feel ready.
