**Nachiketas goes to Death**

The Katha Upanishad opens with a boy and a rash word. Nachiketas's father is giving away his possessions at a sacrifice. The boy asks: "Dear father, to whom wilt thou give me?" He says it a second and a third time. "Then the father replied (angrily): 'I shall give thee unto Death'" (1.1.4).

Nachiketas goes. He says: "I go as the first, at the head of many (who have still to die); I go in the midst of many (who are now dying)" (1.1.5). And: "A mortal ripens like corn, like corn he springs up again" (1.1.6).

Yama, Death, is away for three nights, and the boy is not received. When Yama returns he offers three boons. Nachiketas asks first that his father be "pacified, kind, and free from anger towards me" (1.1.10), and then speaks of the heaven-world: "there is no fear" (1.1.12).

**What it shows:** The boy faces what most people fear, and does not run from it. He asks for his father's peace before anything for himself. The story is about death and knowledge, and not about anxiety; its link to fear is ours.

Quotations were read in Müller's translation on Wikisource on 2026-11-14. The detail that Yama is away for three nights, and that the boy is not received, comes from Müller's bracketed explanations and not from the verses themselves. Müller's notes are omitted, and the retelling skips verses 1.1.7-9 except in outline. The reading that Nachiketas does not run from his fear is ours; the text does not describe his feelings.
