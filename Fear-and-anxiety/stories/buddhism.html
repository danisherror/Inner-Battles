A story

The Buddha waits for fear

In MN 4 the Buddha tells a brahmin how he faced fear and dread in remote forest lodgings before his awakening. He recalls: "As I was staying there a deer came by, or a peacock snapped a twig, or the wind rustled the leaves. Then I thought, 'Is this that fear and dread coming?'"

He then reasons: "Why do I always meditate expecting that fear to come? Why don't I get rid of that fear and dread just as it comes, while remaining just as I am?"

So he does. "Then that fear and dread came upon me as I was walking. I didn't stand still or sit down or lie down until I had got rid of that fear and dread while walking." He does the same standing, sitting and lying down.

What it shows: The Buddha does not wait for fear in a safe posture, and he does not run from it; he faces it where he is. The sutta is about fear in the wilderness and not about anxiety as a condition. It shows what facing fear can look like, and it is not advice to do this alone.
