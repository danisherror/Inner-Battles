Fear and anxiety

The mind sounding the alarm about threats that have not happened, or are far bigger in the imagination.

Root causes

The Gita pairs fear with attachment. Gita 2.56 describes the sage as "free from attachment, fear, and anger", and Gita 4.10 speaks of those "free from attachment, fear, and anger, becoming fully absorbed in Me, and taking refuge in Me".

Gita 18.30 adds judgement: the intellect in the mode of goodness understands "what is to be feared and what is not to be feared". Reading this as meaning that fear grows from mistaking what is to be feared is ours.

The verses do not say that attachment causes fear. They list the two together, and the link between them is the reader's inference.

This is related to fear and anxiety and not identical to them. The Gita is speaking of the steady mind in general, and not of an anxious one.
