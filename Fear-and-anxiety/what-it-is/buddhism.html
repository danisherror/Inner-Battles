Fear and anxiety

The mind sounding the alarm about threats that have not happened, or are far bigger in the imagination.

What it is

The Buddhist texts treat fear as something that can be met and trained. Dhp 39 describes a person with a steady mind: "One whose mind is not festering, whose heart is undamaged, who's given up right and wrong, alert, has nothing to fear."

In the Fear and Dread sutta (MN 4) the Buddha says that some people who go to remote places "summon unskillful fear and dread because of these defects in their conduct". Fear here is something the mind brings to the place.

This is related to fear and anxiety and not identical to them. Dhp 39 describes the end of fear in an awakened person, and MN 4 describes fear in the wilderness; neither describes anxiety as a condition.
