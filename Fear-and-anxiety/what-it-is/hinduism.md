Hinduism has no single word in the Bhagavad Gita for anxiety as the modern lens describes it, but the Gita speaks of fear and of its absence. Gita 2.56: "One whose mind remains undisturbed amidst misery, who does not crave for pleasure, and who is free from attachment, fear, and anger, is called a sage of steady wisdom."

Gita 16.1-3 begins its list of "saintly virtues of those endowed with a divine nature" with "fearlessness". And Gita 18.30 speaks of the intellect that understands "what is to be feared and what is not to be feared".

This is related to fear and anxiety and not identical to them. The Gita is describing a steady mind and its qualities, and it does not describe anxiety as a condition. Reading these verses as speaking to it is ours.
