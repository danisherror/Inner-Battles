Fear and anxiety

The mind sounding the alarm about threats that have not happened, or are far bigger in the imagination.

What it is

Fear is the alarm that goes off at a threat, and anxiety is the same alarm sounding about a threat that may come. Fear is usually tied to something in front of you; anxiety tends to look ahead and can be vague ("what if...?"). Some fear and anxiety is normal and useful. It becomes a problem when it is out of proportion or will not switch off.

The World Health Organization says: "Everyone can feel anxious sometimes, but people with anxiety disorders often experience fear and worry that is both intense and excessive." These feelings, it adds, "are difficult to control, cause significant distress and can last a long time if untreated". Of people with an anxiety disorder it says: "Usually they avoid the situations that make them anxious."

It is common. The WHO estimates that "5.8% of the global population currently experience an anxiety disorder", and that in 2023 "470 million people in the world had an anxiety disorder, making anxiety disorders the most common of all mental disorders".

Everyday signs, as people commonly describe them and not as findings from the WHO page: a racing heart before a talk, a mind that rehearses disasters, putting off a phone call, lying awake with "what if". Worry that loops on one subject overlaps with overthinking.

Feeling anxious is not a failing, and an anxiety disorder is a health condition that can be treated. If your fear or anxiety is intense, constant or getting in the way of daily life, please talk to a doctor or therapist.
