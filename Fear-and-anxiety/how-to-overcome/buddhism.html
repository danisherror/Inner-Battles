Inner battle

Fear and anxiety

The mind sounding the alarm about threats that have not happened, or are far bigger in the imagination.

How to overcome it

The Buddhist texts teach staying with the present and facing fear directly.

  1. Do not run back or ahead. MN 131: "Don't run back to the past, don't anticipate the future. What's past is left behind, the future has not arrived; and any present phenomenon you clearly discern in every case."
  2. Meet fear as it comes. In MN 4 the Buddha says: "Why don't I get rid of that fear and dread just as it comes, while remaining just as I am?" (see the story).
  3. Fortify the mind. Dhp 40: "fortifying the mind like a citadel".
  4. Be alert. Dhp 39: "alert, has nothing to fear".

This is related to fear and anxiety and not identical to them. The passages are about meditation and awakening, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for intense or constant anxiety. Reading MN 131 as speaking to anxiety about the future is ours.
