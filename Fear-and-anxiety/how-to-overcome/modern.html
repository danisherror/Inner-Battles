How to overcome it

The WHO says plainly: "There are several effective treatments for anxiety disorders. People with symptoms of anxiety should seek care."

What the sources read here say works:

  1. Talk therapy. "The psychological interventions with the most evidence for treating a range of anxiety disorders are those based on principles of cognitive-behavioural therapy. These include exposure therapy, during which people learn to face their fears." A review of meta-analyses found "the strongest support exists for CBT of anxiety disorders, somatoform disorders, bulimia, anger control problems, and general stress".
  2. Facing fears gradually. Craske and colleagues call exposure therapy "an effective approach for treating anxiety disorders", though "a substantial number of individuals fail to benefit or experience a return of fear after treatment". It works best with a therapist.
  3. Calming skills. "Learning stress management skills, such as relaxation skills and mindfulness skills, can help reduce symptoms of anxiety disorders."
  4. Exercise. "Exercise programmes can also be effective in preventing anxiety disorders in adults."
  5. Medicine is for a doctor to decide. The WHO says antidepressants "can also be useful in treating adults with anxiety disorders", and that benzodiazepines "are generally not recommended" for anxiety disorders.

Common advice, not tied to a source here: name the feeling ("this is anxiety"), do the thing in small steps instead of avoiding it, and write down a worry and a next step so it is not only in your head.

If you are thinking of harming yourself, contact your local emergency services or a crisis line now.
