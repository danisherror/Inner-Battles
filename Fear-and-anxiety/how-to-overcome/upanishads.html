How to overcome it

The Katha Upanishad gives the picture of a charioteer who holds the reins, and it points to a "fearless shore".

  1. Hold the reins. Katha 1.3.3: "Know the Self to be sitting in the chariot, the body to be the chariot, the intellect (buddhi) the charioteer, and the mind the reins."
  2. Do not let the horses run. Katha 1.3.5-6: "He who has no understanding and whose mind (the reins) is never firmly held, his senses (horses) are unmanageable, like vicious horses of a charioteer. But he who has understanding and whose mind is always firmly held, his senses are under control, like good horses of a charioteer."
  3. Aim for the fearless shore. Katha 1.3.2 speaks of "the highest, imperishable Brahman for those who wish to cross over to the fearless shore".

This is related to fear and anxiety and not identical to them. The passages are about self-mastery and the Self, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for intense or constant anxiety. Reading the charioteer as a picture of steadying an anxious mind is ours.
