Fear and anxiety

How to overcome it, read through each lens side by side.

Modern

The WHO says plainly: "There are several effective treatments for anxiety disorders. People with symptoms of anxiety should seek care."

What the sources read here say works:

  1. Talk therapy. "The psychological interventions with the most evidence for treating a range of anxiety disorders are those based on principles of cognitive-behavioural therapy. These include exposure therapy, during which people learn to face their fears." A review of meta-analyses found "the strongest support exists for CBT of anxiety disorders, somatoform disorders, bulimia, anger control problems, and general stress".
  2. Facing fears gradually. Craske and colleagues call exposure therapy "an effective approach for treating anxiety disorders", though "a substantial number of individuals fail to benefit or experience a return of fear after treatment". It works best with a therapist.
  3. Calming skills. "Learning stress management skills, such as relaxation skills and mindfulness skills, can help reduce symptoms of anxiety disorders."
  4. Exercise. "Exercise programmes can also be effective in preventing anxiety disorders in adults."
  5. Medicine is for a doctor to decide. The WHO says antidepressants "can also be useful in treating adults with anxiety disorders", and that benzodiazepines "are generally not recommended" for anxiety disorders.

Common advice, not tied to a source here: name the feeling ("this is anxiety"), do the thing in small steps instead of avoiding it, and write down a worry and a next step so it is not only in your head.

If you are thinking of harming yourself, contact your local emergency services or a crisis line now.

Hinduism

The Gita points to a steady mind that can stay level, and to taking refuge.

  1. Make the mind a friend. Gita 6.5: "Elevate yourself through the power of your mind, and not degrade yourself, for the mind can be the friend and also the enemy of the self."
  2. Stay undisturbed in misery. Gita 2.56 describes a sage "whose mind remains undisturbed amidst misery".
  3. Be like the ocean. Gita 2.70: "Just as the ocean remains undisturbed by the incessant flow of waters from rivers merging into it, likewise the sage who is unmoved despite the flow of desirable objects all around him attains peace".
  4. Take refuge. Gita 4.10 speaks of those "free from attachment, fear, and anger, becoming fully absorbed in Me, and taking refuge in Me".
  5. Hear the reassurance. Gita 11.49: "Be neither afraid nor bewildered on seeing this terrible form of Mine. Be free from fear and with a cheerful heart".

This is related to fear and anxiety and not identical to them. These verses speak of steadiness and devotion in general, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for intense or constant anxiety.

Upanishads

The Katha Upanishad gives the picture of a charioteer who holds the reins, and it points to a "fearless shore".

  1. Hold the reins. Katha 1.3.3: "Know the Self to be sitting in the chariot, the body to be the chariot, the intellect (buddhi) the charioteer, and the mind the reins."
  2. Do not let the horses run. Katha 1.3.5-6: "He who has no understanding and whose mind (the reins) is never firmly held, his senses (horses) are unmanageable, like vicious horses of a charioteer. But he who has understanding and whose mind is always firmly held, his senses are under control, like good horses of a charioteer."
  3. Aim for the fearless shore. Katha 1.3.2 speaks of "the highest, imperishable Brahman for those who wish to cross over to the fearless shore".

This is related to fear and anxiety and not identical to them. The passages are about self-mastery and the Self, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for intense or constant anxiety. Reading the charioteer as a picture of steadying an anxious mind is ours.

Christianity

The Bible's answer to fear is presence and courage, not being told to feel nothing.

  1. Remember you are not alone. Psalm 23:4: "Even though I walk through the darkest valley, I fear no evil, for you are with me; your rod and your staff, they comfort me."
  2. Be courageous with company. Joshua 1:9: "Be strong and courageous; do not be frightened or dismayed, for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go."
  3. Remember the spirit you were given. 2 Timothy 1:7: "for God did not give us a spirit of cowardice but rather a spirit of power and of love and of self-discipline."
  4. Let love push out fear. 1 John 4:18: "There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear".
  5. Take your cares somewhere. Psalm 94:19: "When the cares of my heart are many, your consolations cheer my soul." (See also Philippians 4:6-7 in Overthinking.)

This is related to fear and anxiety and not identical to them. These verses are about trust and courage in general, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for intense or constant anxiety. They are not a command to stop feeling afraid.

Buddhism

The Buddhist texts teach staying with the present and facing fear directly.

  1. Do not run back or ahead. MN 131: "Don't run back to the past, don't anticipate the future. What's past is left behind, the future has not arrived; and any present phenomenon you clearly discern in every case."
  2. Meet fear as it comes. In MN 4 the Buddha says: "Why don't I get rid of that fear and dread just as it comes, while remaining just as I am?" (see the story).
  3. Fortify the mind. Dhp 40: "fortifying the mind like a citadel".
  4. Be alert. Dhp 39: "alert, has nothing to fear".

This is related to fear and anxiety and not identical to them. The passages are about meditation and awakening, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for intense or constant anxiety. Reading MN 131 as speaking to anxiety about the future is ours.

Philosophical

The Stoics' advice is to test the fear, to stay with the present, and to act on what is yours.

  1. Test the proofs. Seneca, Letters 13.8: "consider whether your proofs of future trouble are sure." And: "We do not put to the test those things which cause our fear; we do not examine into them".
  2. Do not meet trouble early. Seneca, 13.10: "what does it avail to run out to meet your suffering? You will suffer soon enough, when it arrives".
  3. Take one thing at a time. Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 8.36: "Do not disturb thyself by thinking of the whole of thy life. Let not thy thoughts at once embrace all the various troubles which thou mayest expect to befall thee".
  4. Ask what is unbearable. Marcus, 8.36: "on every occasion ask thyself, What is there in this which is intolerable and past bearing?"

This is related to fear and anxiety and not identical to them. These are practices for fear in general, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for intense or constant anxiety.
