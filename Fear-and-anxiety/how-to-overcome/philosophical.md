The Stoics' advice is to test the fear, to stay with the present, and to act on what is yours.

1. **Test the proofs.** Seneca, *Letters* 13.8: "consider whether your proofs of future trouble are sure." And: "We do not put to the test those things which cause our fear; we do not examine into them".
2. **Do not meet trouble early.** Seneca, 13.10: "what does it avail to run out to meet your suffering? You will suffer soon enough, when it arrives".
3. **Take one thing at a time.** Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 8.36: "Do not disturb thyself by thinking of the whole of thy life. Let not thy thoughts at once embrace all the various troubles which thou mayest expect to befall thee".
4. **Ask what is unbearable.** Marcus, 8.36: "on every occasion ask thyself, What is there in this which is intolerable and past bearing?"

This is related to fear and anxiety and not identical to them. These are practices for fear in general, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for intense or constant anxiety.
