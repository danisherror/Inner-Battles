Modern lens

What it is

"The true self" is an idea from religion and philosophy: that beneath our roles, moods and habits there is a deeper self, and that we are mostly mistaken about it. Psychology has not established that such a self exists. What it can say is how hard it is to know ourselves, and what people believe about their "true" selves.

Wilson and Dunn review the research and begin: "Because of personal motives and the architecture of the mind, it may be difficult for people to know themselves." Strohminger, Knobe and Newman describe a recent line of work in which, "even among characteristics that are internal to the self, people pick out a subset as belonging to the true self." These are "judged as making people who they really are, deep down", and they find that the true self "is perceived as positive and moral". That is a finding about what people believe, and it does not show that a true self exists.

Everyday signs, as people commonly describe them and not as findings from these papers: feeling empty when a role or job ends, being surprised by your own reaction, and feeling that the person others see is not quite you. It overlaps with ego and validation and denial.

This is not a diagnosis. If you feel unreal, lost or detached, or that you do not know who you are, a doctor or therapist can help.
