Plato pictures people who mistake shadows for the real. In the *Republic* Socrates asks us to behold "human beings living in a underground den", chained from childhood, who "can only see before them". When Glaucon says they are strange, Socrates answers: "Like ourselves... they see only their own shadows". And: "To them... the truth would be literally nothing but the shadows of the images."

This is related to the idea of the true self and not identical to it. The cave is a picture of ignorance of reality, and the passage is not mainly about the self. Reading it as a picture of mistaking oneself is ours.
