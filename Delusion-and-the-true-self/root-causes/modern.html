Root causes

The sources read here point to why knowing ourselves is hard, and they say little about a hidden "true" self.

  • Motives and the structure of the mind. Wilson and Dunn write that "people often attempt to block out unwanted thoughts and feelings through conscious suppression and perhaps through unconscious repression, though whether such attempts are successful is controversial." They add: "A more common source of self-knowledge failure is the inaccessibility of much of the mind to consciousness". This is a review of research, and its abstract is a summary.
  • A flattering picture. Strohminger and colleagues find that the true self "is perceived as positive and moral". That suggests we may imagine our true self to be better than we are, and the abstract does not say so itself.

Common explanations, not findings from these papers: being defined by others' expectations early on, taking on roles that fit a family or a job, and living busily enough that there is little time to look inward.

Sources

  • Wilson and Dunn (2004), Annual Review of Psychology 55, 493-518. doi:10.1146/annurev.psych.55.090902.141954 (external link)
  • Strohminger, Knobe and Newman (2017), Perspectives on Psychological Science 12(4), 551-560. doi:10.1177/1745691616689495 (external link)

Both were read in the abstracts (Europe PMC, 2026-11-15), not in full. The sentence "That suggests we may imagine our true self to be better than we are" is our inference from the abstract and is not stated by the authors. The last paragraph cites no study.
