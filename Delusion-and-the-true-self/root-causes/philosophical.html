Root causes

Plato's root is a life lived facing the wall. Republic 7: the prisoners have been in the den "from their childhood", with "their legs and necks chained so that they cannot move, and can only see before them, being prevented by the chains from turning round their heads."

Hume offers a different view, and a puzzle for any theory of a true self. Treatise 1.4.6: "when I enter most intimately into what I call myself, I always stumble on some particular perception or other, of heat or cold, light or shade, love or hatred, pain or pleasure. I never can catch myself at any time without a perception, and never can observe any thing but the perception."

This is related to the idea of the true self and not identical to it. Plato's passage is about a life of illusion, and Hume's is his report of looking inward. They do not agree, and this site does not take a side. The reading of Plato as a root of ignorance of oneself is ours.
