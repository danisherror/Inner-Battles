> **Note:** This is an invented composite story, not a real case. It is built from patterns people commonly describe, so you can see how a narrow picture of oneself works and how people widen it.

**Haroon's story**

For thirty years Haroon was "the one who runs the department". When it restructured and his post went, he found that he did not know what to do with a Tuesday. He said "I'm nothing without the job" so often that his family stopped answering.

What helped:

1. **He expected to be partly wrong.** The sentence "I'm nothing" felt like a fact. He began to treat it as one view among others.
2. **He asked other people.** A former colleague said what he had been like to work with. A neighbour said what he was like at home. Neither was the picture he had of himself.
3. **He did not trust looking inward alone.** Sitting and thinking led in circles, so he wrote down what he did in a week and looked at that.
4. **He questioned the flattering picture.** He had imagined his real self as the manager. He noticed he also liked teaching a younger neighbour to repair things.
5. **He checked roles against values.** He listed the roles he had and asked which he had chosen and which had simply happened.
6. **He asked for help.** The flat feeling did not lift, and he spoke to his doctor, who referred him to a counsellor.

The old role did not stop mattering. It stopped being the whole of him.

**What it shows:** Haroon did not find a hidden self. He found that the picture he had was narrower than the life he was living, and he widened it with the help of other people.
