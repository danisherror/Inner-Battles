A story

Saul on the road to Damascus

In Acts 9 Saul is "still breathing threats and murder against the disciples of the Lord", on his way to Damascus to bring any who belong to the Way "bound to Jerusalem". "Suddenly a light from heaven flashed around him. He fell to the ground and heard a voice saying to him, 'Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?'" (9:3-4).

"Saul got up from the ground, and though his eyes were open, he could see nothing" (9:8). For three days he is without sight. Then Ananias comes to him: "Brother Saul, the Lord Jesus, who appeared to you on your way here, has sent me so that you may regain your sight". And "immediately something like scales fell from his eyes, and his sight was restored" (9:17-18).

What it shows: Saul is sure of what he is doing, and the text describes his being stopped and then seeing again. The story is about a conversion and not about the true self as such; the link is ours, and it has a long history of interpretation that was not read.
