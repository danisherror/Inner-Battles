A story

Krishna begins the teaching

The Gita has no tale of delusion about the self, so this is the opening of Krishna's teaching and not a story. Arjuna has told Krishna that he is confused and grieving (2.7-2.8 (external link)). Krishna answers: "While you speak words of wisdom, you are mourning for that which is not worthy of grief. The wise lament neither for the living nor for the dead" (2.11).

He goes on: "Never was there a time when I did not exist, nor you, nor all these kings; nor in the future shall any of us cease to be" (2.12). And: "The soul is neither born, nor does it ever die" (2.20).

What it shows: The teaching starts by telling Arjuna that what he mourns is not the whole of who he and the others are. The passage is about grief on a battlefield and the soul, and not about delusion as a habit; the link is ours.
