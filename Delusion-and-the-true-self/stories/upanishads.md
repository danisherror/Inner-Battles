**Svetaketu and the salt**

In the Chandogya Upanishad a father asks his son to do something simple: "Place this salt in water, and then wait on me in the morning." In the morning he says: "Bring me the salt, which you placed in the water last night." The son looks for it, "found it not, for, of course, it was melted."

The father says: "Taste it from the surface of the water. How is it?" "It is salt." "Taste it from the middle." "It is salt." "Taste it from the bottom." "It is salt." Then: "Here also, in this body, forsooth, you do not perceive the True (Sat), my son; but there indeed it is."

He ends: "That which is the subtile essence, in it all that exists has its self. It is the True. It is the Self, and thou ... art it" (6.13.1-3).

**What it shows:** What cannot be seen is still there, and it can be known by taste. The teaching is about the Self and the True, and the story is told to one student; reading it as a lesson about ignorance of oneself is ours.
