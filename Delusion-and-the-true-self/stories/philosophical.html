Plato's cave

In Book 7 of the Republic Socrates asks Glaucon to picture "human beings living in a underground den", chained from childhood so that they can see only the wall in front of them. Behind them a fire burns, and people carry objects along a raised way. The prisoners see only shadows. Glaucon says: "You have shown me a strange image, and they are strange prisoners." Socrates answers: "Like ourselves".

If one is freed, "he will suffer sharp pains; the glare will distress him". He comes slowly to see reflections, then things, then the sun. Remembering the den, "do you not suppose that he would felicitate himself on the change, and pity them?"

What it shows: The prisoners take the shadows for the real, and Socrates says they are "like ourselves". The cave is a picture of ignorance and education and not of the self as such; the link is ours, and it is one philosopher's image.
