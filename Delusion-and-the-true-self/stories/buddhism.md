**The Buddha and the group of five**

In SN 22.59 the Buddha addresses the group of five mendicants at Isipatana. He says: "form is not-self. For if form were self, it wouldn't lead to affliction. And you could compel form: 'May my form be like this! May it not be like that!'" He asks whether form is permanent, and they answer that it is impermanent, then: "But if it's impermanent, suffering, and perishable, is it fit to be regarded thus: 'This is mine, I am this, this is my self'?" "No, sir." The same is asked of feeling, perception, choices and consciousness.

He concludes: "So you should truly see any kind of form at all ... with right understanding: 'This is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self.'" At the end, "the minds of the group of five mendicants were freed from defilements by not grasping."

**What it shows:** The Buddha does not tell them what the self is. He asks, of each part of a person, whether it is fit to be called "my self". The sutta teaches not-self and does not speak of a true self; this topic's title does not fit it.
