Effects on life

The sources read here say little about effects, and what they say is cautious.

  • Self-knowledge has value, with limits. Wilson and Dunn write: "It is not always advantageous to hold self-perceptions that correspond perfectly with reality, but increasing awareness of nonconscious motives and personality is generally beneficial." The abstract does not list what the benefits are.
  • What this does not show. Neither abstract tests what happens to a person who holds a mistaken view of who they are, and neither shows that a "true self" exists.

Commonly described effects, not findings from these papers: decisions that do not fit your values, being thrown by a change of job or role, and a gap between how you act and how you feel. If you feel lost or unreal, a doctor or therapist can help.

Sources

  • Wilson and Dunn (2004), Annual Review of Psychology 55, 493-518. doi:10.1146/annurev.psych.55.090902.141954 (external link)
  • Strohminger, Knobe and Newman (2017), Perspectives on Psychological Science 12(4), 551-560. doi:10.1177/1745691616689495 (external link)

Both were read in the abstracts (Europe PMC, 2026-11-15), not in full. The Strohminger abstract says only that the findings have "implications for a variety of issues in psychology", and the issues were not read. The last paragraph cites no study.
