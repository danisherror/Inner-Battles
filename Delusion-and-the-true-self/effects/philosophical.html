Philosophical lens

Effects on life

Plato says seeing the truth first hurts. Republic 7: when a prisoner is freed and made to look towards the light, "he will suffer sharp pains; the glare will distress him, and he will be unable to see the realities of which in his former state he had seen the shadows".

Marcus Aurelius reduces what we take ourselves to be. Meditations 4: "Thou art a little soul bearing about a corpse, as Epictetus used to say."

This is related to the idea of the true self and not identical to it. Plato is describing the pain of leaving illusion, and Marcus is repeating a saying of Epictetus about the body. Reading them as the cost of ignorance of the self is ours.
