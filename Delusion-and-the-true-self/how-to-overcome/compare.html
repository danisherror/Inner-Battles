Inner battle

Delusion and the true self

How to overcome it, read through each lens side by side.

TopicWhat it isRoot causesEffects on lifeHow to overcome itA story▦ All sections
Lenses to show

Modern

The research read here suggests ways to know yourself better, and it says that obstacles are large. The rest is common advice.

  1. Expect to be partly wrong about yourself. Wilson and Dunn: "it may be difficult for people to know themselves."
  2. Use other people's eyes. They list "looking at ourselves through the eyes of others and observing our own behavior" as ways of increasing self-knowledge, and add that "major obstacles exist".
  3. Be careful with introspection. "Introspection cannot provide a direct pipeline to these mental processes, though some types of introspection may help people construct beneficial personal narratives." Looking inward is not a clear window.
  4. Notice what you imagine your "true self" to be. Strohminger and colleagues find people see it as "positive and moral". Ask whether that picture is a hope more than an observation. That use of the finding is ours, and the study did not test it.
  5. Check roles against values. Write down the roles you hold and ask which you chose. That is common advice.
  6. Get help if you feel lost. If you feel unreal, detached or that you do not know who you are, a doctor or therapist can help. That is common advice.

Sources

  • Wilson and Dunn (2004), Annual Review of Psychology 55, 493-518. doi:10.1146/annurev.psych.55.090902.141954 (external link)
  • Strohminger, Knobe and Newman (2017), Perspectives on Psychological Science 12(4), 551-560. doi:10.1177/1745691616689495 (external link)

Both were read in the abstracts (Europe PMC, 2026-11-15), not in full. Wilson and Dunn is a review, and the abstract does not say which methods work best. Steps 5 and 6 are common advice, and step 4 uses a finding in a way the paper did not test.

Hinduism

The Gita's counsel is to see what changes and what does not, and to see all beings alike.

  1. See the body change. Gita 2.22: "As a person sheds worn-out garments and wears new ones, likewise, at the time of death, the soul casts off its worn-out body and enters a new one."
  2. Know what does not change. Gita 2.20: "The soul is neither born, nor does it ever die".
  3. Look with an equal eye. Gita 6.29: "The true yogis, uniting their consciousness with God, see with equal eye, all living beings in God and God in all living beings."
  4. Use the mind as a friend. Gita 6.5: "Elevate yourself through the power of your mind, and not degrade yourself, for the mind can be the friend and also the enemy of the self."

This is related to the idea of the true self and not identical to it. These verses are about the soul, yoga and the mind, and the Gita sets them within its own path. Reading them as a way through ignorance of the self is ours, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist.

Upanishads

The Upanishads point to knowing the Self, and to guiding the chariot.

  1. Know the Self in the chariot. Katha 1.3.3: "Know the Self to be sitting in the chariot, the body to be the chariot, the intellect (buddhi) the charioteer, and the mind the reins."
  2. Hold the reins. Katha 1.3.9: "But he who has understanding for his charioteer, and who holds the reins of the mind, he reaches the end of his journey".
  3. Hear the teaching. Chandogya 6.8.7: "It is the True. It is the Self, and thou ... art it."
  4. Do not grieve. Katha 1.2.22: "The wise who knows the Self as bodiless within the bodies, as unchanging among changing things, as great and omnipresent, does never grieve."

This is related to the idea of the true self and not identical to it. These verses are about the Self, the chariot and a teaching to a student. Reading them as a way through ignorance of the self is ours, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist.

Christianity

The New Testament's counsel is to renew the mind and to find one's life in Christ.

  1. Be transformed. Romans 12:2: "Do not be conformed to this age, but be transformed by the renewing of the mind".
  2. Put on the new self. Ephesians 4:23-24: "to be renewed in the spirit of your minds, and to clothe yourselves with the new self, created according to the likeness of God in true righteousness and holiness."
  3. Take your life as given. Galatians 2:20: "it is no longer I who live, but it is Christ who lives in me."
  4. Know where your life is. Colossians 3:3: "for you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God."

This is related to the idea of the true self and not identical to it. These verses are about renewal and life in Christ, and they sit within the Christian life. Reading them as a way through ignorance of the self is ours, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist.

Buddhism

The Buddhist texts counsel seeing the five aggregates for what they are.

  1. Ask the question. SN 22.59: "is it fit to be regarded thus: 'This is mine, I am this, this is my self'?" "No, sir."
  2. See with right understanding. SN 22.59: "This is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self."
  3. See it with wisdom. Dhp 279: "All things are not-self—when this is seen with wisdom, one grows disillusioned with suffering: this is the path to purity."
  4. Let ignorance fade. SN 12.1: "When ignorance fades away and ceases no residue left behind, choices cease."

This is related to the idea of the true self and not identical to it. These passages are about the five aggregates and not-self, and they sit within the Buddhist path. The texts do not say that a person does not matter, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist.

Philosophical

The philosophers' counsel is to turn towards the light and to retire into oneself.

  1. Turn towards the light. Plato, Republic 7: the freed prisoner is "compelled suddenly to stand up and turn his neck round and walk and look towards the light".
  2. Retire into yourself. Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 4: "it is in thy power whenever thou shalt choose to retire into thyself."
  3. Order the mind. Marcus: "tranquility is nothing else than the good ordering of the mind."
  4. Renew yourself. Marcus: "Constantly then give to thyself this retreat, and renew thyself".

This is related to the idea of the true self and not identical to it. These passages are one school's discipline and a picture, and Hume's report in root causes shows that philosophers do not agree on what is found when we look inward. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist.
