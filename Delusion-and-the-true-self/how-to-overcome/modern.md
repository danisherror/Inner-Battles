The research read here suggests ways to know yourself better, and it says that obstacles are large. The rest is common advice.

1. **Expect to be partly wrong about yourself.** Wilson and Dunn: "it may be difficult for people to know themselves."
2. **Use other people's eyes.** They list "looking at ourselves through the eyes of others and observing our own behavior" as ways of increasing self-knowledge, and add that "major obstacles exist".
3. **Be careful with introspection.** "Introspection cannot provide a direct pipeline to these mental processes, though some types of introspection may help people construct beneficial personal narratives." Looking inward is not a clear window.
4. **Notice what you imagine your "true self" to be.** Strohminger and colleagues find people see it as "positive and moral". Ask whether that picture is a hope more than an observation. That use of the finding is ours, and the study did not test it.
5. **Check roles against values.** Write down the roles you hold and ask which you chose. That is common advice.
6. **Get help if you feel lost.** If you feel unreal, detached or that you do not know who you are, a doctor or therapist can help. That is common advice.

**Sources**

- Wilson and Dunn (2004), *Annual Review of Psychology* 55, 493-518. [doi:10.1146/annurev.psych.55.090902.141954](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.55.090902.141954)
- Strohminger, Knobe and Newman (2017), *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 12(4), 551-560. [doi:10.1177/1745691616689495](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616689495)

Both were read in the abstracts (Europe PMC, 2026-11-15), not in full. Wilson and Dunn is a review, and the abstract does not say which methods work best. Steps 5 and 6 are common advice, and step 4 uses a finding in a way the paper did not test.
