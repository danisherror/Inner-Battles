**Joseph and his brothers**

Genesis 37 introduces Joseph, his father Jacob's favourite son. Jacob gives him a long robe, and the brothers "hated him, and could not speak peaceably to him." Joseph also tells them dreams in which they bow down to him, which makes it worse. Acts 7:9 says the patriarchs, "jealous of Joseph, sold him into Egypt."

The brothers first plan to kill him, then sell him to traders for twenty pieces of silver, and tell their father an animal killed him.

In Egypt, Joseph goes through slavery and prison and rises to rule under Pharaoh. Years later, famine brings the brothers to buy grain. They do not recognise him. After testing them, he tells them who he is (Genesis 45). They are afraid. He says, "Do not be distressed or angry with yourselves because you sold me here, for God sent me before you to preserve life."

After their father dies, the brothers fear revenge. Joseph weeps and tells them: "Even though you intended to do harm to me, God intended it for good" (Genesis 50:20).

**What it shows:** Envy led to a crime, but the story ends in forgiveness.
