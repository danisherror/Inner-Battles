**Devadatta**

Devadatta was the Buddha's cousin, and he joined the Sangha. Tradition says he developed psychic powers, but also a hunger for status and a growing envy of the Buddha's standing. The account is in the Vinaya (Cullavagga, chapter 7).

At one point he asked the Buddha to hand over leadership of the Sangha, saying the Buddha was old. The Buddha refused, saying he would not even hand it to Sariputta and Moggallana, and certainly not to someone who has "swallowed spittle."

Devadatta held a grudge. He is said to have won over Prince Ajatasattu, and then to have tried to have the Buddha killed, by hired men, by a rolling rock, and by a maddened elephant. He also tried to split the Sangha by pressing five strict rules, and some young monks followed him until Sariputta and Moggallana brought them back.

Traditional accounts say Devadatta's end was grim, though they also hold that he can eventually be released.

**What it shows:** Envy of status, even among those who have renounced the world, can grow into harm. It is why the tradition teaches *mudita*.
