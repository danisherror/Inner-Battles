A story

A parable: the two birds

The Mundaka Upanishad (3.1.1-2) and the Shvetashvatara Upanishad (4.6-7) use an image from the Rig Veda: two birds, close companions, sit on the same tree.

One of them eats the sweet fruit of the tree. It hops from branch to branch, tastes, wants more, and sometimes finds bitter fruit. It feels lost and helpless and grieves.

The other bird does not eat. It simply looks on, calm and bright.

The text says that when the first bird, which has been caught up in the fruit, turns and sees the other, the Lord, and sees his greatness, its sorrow passes.

What it shows: Envy lives in the bird that is busy comparing fruit. The teaching is not to find better fruit but to notice the witness that is already there, and is the same as you.
