Effects on life

Philosophers say envy distorts judgment and corrodes society.

  • Pain with no gain. Aristotle (Rhetoric 2.10) notes that the envious person is pained not for any benefit to themselves but by the other's good.
  • A vice, not an emotion alone. In NE 2.7, Aristotle lists envy (phthonos) among the dispositions that are always bad, with no mean.
  • Enmity. Kant says envy, ingratitude and malice are "diabolical" in being direct hatred of humanity (Doctrine of Virtue 36).
  • Social friction. Hume and Rousseau both see relative comparison as the source of resentment between ranks.
  • Unhappiness. Bertrand Russell (The Conquest of Happiness, ch. 6) calls envy one of the strongest causes of unhappiness, since the envious person takes pain from what they have as well as what others have.
