Philosophy gives ways to turn comparison into proportion.

- **Emulation, not envy.** Aristotle (*Rhetoric* 2.11) separates envy from emulation: the emulous person is pained at not having the good thing and works to get it, rather than wanting the other to lose it.
- **Ask the price.** Epictetus (*Enchiridion* 25): has someone been honoured above you? Consider what they paid for it, and whether you would pay the same.
- **Mind what is yours.** The Stoic split between what is up to us and what is not (*Enchiridion* 1) puts others' success among the things we do not control.
- **Look wider.** Marcus Aurelius (*Meditations* 9.30) advises seeing life from a wide view in which rank means little.
- **Duty of sympathy.** Kant (*Doctrine of Virtue* 34) asks for sympathetic joy and sorrow as a duty to cultivate.
