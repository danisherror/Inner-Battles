Christianity lens

Root causes

Christian writers find envy's roots in pride, a distorted view of one's own worth, and distrust of God's goodness.

  • From pride. Gregory the Great (Moralia 31.45) lists envy as a daughter of vainglory. If I must be the best, another's excellence threatens me.
  • Suspicion of God's fairness. In Matthew 20:1-16 the workers hired first complain about those hired last, and the owner asks, "Are you envious because I am generous?" Envy objects to grace given to someone else.
  • The devil's envy. Wisdom 2:24 says death came into the world through the devil's envy.
  • Zero-sum thinking. The older brother in Luke 15:25-32 sees the father's welcome of his brother as a loss to himself.
  • Disordered love. Aquinas (ST II-II q.36) says we grieve at another's good when we see it as diminishing our own excellence.
