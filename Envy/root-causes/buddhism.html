Envy

Pain at another person's good fortune.

Buddhism lens

Root causes

Buddhism sees envy as coming from aversion, attachment to self and gain, and attention to comparison.

  • Rooted in aversion. In the Abhidhamma analysis, issa is a factor of the hatred (dosa) family: dislike of another's good.
  • Attachment to gain and status. AN 8.6 lists the eight worldly winds: gain and loss, fame and disrepute, praise and blame, pleasure and pain. A mind pushed by them envies those who get more of what it likes.
  • Conceit. Comparison ("they have more than I") is a form of mana, as in SN 22.89.
  • Stinginess. Macchariya, clinging to what one has, goes with envy at what others have.
  • Not seeing not-self. If you take the self as fixed and in need of defending, others' rise feels like your fall (SN 22.59).
