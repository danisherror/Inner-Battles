**Sigalaka and the six directions**

In the *Sigalovada Sutta* (DN 31), the Buddha is staying in the Bamboo Grove near Rajagaha. A young householder, Sigalaka, rises early and, with his clothes and hair wet, raises his cupped palms to revere the six directions: east, south, west, north, below and above.

The Buddha sees him and asks why. Sigalaka says that on his deathbed his father told him: "My dear, please revere the quarters." He says he is honouring his father's words.

The Buddha answers: "Householder's son, that's not how the six directions should be revered in the training of the Noble One." He then teaches what it really means: giving up corrupt deeds, avoiding six drains on wealth, telling fake friends from good-hearted ones, and so "covering" the six directions in daily conduct.

One of those drains is habitual laziness. The Buddha says its drawback is that "You don't get your work done because you think: 'It's too cold! It's too hot. It's too late! It's too early! I'm too hungry! I'm too full!'"

**What it shows:** The sutta is not about procrastination alone, but it names its excuses very exactly. The teaching also moves the young man from a ritual that costs nothing but a wet morning to the daily conduct that would change his life, which is a version of "stop performing, start doing".
