Root causes

The Upanishads place the root in the uncontrolled mind and in the pull of what pleases now.

  • The pleasant over the good. Katha 1.2.2: "the wise prefers the good to the pleasant, but the fool chooses the pleasant through greed and avarice."
  • Delusion by wealth and comfort. Katha 1.2.6: "The Hereafter never rises before the eyes of the careless child, deluded by the delusion of wealth. 'This is the world,' he thinks, 'there is no other.'"
  • Reins that are not held. Katha 1.3.3-6 compares the self to a rider in a chariot: the body is the chariot, the intellect the charioteer, the mind the reins and the senses the horses. "He who has no understanding and whose mind (the reins) is never firmly held, his senses (horses) are unmanageable, like vicious horses of a charioteer."

Sources

  • Katha Upanishad (external link) (Max Müller's translation) 1.2.2, 1.2.6, 1.3.3-6

The quotations were read in Müller's translation on Wikisource on 2026-11-14. Reading these passages as roots of everyday delay is an interpretation; the text speaks of the pursuit of the Self.
