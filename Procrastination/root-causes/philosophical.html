Procrastination

Putting off what matters until later, though you know the delay will cost you.

Root causes

The philosophers offer several explanations of why we know and still do not act.

  • Ignorance (Socrates). In Aristotle's report, Socrates held that "no one... when he judges acts against what he judges best" and that people act so "only by reason of ignorance". On that view delay is a failure to see clearly what is best.
  • Emotion overcoming a decision (Aristotle). Aristotle separates two kinds of incontinence: "impetuosity" and "weakness". In the second, "some men after deliberating fail, owing to their emotion, to stand by the conclusions of their deliberation". This is the one closest to the person who plans well and does not follow through.
  • Carelessness (Seneca). In his first letter to Lucilius he writes: "The most disgraceful kind of loss, however, is that due to carelessness."
  • Waiting for someone else (Epictetus). "What other master, then, do you wait for, to throw upon that the delay of reforming yourself?"
