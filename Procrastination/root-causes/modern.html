Procrastination

Root causes

Research points to several causes that often work together.

  • The task feels bad. In a meta-analysis of 691 correlations, the strong and consistent predictors of procrastination were task aversiveness, task delay, self-efficacy and impulsiveness, plus conscientiousness and its facets: self-control, distractibility, organisation and achievement motivation. Neuroticism, rebelliousness and sensation seeking showed only a weak connection.
  • Now beats later. The authors explain these results with temporal motivation theory, which combines expectancy theory with hyperbolic discounting. In plain terms: a reward loses pull the further away it is, so the relief of avoiding a task today can outweigh a payoff that comes later.
  • Mood repair. Sirois and Pychyl argue that procrastination has a great deal to do with short-term mood repair and emotion regulation: putting a task off makes an uncomfortable feeling go away for now. They add that the costs of delay fall mainly on the future self.
  • Doubt about being able to do it. Low self-efficacy, the belief that you can carry the task out, is on the list above. Starting feels risky when you expect to do it badly.
