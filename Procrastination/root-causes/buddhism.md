The Buddhist texts point to excuses, a sunken mind and a mind pulled away from the present.

- **Excuses.** In the *Sigalovada Sutta* (DN 31), the Buddha says of habitual laziness: "You don't get your work done because you think: 'It's too cold! It's too hot. It's too late! It's too early! I'm too hungry! I'm too full!'"
- **A sunken, heavy mind.** Dhp 280: "They don't get going when it's time to start; they're young and strong, but given to sloth. Their mind depressed in sunken thought, lazy and slothful, they can't discern the path."
- **Heedlessness.** Dhp 21 contrasts heedfulness with heedlessness, "the state of death".
- **A mind that is elsewhere.** MN 131 begins: "Don't run back to the past, don't anticipate the future. What's past is left behind, the future has not arrived; and any present phenomenon you clearly discern in every case." The advice implies that attention drifting to what was or will be keeps us from the work in front of us. That reading is ours.
