The philosophers describe the cost of delay as lost time, lost character and eventually a life half lived.

- **Ruin.** Hesiod: "a man who puts off work is always at hand-grips with ruin" (*Works and Days*, Evelyn-White).
- **Time slips away.** Seneca, Letter 1: "While we are postponing, life speeds by." And: "Nothing, Lucilius, is ours, except time."
- **No progress, then no life.** Epictetus warns that if you "always add procrastination to procrastination, purpose to purpose, and fix day after day in which you will attend to yourself, you will insensibly continue without proficiency, and, living and dying, persevere in being one of the vulgar" (*Enchiridion*, Higginson's translation).
- **Opportunities pass.** Marcus Aurelius: "how often thou hast received an opportunity from the gods, and yet dost not use it" (*Meditations* 2).
