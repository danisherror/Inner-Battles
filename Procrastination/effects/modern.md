The costs usually arrive later than the relief.

- **Stress and health.** In two studies of students, Tice and Baumeister found that procrastinators reported less stress and less illness early in the semester, but more stress and more illness late in the term, and were sicker overall. They also received lower grades on all assignments.
- **A loop.** Avoiding a task brings quick relief, and the guilt and pressure come after. That is consistent with the mood-repair account in [Root causes](../root-causes/modern.html), where the cost is carried by the future self. Reading it as a loop is our inference, not a finding of these studies.
- **A growing habit.** Steel notes that the prevalence of procrastination appears to be growing.
- **Everyday costs.** Rushed work, late fees and strained trust with people who were waiting on you are common results, though these are general observations and not from the studies above.

These studies were of students at one time and place, and they show association, not proof that delay caused the illness or the grades.
