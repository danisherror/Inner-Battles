The Buddhist remedies are to begin now, with vigour, and to use the practical steps the Buddha gave.

- **Begin today.** MN 131: "Today's the day to keenly work— who knows, tomorrow may bring death!"
- **Strive.** Dhp 313: "If one is to do what should be done, one should staunchly strive."
- **Stay with the present.** MN 131: "Don't run back to the past, don't anticipate the future."
- **Don't hide behind the weather.** DN 31 praises the one "who considers heat and cold as no more than blades of grass", who does their duties, and "happiness never fails".
- **Practise steadily.** Dhp 23: "They who regularly meditate, always staunchly vigorous; the attentive realize extinguishment."
- **Use a ladder of remedies.** For drowsiness, the Buddha gave Moggallana a sequence from the lightest to the most active (AN 7.61): shift attention, think over the teaching, recite it aloud, rub the limbs, wash the eyes, walk about. The same idea applies to delay: try the smallest change first, then a bigger one. See [A story in sloth](../../Sloth/stories/buddhism.html).
