How to overcome it

The Buddha's answer is the Fourth Noble Truth, the Noble Eightfold Path, a middle way between indulgence and harsh self-denial.

  1. See it clearly. Right understanding means seeing that craving brings suffering and that it can end.
  2. Keep the precepts. Right action includes avoiding sexual misconduct, which protects you and others from harm.
  3. Make effort. Right effort means not feeding the unwholesome state that has arisen and cultivating wholesome states in its place.
  4. Practise mindfulness. Right mindfulness means noticing the urge, the sensation and the thought as they arise, without being carried off by them. Craving rises and passes when it is watched.
  5. Guard the senses. Be careful what you look at and linger on.
  6. Reflect on impermanence. Remember that pleasure fades, and that clinging to it brings sorrow (Dhp 186-187, 215-216).
  7. Develop loving-kindness (metta). Seeing others as persons with their own lives counters objectifying.
  8. Meditate. Concentration steadies the mind, which is why sensual desire is treated as the first hindrance to overcome in practice.
  9. Seek good friends. The tradition calls spiritual friendship (kalyana-mitta) the whole of the path.
