Hindu teaching offers a path of **discipline, redirection and devotion**.

1. **Know the enemy.** In 3.37 Krishna names desire and anger as the foe. In 3.41-43 he advises starting with control of the senses, then understanding that the senses are subordinate to the mind, the mind to intellect, and intellect to the Self, and finally steadying the mind by the Self.
2. **Practice and detachment.** To Arjuna's worry that the mind is restless (6.35), Krishna answers: through *abhyasa* (repeated practice) and *vairagya* (non-attachment).
3. **Guard the input.** Since the chain begins with dwelling on sense objects (2.62), watch what you look at and think about, and who you keep company with (*satsang*).
4. **Give desire a higher object.** Redirect energy into service (*seva*), study, meditation, *japa* or devotion (*bhakti*), so that the mind has something worthier to hold.
5. **Live by dharma.** Desire that stays within one's duties and commitments is not the enemy (7.11). Many traditions also value *brahmacharya* (moderation or restraint of sexual energy) at different stages of life.
6. **Cultivate sattva.** Regular routines, simple food, and early rising are said to calm *rajas* and *tamas*.
