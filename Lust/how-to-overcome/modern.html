How to overcome it

These are general, practical habits that therapists and self-help guides commonly suggest. They are not a treatment plan.

  1. Find your triggers. Keep a simple log for two weeks: time, place, mood and what you were doing before an urge. Patterns usually appear fast.
  2. Change the environment, not just your willpower. Use content blockers, keep the phone out of the bedroom, and avoid being alone and bored at night with a screen.
  3. Meet the real need. If it is loneliness, schedule time with people. If it is stress, use exercise, sleep or a walk.
  4. Delay and redirect. When an urge comes, wait ten minutes and do something physical or absorbing. Urges often rise and fall without being acted on.
  5. Drop the shame spiral. After a slip, return to the plan the same day. Self-hatred tends to restart the cycle.
  6. Build real intimacy. Honest conversation, shared activity and trust do more than any rule.
  7. Get help if it is out of control. A therapist or doctor can help, especially if the pattern harms your relationships or daily life or feels compulsive. A talk with a trusted friend counts as a first step too.
