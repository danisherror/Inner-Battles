The Upanishadic remedy is **to put discernment back in the driver's seat**, and to turn desire toward the highest object.

1. **Choose the good over the pleasant.** Katha 1.2 presents this as the basic act of wisdom.
2. **Train the chariot.** Let the intellect be the charioteer, hold the mind's reins firmly, and keep the senses under control, like well-trained horses (Katha 1.3.3-6).
3. **Reshape your desire.** Since desire becomes will, deed and destiny (Brihadaranyaka 4.4.5), change what you desire at the root, and the rest follows.
4. **Study and reflect (*shravana*, *manana*, *nididhyasana*).** Hear the teaching, think it through, and meditate on it until it becomes your own.
5. **Practise self-restraint and *brahmacharya*.** The Upanishads praise discipline of the senses and of sexual energy as part of the student's training.
6. **Realise the Self.** The deepest answer is knowing that you are already complete, which removes the need for outer fulfilment. The Brihadaranyaka and Katha both speak of a person becoming free when the desires of the heart are cast away.
7. **Seek a teacher.** The Upanishads are taught person to person, and Katha 1.2.8 and 1.2.9 stress the need for a qualified guide.
