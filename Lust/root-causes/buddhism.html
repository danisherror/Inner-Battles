Buddhism lens

Root causes

Buddhism traces lust back through a chain rather than a single cause.

  • Craving (tanha). The Second Noble Truth names craving, including craving for sense pleasure, as the origin of suffering.
  • Ignorance (avijja). Underneath craving lies a mistaken view: that pleasure will be lasting, that it can satisfy, and that there is a fixed "me" who needs it. In Buddhist teaching, all conditioned things are impermanent, and clinging to them brings grief.
  • Contact and feeling. In the standard chain of dependent origination, contact through the senses gives rise to feeling, feeling to craving, and craving to clinging. The point where it is easiest to intervene is between feeling and craving, because a pleasant feeling does not have to become grasping.
  • Unguarded senses. The tradition stresses guarding the sense doors and being mindful of what we look at and dwell on.
  • Attachment. The Dhammapada says affection and lust give rise to grief and fear, and that the one who is free of them has neither (Dhp 212-216).
