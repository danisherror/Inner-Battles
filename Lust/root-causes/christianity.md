Scripture places the root in the heart and in the "flesh" in the Pauline sense, meaning human nature turned in on itself, rather than in the body as such.

- **Desire that is fed.** James 1:14-15 says each person is tempted when drawn away by their own desire; desire then conceives and gives birth to sin. The sequence is temptation, entertaining it, acting.
- **The heart as the source.** Jesus teaches in Matthew 5:27-28 that the matter starts with the look and the thought, not just the deed.
- **The conflict within.** Galatians 5:16-17 describes the flesh and the Spirit pulling against each other, and 5:19-21 lists sexual immorality among the "works of the flesh".
- **Weakness acknowledged.** Jesus says the spirit is willing but the flesh is weak (Matthew 26:41), a reminder that struggling is part of the human condition and is why watchfulness and prayer are urged.

The classical Christian view also sees lust as a disordering of love: a good desire, to love and be united with another, bent into using them.
