The Gita describes the consequences as a falling staircase. In 2.62-63, after desire comes anger, then delusion, then confusion of memory, then loss of discernment, and from that loss the person is destroyed.

Other effects the tradition points to:

- **Being devoured.** In 3.37 desire is called *mahashano*, all-devouring: the more it is fed, the more it demands, and it is never filled.
- **Covered knowledge.** In 3.38-39 Krishna compares knowledge being covered by desire to fire by smoke, a mirror by dust, and an embryo by the womb. The discerning mind is clouded and cannot see clearly.
- **Self-destruction.** Along with anger and greed, desire is called a gate to hell (16.21), meaning ruin of the self.

The effect is less a punishment from outside than a **loss of clarity and freedom**. The person who was meant to steer the senses is steered by them.
