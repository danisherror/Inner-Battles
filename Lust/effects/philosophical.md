Philosophers focus on what lust does to **freedom, character and our view of other people**.

- **Loss of freedom.** The person ruled by appetite is not choosing. Plato compares them to a city governed by its worst impulses.
- **Damaged character.** For Aristotle, repeated indulgence builds a vice. Each choice makes the next excess easier and moderation harder (*Nicomachean Ethics* II).
- **Instrumentalising people.** In Kant's ethics, treating a person merely as a means is a wrong. Sexual desire without regard for the person is a standard example in his lectures on ethics.
- **Dulled enjoyment.** Aristotle notes that the self-indulgent person ends up pursuing pleasure more and enjoying it less, since the desire outruns the satisfaction.

The common thread is that excess does not buy happiness. It trades long-term flourishing (*eudaimonia*) for short-term pleasure.
