The Buddhist account of what lust does is blunt and practical.

- **It never satisfies.** The Dhammapada says that there is no satisfying sensual desires, even with a rain of gold coins, and that sensual pleasures give little satisfaction and much pain (Dhp 186-187).
- **It brings grief and fear.** Verses 215-216 say that lust and craving give rise to sorrow and fear, and that for the one free of craving there is neither.
- **It binds.** Attachment creates bonds, and the person who is attached to what they love suffers when they are parted from it (Dhp 210-212).
- **It clouds the mind.** In the classic teaching, sensual desire is the first of the Five Hindrances that block clear meditation, along with ill will, sloth, restlessness and doubt.
- **It keeps the cycle turning.** Craving is what drives rebirth in the traditional view, so unchecked desire keeps suffering going.

The effect is a restless dissatisfaction, which the Buddha calls *dukkha*.
