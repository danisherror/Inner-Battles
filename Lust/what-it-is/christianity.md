Lust is one of the seven capital (deadly) sins. The Catechism of the Catholic Church defines it as an *inordinate craving for sexual pleasure*, one that is disordered when pursued apart from the purposes of love and commitment it is meant to serve (paragraph 2351).

In the Bible the concern reaches beyond the act to the heart. In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus teaches that looking at someone with lustful intent is already adultery in the heart (Matthew 5:27-28). Paul calls believers to flee sexual immorality and describes the body as a temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 6:18-20). James traces the path: desire, when it is fed, gives birth to sin (James 1:14-15).

The key idea is **treating a person as an object**. Christianity holds that every person bears the image of God, so using someone as a means to pleasure, even only in thought or through a screen, offends both their dignity and your own. The Catechism says this directly in its discussion of pornography (2354).

Christianity also affirms that sexuality is a good gift of creation. Lust is its distortion, not the thing itself.
