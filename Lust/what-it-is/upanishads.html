What it is

The Upanishads, the philosophical texts at the end of the Vedas, rarely talk about "sin" as such. They speak about desire (kama) and about two paths a person can choose.

The Katha Upanishad presents them as shreyas, the good, and preyas, the pleasant. The wise choose the good, while those who are shortsighted choose the pleasant (Katha 1.2.1-2). Lust is the clearest example of the pleasant that pulls against the good.

The same text gives the famous chariot image (Katha 1.3.3-9). The Self is the rider, the body the chariot, the intellect the charioteer, the mind the reins, the senses the horses, and the sense-objects the roads. A person without discernment has unmanageable horses. A person with discernment has well-trained ones.

The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad adds that a person is shaped by what they desire: as the desire, so the will; as the will, so the deed; as the deed, so the destiny (4.4.5). Desire is therefore not a small matter: it forms who we become.

So in this view lust is desire that has taken the reins from the intellect, and has pointed the self toward the pleasant instead of the good.

This is related to lust and not identical to it: the texts speak of desire (kama) in general, not of sexual desire as a separate sin.
