A story

King Pasenadi's meals

The Donapaka Sutta (SN 3.13) tells of King Pasenadi of Kosala, a patron of the Buddha. The king used to eat boiled rice by the bucketful. After one such meal he went up to the Buddha, huffing and puffing.

The Buddha, seeing this, spoke a verse: "When a man is always mindful, knowing moderation in eating, his discomfort fades, and he ages slowly, taking care of his life" (Sujato's translation).

The king did not follow it on his own. He asked the student Sudassana, who was standing behind him, to memorise the verse and recite it whenever a meal was served, and offered him a daily allowance for doing so.

The king gradually got used to having at most a cup of rice, and after some time his body slimmed right down. He then said: "In both ways the Buddha has sympathy for me: in the good of this life and the good of lives to come."

What it shows: The Buddha did not shame him. A short verse, someone to remind him and small steps worked better than a rule or a vow.
