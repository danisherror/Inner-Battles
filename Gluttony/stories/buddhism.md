**King Pasenadi's meals**

The *Donapaka Sutta* (SN 3.13) tells of King Pasenadi of Kosala, a patron of the Buddha. After eating a full bucket of rice with its curries, he came to see the Buddha, breathing heavily and plainly uncomfortable.

The Buddha, seeing this, spoke a verse: "When a person is always mindful, knowing moderation in the food he eats, his ailments grow slight; he ages gently, guarding his life."

The king did not follow it on his own. He asked his young relative, a brahmin named Sudassana, to memorise the verse and to recite it each mealtime, and to remind him to take a little less each time.

Over time the king ate less, and his body became more slender and comfortable. He later said to the Buddha how well the Buddha had cared for his welfare in this life, as well as the next.

**What it shows:** The Buddha did not shame him. A short verse, someone to remind him and small steps worked better than a rule or a vow.
