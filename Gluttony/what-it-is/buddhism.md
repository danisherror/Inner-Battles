Buddhism treats it as **lack of moderation in eating** (*bhojane mattannuta*, "knowing the right amount in eating"), a quality the Buddha taught monks and nuns, and a form of sensual craving (*kama-tanha*).

The *Maha-Assapura Sutta* (MN 39) lists moderation in eating as part of training. The *Kitagiri Sutta* (MN 70) describes the Buddha giving up the evening meal and finding the benefits in health and ease. The Buddha also gave a lay example: in SN 3.13 (Doṇapāka Sutta) he advised King Pasenadi, who ate a large amount, to take a little less at each meal, and the king's health improved.

For lay followers, the standard guidance is simply to avoid excess, and eight-precept days include not eating after noon. This is a training rule, not a sin in the Christian sense.
