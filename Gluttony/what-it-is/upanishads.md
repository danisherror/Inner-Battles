The Upanishads treat food as sacred and as something to be handled with care. The Taittiriya Upanishad describes food (*anna*) as the first sheath of the self (2.2) and, in the Bhrigu chapter, says food is Brahman (3.2). Its rules on food are: "Let him never abuse food, that is the rule" (3.7), "Let him never shun food" (3.8) and "Let him acquire much food" (3.9), in the Müller translation.

The Chandogya Upanishad (7.26.2) links what one takes in to purity of mind: "When the intellectual aliment has been purified, the whole nature becomes purified" (Müller translation; traditional commentators differ on whether *ahara* means literal food or all that the senses take in).

Gluttony in this view is the misuse of something holy: eating without awareness, or taking more than the body needs, or hoarding it from others. Taittiriya 3.10 also ties food to generosity, praising the one who gives food to others.

This is related to gluttony and not identical to it: the texts speak of respect for food, not of a sin of overeating.
