How to overcome it

Buddhism offers specific training in eating.

  • Reflect before you eat. MN 2 gives the monk's reflection: "Not for fun, indulgence, adornment, or decoration, but only to sustain this body, to avoid harm, and to support spiritual practice" (Sujato's translation).
  • Know the right amount. Bhojane mattannuta: stop while still able to eat a little more. SN 3.13 shows the king counselled to take a little less each day.
  • Mindfulness of eating. Notice taste, texture, hunger and satisfaction. Many teachers add eating slowly and in silence.
  • Guard the sense doors. Indriya-samvara, as in MN 27.
  • Eight precepts. On observance days, lay people refrain from food after noon, a temporary training and not a rule for everyone.
  • Generosity. Offering food (dana) puts the focus on giving.
