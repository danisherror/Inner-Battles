Philosophical lens

Effects on life

Philosophers say greed damages character and community.

  • Corrupted character. Aristotle (NE 4.1) says illiberality is incurable and more natural than prodigality, so the habit of taking is hard to unlearn. He adds that those who take too much are often willing to take from unworthy sources.
  • Endless lack. Seneca (Letters 2.6, 119) holds that the greedy are poor in the way that matters: they always want.
  • An empty life. Epicurus says a life spent on empty desires brings anxiety and not pleasure (Principal Doctrines 29-30).
  • Loss of justice. Plato (Republic 8) argues that a city ruled by love of wealth splits into the rich and the poor, with unrest following.
  • Using people. Kant says avarice conflicts with the duty of benevolence, treating others' needs as no concern of ours.
