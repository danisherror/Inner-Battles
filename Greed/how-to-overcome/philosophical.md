Philosophy offers habits of proportion.

- **Set the limit.** Epicurus: natural wealth is limited and easy to get (*Principal Doctrines* 15). Decide what is natural and necessary, and stop there.
- **Practise voluntary poverty.** Seneca (*Letters* 18) suggests living on little for a few days at a time to learn that you can, so loss holds less fear.
- **Aim at the mean.** Aristotle (*NE* 4.1) says to give and take at the right times, in the right amounts and for the right reasons, and to train this by habit.
- **Use wealth as a tool.** Keep it as a means to a good life and not the aim (*Politics* 1.9).
- **Give.** Kant treats helping others as a duty that cannot be dropped.
- **Ask what you are afraid of.** Stoic exercises (Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 1) separate what is up to you from what is not, and wealth is the latter.
