The aim is not to be poor but to be free of the compulsion.

1. **Define "enough".** Pick a number or standard for housing, savings and spending, and write it down. Without one, "more" has no end.
2. **Slow purchases.** Wait 48 hours before non-essential buys.
3. **Limit comparison.** Mute accounts and ads that make you feel behind.
4. **Give regularly.** Fixed, planned giving of money or time weakens the grip. Studies find spending on others tends to raise happiness more than spending on yourself.
5. **Spend on experiences and people**, not only on things.
6. **Practise gratitude.** Write down three things you already have.
7. **Check the feeling underneath.** If you shop or chase money when anxious or lonely, deal with that.

**Sources**

- Dunn, Aknin & Norton (2008), "Spending money on others promotes happiness," *Science* 319(5870), 1687-1688. [doi:10.1126/science.1150952](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1150952)

Steps are general advice, not a tested programme. The citation was from memory and not re-checked. If spending, gambling or hoarding feels out of control, speak with a doctor or therapist.
