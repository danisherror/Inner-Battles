Deadly sin

Greed

Wanting more than you need, and never feeling you have enough.

Philosophical lens

Root causes

Philosophers give several causes.

  • Unlimited aim. Aristotle (Politics 1.9) says that when wealth becomes an end and not a means to a good life, there is no limit to it, and desire spreads without end.
  • Mistaking wants for needs. Epicurus divides desires into natural and necessary, natural but unnecessary, and empty. Greed feeds on the empty kind (Letter to Menoeceus, 127-130).
  • Fear of lacking. Seneca (Letters 119) argues that desire for more is a restlessness of the mind, and that "nature demands nothing except mere food" (Letters 119.13, Gummere translation).
  • Social structure. Plato (Republic 8, 550c-555a) describes the oligarchic person who has made money-making the single aim, with the other desires pushed down.
