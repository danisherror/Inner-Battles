Deadly sin

Greed

Wanting more than you need, and never feeling you have enough.

What it is

The Sanskrit word is lobha, greed, one of the six inner enemies (shadripu). Its opposite is aparigraha, non-possessiveness, one of the yamas (restraints) in Patanjali's Yoga Sutras (2.30, 2.39).

The Gita names it with desire and anger as a gate to self-destruction (16.21). In 16.12-13 it describes people who, bound by hundreds of hopes, try to amass wealth unjustly to satisfy desires, and who think "this much I have today, and I will get more." In 3.12, the one who enjoys the gifts of life without giving anything back is called a thief.

Hinduism does not condemn wealth. Artha, prosperity honestly earned, is one of the four aims of life. The fault is in clutching it without limit or without sharing.
