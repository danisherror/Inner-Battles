Deadly sin

Greed

Wanting more than you need, and never feeling you have enough.

What it is

The Pali word is lobha, greed or grasping. With hatred (dosa) and delusion (moha) it is one of the three unwholesome roots from which harmful action grows. Its wider form is tanha (craving), which the First Noble Truth's teaching names as the origin of suffering (SN 56.11, the first sermon).

The Dhammapada puts the problem plainly: "There is no satisfying sensual desires even with the rain of gold coins" (verses 186-187, Buddharakkhita translation). Another verse says wealth destroys the foolish who do not seek the far shore (355). Generosity (dana) is the standard remedy, and the Dhammapada (177) says misers do not go to the heavenly realms.

Buddhism does not demand poverty from lay people. It asks you to earn rightly, use wealth wisely and not cling.
