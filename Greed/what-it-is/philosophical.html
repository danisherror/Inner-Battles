Greed

Wanting more than you need, and never feeling you have enough.

Philosophical lens

What it is

Philosophers describe greed as a failure of proportion about wealth.

Aristotle. In the Nicomachean Ethics (4.1) the virtue about wealth is liberality, giving and taking rightly. Its excess, in taking, is illiberality or meanness. In the Politics (1.8-9) he adds that the art of getting wealth has no natural limit once money becomes the aim, and he uses the story of Midas.

Epicurus. Natural wealth is limited and easy to get; it is empty desire that has no limit (Principal Doctrines 15, 29-30).

Seneca. "It is not the man who has too little, but the man who craves more, that is poor" (Letters 2.6).

Kant. In the Metaphysics of Morals (Doctrine of Virtue), avarice (Geiz) is a failure of the duty to use wealth in line with benevolence.
