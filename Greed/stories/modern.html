Greed

Wanting more than you need, and never feeling you have enough.

A story

Note: This is an invented composite story, not a real case. It is built from patterns people commonly describe, so you can see how greed works and how people step out of it.

Anil's story

Anil was 38 and ran a small import business that did well. Each year he told himself he'd slow down once he reached a number. When he reached it, the number moved. He bought a bigger house, then a second property he hardly visited, and checked his business rivals' growth every morning.

He missed his daughter's school recital for a supplier call. He pushed a partner out of a deal on terms that were legal but not fair, and the partner stopped speaking to him. Money was up and he felt worse.

The turning point was small. His father, who had little, was ill, and Anil visited and found him calm. "Why aren't you afraid?" he asked. His father said, "I have enough."

What helped:

  1. He wrote down "enough": income, savings and what he would need for the family.
  2. He fixed the partner deal, paying what he should have, and said sorry.
  3. He gave on a schedule, a fixed share to a school fund, so it didn't depend on mood.
  4. He blocked time off, with no supplier calls after seven.
  5. He stopped watching rivals, and checked his own numbers weekly instead.

The business grew more slowly. He slept better and was at the next recital.

What it shows: Greed has no finish line unless you draw one. "Enough" is a decision, not a number the world gives you.
