A story

The golden goose

The Suvannahamsa Jataka (Jataka 136) is a tale of one of the Buddha's past lives. A Brahmin dies and is reborn as a golden goose that remembers its past life. Seeing his wife and three daughters in poverty, the goose visits them and gives them one golden feather, then returns from time to time to give another. The family's life improves.

The wife begins to think the goose could be slow and that they should get more. She tells her daughters she will take all the feathers at once. When the goose next arrives, she grabs it and plucks every feather. But the feathers, taken by force, turn into ordinary feathers. When new ones grow, they are white, not gold. The goose flies away and does not return.

The family's wealth ends, and they have lost even their steady supply.

What it shows: Greed destroys the very source it wants more of. The goose, the Buddha explains in the frame story, was himself, and the wife was a past life of the woman who is greedy in the present.
