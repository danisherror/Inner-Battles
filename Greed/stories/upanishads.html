A story

Nachiketa refuses wealth

The Katha Upanishad (Part 1) tells of a boy, Nachiketa, whose father Vajashrava performs a sacrifice in which he gives away only old, useless cows. Nachiketa sees the gift is empty and asks, "To whom will you give me?" His angry father says, "To Death."

Nachiketa goes to Death's house and waits three days without food. Death, ashamed, grants him three wishes. For the third, the boy asks what happens after death.

Death tries to talk him out of it. He offers sons and grandsons who will live a hundred years, cattle, elephants, gold, a vast land, long life, chariots and music. "Ask for anything else," he says. Nachiketa answers that all this lasts only until tomorrow, and wears out the senses. Wealth cannot satisfy a person, and he will live only as long as Death allows. He repeats his question.

Death, seeing his steadiness, praises him for choosing the good (shreyas) over the pleasant, and teaches him.

What it shows: The choice is between what feels full now and what is lasting. Nachiketa's freedom from greed is what lets him learn.
