A story

Zacchaeus

Luke 19:1-10 tells of Zacchaeus, a chief tax collector in Jericho, and rich. Tax collectors were widely seen as collaborators who took more than they should, and Zacchaeus was disliked.

Hearing Jesus would pass by, he wanted to see him. Being short, and blocked by the crowd, he ran ahead and climbed a sycamore tree. Jesus stopped under it, looked up and said, "Zacchaeus, hurry and come down; for I must stay at your house today."

People grumbled that Jesus was the guest of a sinner. Zacchaeus stood and said, "Look, half of my possessions, Lord, I will give to the poor; and if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I will pay back four times as much."

Jesus replied, "Today salvation has come to this house."

What it shows: The cure is not only feeling sorry. Zacchaeus' change shows up in what he does with his money. The story is a contrast with the rich ruler of Luke 18:18-25, who could not let go.
