**King Midas**

The Greek myth, told in Ovid's *Metamorphoses* (Book 11), is about King Midas of Phrygia. He did a kindness to Silenus, a companion of the god Dionysus, and in return Dionysus offered him a wish. Midas asked that everything he touched turn to gold.

At first he was delighted, and turned twigs, stones and apples to gold. Then he sat to eat. The bread became gold in his hand, the wine became liquid gold in his mouth. Some versions add that his daughter turned to gold when he embraced her.

Starving and horrified, Midas begged to be released. Dionysus told him to wash in the river Pactolus. The gold passed into the water, and the river's sands have been golden since.

Aristotle uses the story in the *Politics* (1.9, 1257b) to make a point about money: wealth is not what a person needs to live, and a man with plenty of it may die of hunger.

**What it shows:** Wanting without a limit can destroy the thing you wanted wealth for, which is living well.
