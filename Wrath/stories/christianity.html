A story

Moses at the rock

Numbers 20:1-13 tells of the people of Israel in the wilderness of Zin. There was no water, and the people quarrelled with Moses and Aaron, saying they had been brought out only to die. Moses and Aaron went to the tent of meeting and fell on their faces.

God told Moses to take his staff, gather the people, and speak to the rock before their eyes so that it would give water.

Moses took the staff and gathered the community. Then he said, "Listen, you rebels, shall we bring water for you out of this rock?" and struck the rock twice with his staff. Water came out in abundance, and the people and animals drank.

But God said to Moses and Aaron, "Because you did not trust in me, to show my holiness before the eyes of the Israelites, therefore you shall not bring this assembly into the land that I have given them."

Moses had led the people for forty years. He saw the promised land from a mountain but did not enter it (Deuteronomy 34:1-4).

What it shows: After decades of patience, one moment of angry impatience cost Moses what he had worked for. The story shows how even a great leader's anger can undo good work.
