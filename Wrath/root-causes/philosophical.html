Philosophical lens

Root causes

Philosophers see anger as arising from judgment about a wrong, plus bodily and social conditions.

  • A judgment of slight. Aristotle (Rhetoric 2.2) says anger follows an apparent slight, belittlement or insult, and is mixed with the pleasure of hoped-for revenge.
  • False opinion. The Stoics held that anger rests on a judgment that one has been wronged and that revenge is appropriate. Epictetus (Enchiridion 20) says it is not the person who insults you that harms you, but your belief that you are harmed.
  • Weak character. Seneca (On Anger 1.20 and 2.19) says anger is more common in the soft, the sick and the over-indulged.
  • Spirited part unruled. Plato (Republic 4, 439e-441c) shows anger as the spirited part, which needs to be trained by reason.
  • Self-importance. Marcus Aurelius (Meditations 11.18) lists reminders that others act from ignorance, and that we too offend.
