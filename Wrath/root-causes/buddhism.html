Wrath

Uncontrolled anger, resentment and the wish to get even.

Buddhism lens

Root causes

Buddhism traces anger to aversion, attachment to a self and wrong view.

  • Aversion. Dosa is the root: pushing away what is unpleasant. Craving and aversion are two sides of the same grip (SN 56.11).
  • Self-view. If there is a "me" to defend, every insult is an attack on it. SN 22.59 teaches that the aggregates are not self.
  • Dwelling on grievances. Dhammapada 3-4 describes thoughts of "He abused me, he struck me" (Buddharakkhita translation), which keep hatred alive.
  • Unwise attention. The suttas say that attending to the repulsive sign feeds ill will, as with the attractive sign for sensual desire (SN 46.51).
  • Conditions of the body and mind. Pain, tiredness and hunger make irritation easier (MN 2 notes that discomfort and hardship are to be endured).
