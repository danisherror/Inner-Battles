Philosophical lens

Effects on life

Philosophers describe anger as a kind of temporary madness.

  • Loss of reason. Seneca (On Anger 1.1) calls anger "a short madness" (Stewart translation; he attributes the saying to "some of the wisest of men"), and says it makes people deaf to counsel and blind to what is useful.
  • Harm without gain. Seneca (2.12) argues that anger is not useful even in war, because it gets in the way of calm judgment.
  • Cruelty. Plutarch (On the Control of Anger) describes how anger leads people into acts they regret.
  • Self-injury. Marcus Aurelius (Meditations 11.18) notes that the anger and grief that follow are worse than the thing that caused them.
  • Disrespect for persons. Kant treats vengefulness as a violation of the respect owed to others.
