Effects on life

Unchecked anger costs health, relationships and judgment.

  • Heart and body. A meta-analysis found that outbursts of anger were followed by a short-term rise in the risk of heart attack and stroke, though the absolute risk in any one episode is small.
  • Relationships. Rage, contempt and silent treatment erode trust. Words said in anger are hard to take back.
  • Work and legal. Angry outbursts can end careers and lead to violence or legal trouble.
  • Judgment. Anger narrows attention and makes people more certain and less careful.
  • Venting doesn't help. Experiments suggest that "letting it out" (for example hitting a pillow) tends to keep anger up, not reduce it.
  • Children. Living with frequent rage can harm those around you, children especially.
