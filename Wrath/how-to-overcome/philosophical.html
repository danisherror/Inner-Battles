How to overcome it

Philosophy offers practices for catching anger early.

  • Delay. Seneca (On Anger 2.29): "The greatest remedy for anger is delay." Wait before you act.
  • Examine the judgment. Epictetus (Enchiridion 20): the insult is in your opinion. Ask whether the claim is true, and if so, why be angry.
  • Evening review. Seneca (On Anger 3.36) describes the custom of the philosopher Sextius, who asked his spirit each night, "What bad habit of yours have you cured to-day? what vice have you checked? in what respect are you better?" Seneca says he follows the practice himself.
  • Assume ignorance. Marcus Aurelius (Meditations 2.1 and 11.18): people act from ignorance of good and evil, and we also offend. Remember we are all going to die soon.
  • Aim at mildness. Aristotle (NE 4.5): train toward the right anger at the right time, which takes practice and judgment.
  • Avoid the company of the irritable, and choose calm company (Seneca 3.8).
