The aim is not to never feel anger, but to respond without being run by it.

1. **Spot the early signs**: tight jaw, heat, faster heartbeat.
2. **Pause.** Step away, breathe slowly (longer out-breath), or count to ten. Wait until the peak has passed before you speak.
3. **Reappraise.** Ask, "Is there another way to read this?" Research finds that reframing is more effective than suppressing.
4. **Find the feeling underneath** (hurt, fear, shame) and say that instead of attacking.
5. **Say what you need**, using "I" statements, calmly, once you are calm.
6. **Don't just vent.** Exercise or a walk can help use up arousal. Hitting things tends to keep anger high.
7. **Look after the basics**: sleep, food, alcohol, stress.
8. **Get help.** Anger-management programmes and therapy work, especially if anger leads to violence or fear at home.
