How to overcome it

Hindu practice answers anger with forgiveness, equanimity and ahimsa.

  • Kshama. Forgiveness is a divine quality (Gita 16.3). The Mahabharata (Udyoga Parva, Vidura's counsel) is often quoted: "conquer anger by freedom from anger" (akrodhena jayet krodham).
  • Equal-mindedness. Gita 12.18-19: equal to friend and foe, in honour and dishonour, in cold and heat.
  • Dear to Krishna. Gita 12.13-14: hating no being, friendly, compassionate, forgiving and content.
  • Ahimsa. Yoga Sutras 2.35: in the presence of one firmly established in non-harm, hostility ceases. Practise non-harm in thought, word and deed.
  • Pratipaksha-bhavana. Yoga Sutras 2.33: when harmful thoughts arise, cultivate the opposite.
  • Mantra, prayer and breath practices to settle the mind.
