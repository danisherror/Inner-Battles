Wrath

Uncontrolled anger, resentment and the wish to get even.

Upanishads lens

What it is

The Upanishads rarely name anger directly. They describe the calm that is its opposite, and treat harshness as a sign of ignorance.

  • Brihadaranyaka 4.4.23 describes the knower as shanta (calm), danta (self-controlled), uparata (withdrawn from distraction), titikshu (patient with hardship) and samahita (collected).
  • Isha 6 says that the one who sees all beings in the Self, and the Self in all beings, does not hate (na vijugupsate).
  • Brihadaranyaka 5.2.3 reports the thunder's advice to humans, gods and demons: da, da, da, which is read as self-control, giving and compassion.
  • Katha 1.1.10 shows anger inside a family: Nachiketa asks that his father, who is angry at him, may calm down and be free of anger (vita-manyu).

This is related to wrath and not identical to it: the texts describe calm and self-control and treat harshness as ignorance, rather than naming a sin of anger.
