Christian tradition calls it **wrath** or **anger** (Latin *ira*): anger that seeks vengeance or turns to hatred. The Catechism says anger is a desire for revenge, and that it is gravely wrong to wish to harm one who must be punished, and a sin to desire to see a neighbour suffer (CCC 2302).

The tradition distinguishes **righteous anger** at injustice from sinful anger. Ephesians 4:26 says, "Be angry but do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger." James 1:19-20 says to be "slow to anger, for your anger does not produce God's righteousness." In Matthew 5:21-22, Jesus links anger with murder at the level of the heart.

Aquinas (*ST* II-II q.158) argues that anger is sinful when it goes against reason, in measure or in aim, and not when it is a proportionate response to wrong.
