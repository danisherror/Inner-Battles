**Vedehikā and her maid**

In MN 21 the Buddha tells of a housewife in Sāvatthī, Vedehikā, who "had this good reputation: 'The housewife Vedehikā is gentle, placid, and calm.'" Her maid Kāḷī wonders: "does she actually have anger in her and just not show it? Or does she have no anger?" She decides to test her mistress.

Kāḷī gets up late in the day, then later, then later still. Each time Vedehikā grows angrier, first scowling, then blurting out angry words, and the third time she "grabbed a door-pin and hit Kāḷī on the head, cracking it open." Kāḷī denounces her to the neighbours, and "Vedehikā got this bad reputation: 'The housewife Vedehikā is fierce, ill-tempered, and not calm at all.'"

The Buddha applies it: "a mendicant may be the gentlest of the gentle, the most placid of the placid, the calmest of the calm, so long as they don't encounter any disagreeable criticism. But it's when they encounter disagreeable criticism that you'll know whether they're really gentle, placid, and calm."

**What it shows:** Anger that is kept out of sight is still there, and it shows when it is tested. The sutta is about anger and criticism and not about resentment as such; the link is ours. The story includes violence, and it is not a model for how to treat anyone.
