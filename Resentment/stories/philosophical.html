A story

Marcus Aurelius meets the difficult people

Marcus Aurelius is not telling a story in Meditations 2, but the opening is a scene of a morning. "Begin the morning by saying to thyself, I shall meet with the busy-body, the ungrateful, arrogant, deceitful, envious, unsocial. All these things happen to them by reason of their ignorance of what is good and evil."

He then says what he will do about it. He knows "the nature of him who does wrong, that it is akin to me", so "I can neither be injured by any of them, for no one can fix on me what is ugly, nor can I be angry with my kinsman, nor hate him". He explains: "we are made for co-operation, like feet, like hands, like eyelids, like the rows of the upper and lower teeth."

What it shows: Marcus prepares for the difficult people before they arrive, and decides in advance not to hate them. The passage is about the Stoic view of other people and not about resentment as such; the link is ours, and it is one philosopher's discipline, which does not say what to do when someone has really harmed you.
