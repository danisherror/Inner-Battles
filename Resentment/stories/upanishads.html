A story

Nachiketas and his father's anger

In the Katha Upanishad, the boy Nachiketas asks his father, who is giving away his possessions, "Dear father, to whom wilt thou give me?" He asks a second and a third time. "Then the father replied (angrily): 'I shall give thee unto Death'" (1.1.4).

Nachiketas goes to Death. When Death offers him three boons, the first thing he asks for is about his father: "that Gautama, my father, be pacified, kind, and free from anger towards me; and that he may know me and greet me, when I shall have been dismissed by thee" (1.1.10).

Death answers: "Through my favour Auddâlaki Âru n i, thy father, will know thee, and be again towards thee as he was before. He shall sleep peacefully through the night, and free from anger, after having seen thee freed from the mouth of death" (1.1.11).

What it shows: The first thing Nachiketas asks for is not for himself, but for his father's anger to end. The story is about death and knowledge and not about resentment; it shows a son who has every reason to be hurt, asking that his father be at peace with him.
