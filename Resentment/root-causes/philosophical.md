Aristotle says why this kind of anger lasts. *Nicomachean Ethics* 4.5: "If this does not happen they retain their burden; for owing to its not being obvious no one even reasons with them, and to digest one's anger in oneself takes time."

Epictetus puts the root in what we assume about the other person. *Enchiridion* 42: "When any person harms you, or speaks badly of you, remember that he acts or speaks from a supposition of its being his duty."

This is related to resentment and not identical to it. Aristotle is describing anger that is held in, and Epictetus how we read someone's motives. Reading them together as roots of a grudge is ours, and Epictetus does not say that every harm is innocent.
