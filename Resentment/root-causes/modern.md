The sources read here describe how a grudge is kept going more than where it starts.

- **Anger that is not worked through.** Gangopadhyay and Singh's survey of 740 people found that "the relationship between anger and hate is serially mediated by anger rumination and grudge". The authors suggest that "when anger is not regulated effectively, it may intensify through ruminative thinking and unresolved grudges, resulting in hate". This is a self-report survey, and it does not show cause.
- **Replaying the offence.** In Witvliet and colleagues' study, people who rehearsed the hurt showed more aversive emotion than people who took the offender's perspective. The study was a short laboratory exercise.

Common explanations, not findings from these papers: feeling wronged and unacknowledged, an apology that never came, a sense of unfairness or powerlessness, and a belief that letting go means saying it did not matter.

**Sources**

- Gangopadhyay and Singh (2026), *The Journal of Psychology*. [doi:10.1080/00223980.2026.2716095](https://doi.org/10.1080/00223980.2026.2716095)
- Witvliet, Ludwig and Vander Laan (2001), *Psychological Science* 12(2), 117-123. [doi:10.1111/1467-9280.00320](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.00320)

Both were read in the abstracts (Europe PMC, 2026-11-14), not in full. The last paragraph cites no study.
