Aristotle describes the person who keeps a grudge. In the *Nicomachean Ethics* (4.5): "Sulky people are hard to appease, and retain their anger long; for they repress their passion."

He adds that "it ceases when they retaliate; for revenge relieves them of their anger, producing in them pleasure instead of pain."

This fits the philosophical lens closely, since Aristotle is describing the sulking, long-kept anger that we would call a grudge. His account is one philosopher's description and not a modern definition, and it is not about forgiveness.

**Sources**

- [Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* 4.5](http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html) (W. D. Ross's translation)

The quotations were read at MIT Classics on 2026-11-14. The page credits Ross as translator; the two sentences are consecutive in the text. The Greek word behind "sulky" was not examined, and the link to modern resentment is ours.
