The research read here describes effects, mostly as statistical associations. Holt-Lunstad and colleagues (2015) report that "social isolation odds ratio (OR) = 1.29, loneliness OR = 1.26, and living alone OR = 1.32, corresponding to an average of 29%, 26%, and 32% increased likelihood of mortality, respectively", and that "initial health status has an influence on the findings". Holt-Lunstad, Smith and Layton (2010), across "148 studies (308,849 participants)", report a "50% increased likelihood of survival for participants with stronger social relationships". These are group averages from observational studies and say nothing certain about any one person.

Cacioppo and Hawkley report that perceived social isolation "is a risk factor for, and may contribute to, poorer overall cognitive performance, faster cognitive decline, poorer executive functioning, increased negativity and depressive cognition".

Common effects, as people describe them and not findings from these papers: poor sleep, low mood, pulling further away, and doing less.

None of this is a reason for alarm. It is a reason to ask for company. Loneliness is common and nothing to be ashamed of. If it is heavy or lasts, or comes with hopeless thoughts, please talk to a doctor, therapist or someone you trust. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, tell someone today, and if you may act on those thoughts contact your local emergency services now. [The International Association for Suicide Prevention points to a directory of verified helplines in over 150 countries](https://www.iasp.info/crisis-centres-helplines/).
