The sources read here point to a few causes, and are limited.

- **Trends.** Masi and colleagues note that "Social and demographic trends are placing an increasing number of adults at risk for loneliness".
- **A threat-watching mind.** Cacioppo and Hawkley report "heightened sensitivity to social threats" and "a confirmatory bias in social cognition that is self-protective and paradoxically self-defeating". A lonely mind may read cold signals into ordinary events, and avoid people, which keeps it lonely. The abstract says research indicates this and does not say which comes first.
- **Social thinking that has gone wrong.** Masi and colleagues say the most successful interventions "addressed maladaptive social cognition", "consistent with current theories regarding loneliness and its etiology".
- **Everyday causes.** Common description, not from these papers: moving, losing someone, a change of job or life stage, illness, shyness, and shame about being alone.

Loneliness is not a character flaw. Loneliness is common and nothing to be ashamed of. If it is heavy or lasts, or comes with hopeless thoughts, please talk to a doctor, therapist or someone you trust. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, tell someone today, and if you may act on those thoughts contact your local emergency services now. [The International Association for Suicide Prevention points to a directory of verified helplines in over 150 countries](https://www.iasp.info/crisis-centres-helplines/).

- Masi, Chen, Hawkley and Cacioppo (2011), "A meta-analysis of interventions to reduce loneliness," *Personality and Social Psychology Review* 15(3), 219-266. [doi:10.1177/1088868310377394](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868310377394)
- Cacioppo and Hawkley (2009), "Perceived social isolation and cognition," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 13(10), 447-454. [doi:10.1016/j.tics.2009.06.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2009.06.005)
