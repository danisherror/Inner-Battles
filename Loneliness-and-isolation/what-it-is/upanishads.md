The Upanishads describe separateness as a kind of small seeing. Isha 6-7: "he who beholds all beings in the Self, and the Self in all beings, he never turns away from it. When to a man who understands, the Self has become all things, what sorrow, what trouble can there be to him who once beheld that unity?"

Chandogya 7.24 draws the line: "Where one sees nothing else, hears nothing else, understands nothing else, that is the Infinite. Where one sees something else, hears something else, understands something else, that is the finite."

This is related to loneliness and not identical to it. Both passages are about seeing the Self in all things and say nothing of lacking company, so reading them as an account of loneliness is ours. They do not say the lonely person has failed to see anything. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and loneliness that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.
