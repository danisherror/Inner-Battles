Hinduism has no single word in the Bhagavad Gita for loneliness, but it treats both company and solitude. Gita 6.10 says: "Those who seek the state of Yog should reside in seclusion, constantly engaged in meditation with a controlled mind and body". That is solitude chosen for practice.

On the other side, 5.29 describes the Lord as "the selfless friend of all living beings": "Having realized Me as the enjoyer of all sacrifices and austerities, the Supreme Lord of all the worlds and the selfless friend of all living beings, My devotee attains peace."

This is related to loneliness and not identical to it. 6.10 is about a discipline of seclusion and not about the ache of being alone, and 5.29 speaks of peace and not of human company. Reading them together as an account of loneliness is ours. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and loneliness that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.
