Modern

Masi and colleagues' meta-analysis names four strategies that loneliness interventions have used: "(a) improving social skills, (b) enhancing social support, (c) increasing opportunities for social contact, and (d) addressing maladaptive social cognition". It reports that "single-group pre-post and nonrandomized comparison studies yielded larger mean effect sizes relative to randomized comparison studies", and that among the randomised studies "the most successful interventions addressed maladaptive social cognition". The steps below are common advice, set against those four strategies.

  1. Name it. "I am lonely" is a report about now, and not a verdict on you. That is common advice.
  2. Check the story. If a short reply reads as a rejection, ask what else it could be. This is the site's own application of strategy (d), and the abstract does not describe it.
  3. Add small contacts. A regular call, a shared walk, a class. That is strategy (c), as common advice.
  4. Find a shared task. Company is easier around something you do together. That is common advice.
  5. Reach out first. Most people wait to be asked. That is common advice.
  6. Get help. Loneliness is common and nothing to be ashamed of. If it is heavy or lasts, or comes with hopeless thoughts, please talk to a doctor, therapist or someone you trust. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, tell someone today, and if you may act on those thoughts contact your local emergency services now. The International Association for Suicide Prevention points to a directory of verified helplines in over 150 countries (external link).

  • International Association for Suicide Prevention, Crisis Centres & Helplines (external link): read on 2026-11-15; the page says the directory it points to lists verified helplines in over 150 countries.

Hinduism

The Gita counsels friendliness and chosen solitude.

  1. Be friendly. 12.13-14: "Those devotees are very dear to Me who are free from malice toward all living beings, who are friendly, and compassionate."
  2. Know the friend. 5.29: "the selfless friend of all living beings, My devotee attains peace."
  3. Use solitude well. 6.10: "Those who seek the state of Yog should reside in seclusion, constantly engaged in meditation with a controlled mind and body".

This is related to loneliness and not identical to it. The verses describe devotion and a discipline, and do not tell a lonely person to be more friendly. Reading them as a way through loneliness is ours, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and loneliness that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.

Upanishads

The Upanishads answer with a way of seeing, not a technique. Isha 6: "he who beholds all beings in the Self, and the Self in all beings, he never turns away from it."

Chandogya 7.25: "He who sees, perceives, and understands this, loves the Self, delights in the Self, revels in the Self, rejoices in the Self".

This is related to loneliness and not identical to it. Neither passage turns into steps, and the site makes no claim about how to practise them. They should not be read as telling a lonely person to look at things differently. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and loneliness that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.

Christianity

The Christian texts counsel asking, not going alone, and finding a home.

  1. Ask. Psalm 25:16: "Turn to me and be gracious to me, for I am lonely and afflicted."
  2. Do not go alone. Ecclesiastes 4:9-10: "Two are better than one because they have a good reward for their toil. For if they fall, one will lift up the other".
  3. Find a home. Psalm 68:6: "God gives the desolate a home to live in".

This is related to loneliness and not identical to it. These verses do not say how a person makes a friend. Reading them as a way through loneliness is ours, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and loneliness that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.

Buddhism

The Buddhist texts counsel seeking an alert companion, and failing that a blameless solitude.

  1. Look for a good friend. SN 45.2: "Good friends, companions, and associates are the whole of the spiritual life."
  2. Walk with an alert one. Dhp 328: "If you find an alert companion, an attentive friend to live happily together, then, overcoming all adversities, wander with them, joyful and mindful."
  3. If you cannot, be at ease alone. Dhp 330: "Wander alone and do no wrong, at ease like a tusker in the wilds."

This is related to loneliness and not identical to it. The verses also say "there's no fellowship with fools", which is the text's own wording and should not be read as calling lonely people fools. Dhp 330 recommends solitude and is not advice on how to ease an ache. Reading the verses as a way through loneliness is ours, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and loneliness that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.

Philosophical

The philosophers' counsel is to choose faithful company, and to be a good companion to yourself.

  1. Choose faithful company. Epictetus, Discourses 3.13: "it is not the sight of a human creature which removes us from solitude, but the sight of one who is faithful and modest and helpful to us."
  2. Be your own companion. Same chapter: "a man ought to be prepared in a manner for this also, to be able to be sufficient for himself and to be his own companion."
  3. Keep wanting friends. Seneca, Letters 9: "the wise man is self-sufficient. Nevertheless, he desires friends, neighbours, and associates, no matter how much he is sufficient unto himself."

This is related to loneliness and not identical to it. Epictetus is arguing for self-sufficiency, and Seneca answers that the wise person still wants friends. Reading the passages as a way through loneliness is ours, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and loneliness that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.
