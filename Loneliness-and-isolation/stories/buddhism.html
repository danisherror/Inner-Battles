A story

The Buddha and the bull elephant

In Ud 4.5 the Buddha is "crowded by monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen; by rulers and their chief ministers, and monastics of other religions and their disciples." He thinks: "Why don't I live alone, withdrawn from the group?" He sets out "to go wandering alone toward Pārileyyaka, with no companion."

A bull elephant, "crowded by other males, females, younglings, and cubs", has had the same thought: "Why don't I live alone, withdrawn from the herd?" He comes to the Buddha and attends on him, "clearing the vegetation from the place where the Buddha stayed, and using his trunk to set out water for drinking and washing."

The Buddha says: "The giant elephant, with tusks like draft-poles, agrees heart to heart with the spiritual giant, since each finds joy in the woods alone."

What it shows: This is solitude chosen and enjoyed, and not loneliness. The elephant who is also alone becomes a companion, and the text counts that as joy. The link to loneliness is ours. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and loneliness that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.
