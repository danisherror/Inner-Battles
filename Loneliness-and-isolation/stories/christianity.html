A story

Ruth and Naomi

In Ruth 1 Naomi, widowed and with both sons dead, sets out for home. She urges her daughters-in-law to turn back, and one does. Ruth does not: "Do not press me to leave you, to turn back from following you! Where you go, I will go; where you lodge, I will lodge; your people shall be my people and your God my God. Where you die, I will die, and there will I be buried."

At Bethlehem the women ask: "Is this Naomi?" She says: "Call me no longer Naomi; call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt bitterly with me. I went away full, but the Lord has brought me back empty".

What it shows: Naomi arrives home with a companion, though she does not feel it yet. The story is about loyalty and loss and not about loneliness as such; the link is ours. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and loneliness that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.
