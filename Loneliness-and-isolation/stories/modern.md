> **Note:** This is an invented composite story, not a real case. It is built from patterns people commonly describe, so you can see how loneliness works and how people ease it.

**Tomás's story**

Tomás moved to a new city for work. The evenings were long and the flat was quiet. A colleague invited him for a drink and he said he was busy, because he assumed the invitation was politeness. A few weeks later he noticed he had stopped checking his phone.

What helped:

1. **He named it.** "I am lonely" was about now. It did not mean something was wrong with him.
2. **He checked the story.** He asked what else the invitation could have been, and decided it might simply have been an invitation.
3. **He added small contacts.** A weekly call with his brother, and a Thursday walk with the colleague.
4. **He found a shared task.** He joined a five-a-side game, where there was something to do besides talk.
5. **He reached out first.** He asked two people from the game to dinner.
6. **He asked for more help.** Some evenings were still heavy, so he told his doctor, who suggested a counsellor.

It took months, and some weeks he still cancelled.

**What it shows:** Tomás did not need a crowd. A few regular contacts, and a different reading of one invitation, were enough to start.
