Perfectionism

Holding yourself to standards so high that starting, finishing or being seen feels unsafe.

Root causes

Researchers point to the culture people grow up in, and to how worth gets tied to performance.

  • Rising demands. Curran and Hill pooled 164 samples and 41,641 college students in the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom, tested between 1989 and 2016. Self-oriented, socially prescribed and other-oriented perfectionism all rose over time, and young people "perceive that others are more demanding of them, are more demanding of others, and are more demanding of themselves." The authors suggest that competitive individualism, emphasised since the 1980s, is part of the reason.
  • Worth tied to results. The "concerns" side of perfectionism is worry about mistakes and about being judged (Stoeber and Otto). When a mistake feels like a verdict on you, avoiding mistakes becomes the goal.
  • Family and school. Early criticism, praise only for results and a fear of disappointing people are commonly given as roots. These are common explanations, not findings from the papers below.

Sources#Link to Sources

  • Curran and Hill (2019), Psychological Bulletin 145(4), 410-429. doi:10.1037/bul0000138 (external link)
  • Stoeber and Otto (2006), Personality and Social Psychology Review 10(4), 295-319. doi:10.1207/s15327957pspr1004_2 (external link)

The findings were read in the abstracts (Europe PMC, 2026-11-14), not the full papers. The cohort study measures college students in three countries and cannot show that culture caused the rise; the abstract says the authors "seemingly" see people responding to cultural change. The third bullet is common explanation and is not drawn from a study read here.
