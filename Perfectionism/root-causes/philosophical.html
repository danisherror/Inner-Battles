Perfectionism

Holding yourself to standards so high that starting, finishing or being seen feels unsafe.

Philosophical lens

Root causes

The Stoics place the root in what we think we own and what we think we must achieve.

  • Putting our worth in what we do not control. Epictetus, Enchiridion 1: "Things in our control are opinion, pursuit, desire, aversion, and, in a word, whatever are our own actions. Things not in our control are body, property, reputation, command, and, in one word, whatever are not our own actions." If a flawless result or other people's praise is what we need, we have put our peace in something we cannot command.
  • The demand to succeed every time. Marcus Aurelius warns against being "disgusted, nor discouraged, nor dissatisfied, if thou dost not succeed in doing everything according to right principles". That he needs to say so suggests that failing to meet one's own principles was a real source of distress.

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. Epictetus and Marcus are speaking of how to live well, not describing a pattern of harsh self-judgement; the link is ours.
