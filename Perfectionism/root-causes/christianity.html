Inner battle

Perfectionism

Holding yourself to standards so high that starting, finishing or being seen feels unsafe.

Christianity lens

Root causes

The Christian texts see the root in trying to earn one's standing, and in forgetting what we are.

  • Earning acceptance. Ephesians 2:8-9: "For by grace you have been saved through faith, and this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God—not the result of works, so that no one may boast." The verse is answering the idea that standing before God has to be earned by flawless works.
  • Forgetting what we are. Psalm 103:13-14: "As a father has compassion for his children, so the LORD has compassion for those who fear him. For he knows how we were made; he remembers that we are dust." A demand for perfection overlooks that.
  • Pride in one's own record. The words "so that no one may boast" place the harm in a wish to stand on one's own achievement.

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. The verses speak of grace, works and pride in theological terms, and do not describe anxiety about mistakes. Linking them to perfectionism is a reading.
