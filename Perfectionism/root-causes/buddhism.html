Perfectionism

Holding yourself to standards so high that starting, finishing or being seen feels unsafe.

Root causes

The Buddhist texts have no account of the roots of perfectionism, but the story of Soṇa (AN 6.55) shows how over-striving starts.

  • Effort measured against the result. Soṇa thinks: "I am one of the Buddha's most energetic disciples. Yet my mind is not freed from defilements by not grasping." He has put in the effort and the result is not there, so he decides the effort or the training must be wrong.
  • Comparison. He compares himself with other disciples ("one of the Buddha's most energetic"). The sutta does not treat this as a fault; it is a fact about how he reasons.
  • An all-or-nothing choice. His thought is to give up and return to "a lesser life", not to adjust his practice.

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. The sutta does not use the language of standards or perfection, and the roots listed above are our reading of Soṇa's thought, not statements in the text.
