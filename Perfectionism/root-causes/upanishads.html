Perfectionism

Holding yourself to standards so high that starting, finishing or being seen feels unsafe.

Upanishads lens

Root causes

The Upanishads do not analyse perfectionism, but two passages touch on its roots: wanting what belongs to someone else, and tying one's worth to learning and achievement.

  • Covetousness. Isha 1 ends: "Do not covet the wealth of any man!" Measuring ourselves against another's accomplishment is a kind of covetousness, though the verse speaks of wealth.
  • Worth through attainment. In the Chandogya Upanishad (6.1), Svetaketu returns from twelve years of study "having then studied all the Vedas,—conceited, considering himself well-read, and stern". His father asks: "have you ever asked for that instruction by which we hear what cannot be heard, by which we perceive what cannot be perceived, by which we know what cannot be known?" The story shows a person whose standing rests on achievement and who still lacks something.

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. Neither passage speaks of fear of mistakes; they are read here for the root they share with perfectionism, which is worth that depends on what we have achieved. That connection is ours.
