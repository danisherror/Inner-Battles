Perfectionism

Holding yourself to standards so high that starting, finishing or being seen feels unsafe.

Hinduism lens

Root causes

The Gita finds the roots in attachment to results and in comparing one's own path with another's.

  • Attachment to the fruit. Gita 2.47: "You have a right to perform your prescribed duties, but you are not entitled to the fruits of your actions. Never consider yourself to be the cause of the results of your activities, nor be attached to inaction." When the result is what the self depends on, anything less than a perfect result feels like a threat.
  • Attachment to success and failure. 2.48 names "attachment to success and failure" as what equanimity gives up.
  • Looking at another's path. 3.35: "It is far better to perform one's natural prescribed duty, though tinged with faults, than to perform another's prescribed duty, though perfectly." The pull to measure oneself against someone else's performance is the root in view here.
  • Seeing only the defect. 18.48 says that people recoil from their duty when they see its defects, and states that "all endeavors are veiled by some evil, as fire is by smoke".

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. The verses speak of attachment to results and of duty; the link to the modern pattern is ours.
