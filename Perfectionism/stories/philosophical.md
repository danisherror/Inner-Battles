**Marcus Aurelius and the sponge**

In the *Meditations*, the emperor Marcus Aurelius writes notes to himself. In one of them he considers what to do on days when he has not lived up to his own principles.

He does not tell himself to try harder. He writes: "Be not disgusted, nor discouraged, nor dissatisfied, if thou dost not succeed in doing everything according to right principles; but when thou hast failed, return back again, and be content if the greater part of what thou doest is consistent with man's nature, and love this to which thou returnest."

Then he adds an image: "do not return to philosophy as if she were a master, but act like those who have sore eyes and apply a bit of sponge and egg, or as another applies a plaster, or drenching with water."

**What it shows:** Marcus treats philosophy as a remedy to be used, and failure as a reason to return to it without drama. The passage does not use the word perfectionism; it speaks to the habit of being disgusted with oneself, and offers a lighter way back.
