A story

Svetaketu and the instruction not yet heard

The Chandogya Upanishad tells of Svetaketu, whose father says to him: "go to school; for there is none belonging to our race, darling, who, not having studied (the Veda), is, as it were, a Brâhmana by birth only" (6.1.1).

He begins at twelve and returns at twenty-four, "having then studied all the Vedas,—conceited, considering himself well-read, and stern" (6.1.2).

His father asks: "have you ever asked for that instruction by which we hear what cannot be heard, by which we perceive what cannot be perceived, by which we know what cannot be known?" (6.1.3). Svetaketu does not know what it is, and the father explains with an image: "as by one clod of clay all that is made of clay is known" (6.1.4).

Svetaketu answers: "Surely those venerable men (my teachers) did not know that. For if they had known it, why should they not have told it me? Do you, Sir, therefore tell me that" (6.1.7). He asks to be taught.

What it shows: Svetaketu has done everything asked of him and is full of knowledge, yet his father shows him that something is still missing. The story does not mention mistakes or perfectionism; it shows a person whose standing rests on achievement, and who learns by asking.

Sources#Link to Sources

  • Chandogya Upanishad, sixth Prapâthaka (external link) 1.1-7 (Max Müller's translation)

The quotations were read in Müller's translation on Wikisource (page images of volume 1) on 2026-11-14. Only 6.1 was read; the father's teaching in the rest of chapter 6 was not. Connecting the story to perfectionism is a reading and the text does not make it.
