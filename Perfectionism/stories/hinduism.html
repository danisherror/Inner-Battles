A story

Krishna's lesson on defects and duty

The Gita is a conversation between Krishna and Arjuna, who is hesitating over his duty on the eve of a battle.

Near the end of the dialogue, Krishna speaks of the work each person is born to: "It is better to do one's own dharma, even though imperfectly, than to do another's dharma, even though perfectly" (18.47).

He then answers the objection that the work has faults: "One should not abandon duties born of one's nature, even if one sees defects in them, O son of Kunti. Indeed, all endeavors are veiled by some evil, as fire is by smoke" (18.48).

The verses are not addressed to perfectionism, but the image fits it: fire gives light and heat, and smoke is part of how it burns.

What it shows: A flaw in the work is not a reason to put the work down. The image of fire and smoke says that every action comes with some imperfection, and the teaching is to act without attachment to a flawless result.
