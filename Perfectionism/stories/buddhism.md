**Soṇa and the harp**

Venerable Soṇa has been practising hard. In private retreat he thinks: "I am one of the Buddha's most energetic disciples. Yet my mind is not freed from defilements by not grasping. But my family has wealth. I could enjoy that wealth and make merit. Why don't I resign the training and return to a lesser life, so I can enjoy my wealth and make merit?"

The Buddha knows his thought and appears in front of him. He asks: "When you were still a layman, weren't you a good player of the arched harp?" Soṇa says yes. "When your harp's strings were tuned too tight, was it resonant and playable?" "No, sir." The Buddha asks the same about strings tuned too slack. Then: "But when your harp's strings were tuned neither too tight nor too slack, but fixed at an even tension, was it resonant and playable?"

The Buddha says: "In the same way, Soṇa, when energy is too forceful it leads to restlessness. When energy is too slack it leads to laziness. So, Soṇa, you should focus on energy and serenity, find a balance of the faculties, and learn the character of this situation."

Soṇa does so, and "soon realized the supreme culmination of the spiritual path in this very life."

**What it shows:** Soṇa was not lazy; he was straining, and he took the lack of a result to mean that he should quit. The Buddha does not tell him to try less or to try harder, but to tune the effort. The sutta is about meditation, and its use for perfectionism is a reading.
