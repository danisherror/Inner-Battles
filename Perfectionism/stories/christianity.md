**Paul and the thorn**

In 2 Corinthians 12, Paul writes of a "thorn" he was given: "a thorn was given me in the flesh, a messenger of Satan to torment me, to keep me from being too elated." He says: "Three times I appealed to the Lord about this, that it would leave me" (12:7-8).

The answer he reports: "My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness" (12:9). And he concludes: "Therefore I am content with weaknesses, insults, hardships, persecutions, and calamities for the sake of Christ, for whenever I am weak, then I am strong" (12:10).

The text does not say what the thorn was, and readers have suggested many things. The story is not about perfectionism, but it shows a person asking for a flaw to be removed and being told that it stays, and that his standing does not depend on being rid of it.

**What it shows:** A flaw can remain and the person can still be accepted and useful. The story does not say that effort is pointless; it says that a person's strength is not measured by the absence of weakness.
