The effects depend on which side of perfectionism is stronger.

- **Strivings can help.** Stoeber and Otto's review found that perfectionistic strivings are associated with positive characteristics, and that "healthy perfectionists" show higher levels of positive characteristics than "unhealthy perfectionists" and non-perfectionists.
- **Worry about mistakes changes that.** The same review concludes that strivings are positive only "if perfectionists are not overly concerned about mistakes and negative evaluations by others."
- **Rising demands.** Curran and Hill's results mean more young people report that others demand more of them and that they demand more of themselves, which makes the second pattern more common.
- **Commonly reported costs.** People describe putting work off for fear it will not be good enough (see [procrastination](../../Procrastination/what-it-is/modern.html)), never calling a piece of work finished, sleeplessness and exhaustion. These are common descriptions, not findings from the papers cited here.

If perfectionism comes with low mood, anxiety, an eating problem or constant exhaustion, a doctor or therapist can help.
