Philosophical lens

Effects on life

The philosophers describe what failing to meet a standard does to a person, and the cost of seeking more exactness than a matter allows.

  • Disgust, discouragement and dissatisfaction. Marcus Aurelius names these three as the reaction to failing to do "everything according to right principles". He tells himself to "return back again" instead.
  • Misplaced exactness. Aristotle (Nicomachean Ethics 1.3) says that "precision is not to be sought for alike in all discussions, any more than in all the products of the crafts", and that in subjects with "variety and fluctuation" we should "indicate the truth roughly and in outline". Demanding more precision than a matter allows is a mistake of method.
  • Ignoring the person. Aristotle's mean is "the mean relative to us" (2.6): the right amount depends on the person, not on a single flawless standard.

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. The effects are our reading of what each philosopher says about exactness and failure.
