The Gita links over-attachment to results with agitation, and the fear of defects with giving up.

- **Loss of equanimity.** Gita 2.48 asks for "abandoning attachment to success and failure", and calls the resulting "equanimity" Yog. By implication, attachment to the outcome costs us that evenness; the verse does not describe the cost directly.
- **Abandoning the work.** 18.48 says: "One should not abandon duties born of one's nature, even if one sees defects in them." The translator's commentary adds that people recoil from their duty when they see a defect in it. Holding out for flawless work can end in doing nothing.
- **Skill is not lost by letting go.** The commentary on 2.50 says that working without attachment to results "does not reduce the quality of our work; instead we become even more skillful than before".

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. The verses speak of duty and attachment; the effects listed here are our reading of them.
