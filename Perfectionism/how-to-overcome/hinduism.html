Inner battle

Perfectionism

Holding yourself to standards so high that starting, finishing or being seen feels unsafe.

How to overcome it

The Gita's answer is to put the effort into the work and to let go of the result.

  1. Do the work, release the fruit. 2.47: "You have a right to perform your prescribed duties, but you are not entitled to the fruits of your actions." Standards stay; the demand on the result goes.
  2. Practise equanimity. 2.48: "Be steadfast in the performance of your duty, O Arjun, abandoning attachment to success and failure. Such equanimity is called Yog."
  3. Work skilfully. 2.50: "strive for Yog, which is the art of working skillfully (in proper consciousness)." The commentary says that working without attachment does not lower the quality of the work.
  4. Accept that no work is free of defect. 18.48: "all endeavors are veiled by some evil, as fire is by smoke". The point is to act anyway.
  5. Do your own work, even imperfectly. 18.47: "It is better to do one's own dharma, even though imperfectly, than to do another's dharma, even though perfectly."

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. The Gita's guidance is about acting without attachment to results, and applying it to perfectionism is our reading.
