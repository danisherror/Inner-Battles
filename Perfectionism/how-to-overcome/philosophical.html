Inner battle

Perfectionism

Holding yourself to standards so high that starting, finishing or being seen feels unsafe.

Philosophical lens

How to overcome it

The philosophers advise fitting the standard to the matter and returning after failing.

  1. Match the precision to the subject. Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics 1.3: "Our discussion will be adequate if it has as much clearness as the subject-matter admits of".
  2. Find the mean for you. Virtue is "the mean relative to us", between excess and defect (2.6).
  3. Return without disgust. Marcus Aurelius: "when thou hast failed, return back again, and be content if the greater part of what thou doest is consistent with man's nature, and love this to which thou returnest".
  4. Keep it light. He adds: "do not return to philosophy as if she were a master, but act like those who have sore eyes and apply a bit of sponge and egg". Treat the practice as a remedy, not a taskmaster.
  5. Sort what you control. Epictetus, Enchiridion 1: opinion, pursuit, desire and aversion are in our control; "reputation" is not.

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. These are rules for living and reasoning, and applying them to perfectionism is a reading.
