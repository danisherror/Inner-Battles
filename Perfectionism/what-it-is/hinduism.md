Hinduism has no single word for perfectionism, but the Bhagavad Gita speaks closely to its two sides: being tied to success and failure, and refusing to act unless the work can be flawless.

On the first, Krishna says in 2.48: "Be steadfast in the performance of your duty, O Arjun, abandoning attachment to success and failure. Such equanimity is called Yog."

On the second, 18.48 says: "One should not abandon duties born of one's nature, even if one sees defects in them, O son of Kunti. Indeed, all endeavors are veiled by some evil, as fire is by smoke."

And 18.47 puts the standard in a different place: "It is better to do one's own dharma, even though imperfectly, than to do another's dharma, even though perfectly."

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. The Gita is speaking of attachment to the results of action and of duty, and it does not describe the modern pattern of self-judgement over mistakes. The Gita also speaks of attaining "perfection" through one's duties (18.45), so the word there refers to spiritual fulfilment and not to flawless performance; that point is a reading of the verse, not checked against the Sanskrit.
