Perfectionism is holding yourself to standards so high, or so rigid, that anything short of flawless feels like failure. Psychologists do not treat it as one thing:

- **Perfectionistic strivings**: setting very high standards and working hard to meet them.
- **Perfectionistic concerns**: worry about mistakes and about how others will judge you.
- **Three directions**: the Multidimensional Perfectionism Scale (Hewitt and Flett, 1991) measures self-oriented perfectionism (demands on yourself), socially prescribed perfectionism (the sense that others demand perfection of you) and other-oriented perfectionism (demands on other people).

High standards are not the problem in themselves. Stoeber and Otto's review found that perfectionistic strivings are associated with positive characteristics, and concluded that self-oriented strivings are positive "if perfectionists are not overly concerned about mistakes and negative evaluations by others". The trouble starts when the standard becomes a test of your worth.

Perfectionism is not a diagnosis, and it can come with anxiety, depression or eating problems. If it is taking over your life, a doctor or therapist can help.
