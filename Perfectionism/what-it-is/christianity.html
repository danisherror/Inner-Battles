Perfectionism

Holding yourself to standards so high that starting, finishing or being seen feels unsafe.

Christianity lens

What it is

Christian texts do not name perfectionism, but the word "perfect" is in the New Testament, and some people take it as a demand for flawlessness. Jesus says in Matthew 5:48: "Be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect."

The same texts also describe weakness and unfinished progress as the setting of faith. Paul writes in Philippians 3:12: "Not that I have already obtained this or have already reached the goal, but I press on to lay hold of that for which Christ has laid hold of me." And in 2 Corinthians 12:9 he reports the answer he was given: "My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness."

Many readers take "perfect" in Matthew 5:48 to mean complete or mature and not faultless. That reading was not checked here against a Greek lexicon, so treat it as one common view.

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. The verses speak of holiness, grace and weakness, and not of anxiety about mistakes.
