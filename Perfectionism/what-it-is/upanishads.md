The Upanishads do not discuss perfectionism, and no verse here is about it directly. What they do offer is a picture of what is whole and flawless, and it is not the person's performance.

The Isha Upanishad describes the Self in verse 8: "He (the Self) encircled all, bright, incorporeal, scatheless, without muscles, pure, untouched by evil; a seer, wise, omnipresent, self-existent". Verse 2 says of work: "Though a man may wish to live a hundred years, performing works, it will be thus with him; but not in any other way: work will thus not cling to a man."

Read together, the two verses suggest that what is untouched and complete is the Self, while work is something done that need not cling to us. That is a reading and not a statement in the text. Müller himself says in his introduction that this Upanishad is "one of the most difficult to understand properly" and that commentators have disputed verse 2.

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. The Upanishad speaks of the Self and of action without clinging, not of standards or of fear of mistakes.
