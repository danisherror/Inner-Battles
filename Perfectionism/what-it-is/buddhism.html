Perfectionism

Holding yourself to standards so high that starting, finishing or being seen feels unsafe.

Buddhism lens

What it is

Buddhist texts have no word for perfectionism, but one famous sutta describes over-striving closely. In AN 6.55, Venerable Soṇa thinks in private retreat: "I am one of the Buddha's most energetic disciples. Yet my mind is not freed from defilements by not grasping." He considers giving up the training and going back to a "lesser life".

The Buddha asks him about the harp he played as a layman. Strings tuned too tight, or too slack, did not make it "resonant and playable". Then he says: "In the same way, Soṇa, when energy is too forceful it leads to restlessness. When energy is too slack it leads to laziness."

Elsewhere the Buddha speaks of "two extremes" and a "middle way of practice" (SN 56.11); there the extremes are sensual indulgence and self-mortification.

This is related to perfectionism and not identical to it. The sutta is about effort in meditation, and the texts do not discuss standards, mistakes or the fear of judgement as a pattern of its own.
