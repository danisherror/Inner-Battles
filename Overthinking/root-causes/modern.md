Researchers describe overthinking as something that develops and is kept going by several factors working together.

- **A habit that grows out of an unresolved goal.** In their model of rumination, Watkins and Roberts say it results from "dwelling on problematic goals developing into a learnt habit". In plain terms, a problem you have not solved or let go of keeps pulling your thoughts back.
- **Abstract thinking.** The same model points to a tendency to process negative information in an abstract way (asking "why does this always happen to me?") and not concretely (asking "what exactly happened, and what is the next step?"). Watkins's earlier review identifies the level of construal, abstract against concrete, as one of the principles that explain whether repetitive thought is constructive or not.
- **Weak mental control and negative bias.** The model adds poor executive control, the ability to steer and switch attention, and a tendency to pick up negative information.
- **Negative styles of thinking.** Nolen-Hoeksema and colleagues report that rumination interacts with negative cognitive styles to predict how long depressive symptoms last.
