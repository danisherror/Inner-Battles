Root causes

The Gita traces the restless, circling mind to dwelling, desire and an undisciplined mind.

  • Dwelling on what attracts. Verse 2.62: "While contemplating on the objects of the senses, one develops attachment to them. Attachment leads to desire, and from desire arises anger." The loop begins with attention that keeps returning.
  • The nature of the mind. Arjuna in 6.34 calls it "restless, turbulent, strong and obstinate". The problem is a mind that moves, not a failing of character.
  • Not being steady. Verse 2.66 says an undisciplined person "who has not controlled the mind and senses, can neither have a resolute intellect nor steady contemplation".
  • Unmet desires pouring in. Verse 2.70 compares the sage to the ocean, unmoved "by the incessant flow of waters from rivers merging into it", and contrasts "the person who strives to satisfy desires".
