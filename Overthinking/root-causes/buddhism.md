The Buddhist texts point to an unsettled mind, to resisting pain, and to the way habits of thought become inclinations.

- **An unsettled mind, attended to unwisely.** SN 46.51 asks what fuels restlessness and remorse: "There is the unsettled mind. Frequent irrational application of mind to that fuels the arising of restlessness and remorse, or, when they have arisen, makes them increase and grow."
- **Resisting pain and reaching for relief.** SN 36.6: when an unlearned person is touched by painful feeling "they resist it", and "look forward to enjoying sensual pleasures", because they "don't understand any escape from painful feeling apart from sensual pleasures."
- **Habit.** MN 19: "Whatever a mendicant frequently thinks about and considers becomes their heart's inclination."
- **A mind that is hard to hold.** Dhp 34 compares it to "a fish pulled from the sea and cast upon the shore", which "flounders about".
