Overthinking

Going round the same thoughts about the past or the future, unable to settle or to act.

Root causes

The philosophers find the roots in our judgments, in running ahead of events, and in not being able to be still.

  • Judgments, not things. Epictetus, Enchiridion 5: "Men are disturbed, not by things, but by the principles and notions which they form concerning things." Marcus Aurelius says the same: "If thou art pained by any external thing, it is not this thing that disturbs thee, but thy own judgement about it" (Meditations 8).
  • Exaggerating, imagining and anticipating. Seneca, Letter 13: "We are in the habit of exaggerating, or imagining, or anticipating, sorrow." He also says: "it is more often the case that we are troubled by our apprehensions".
  • Striving over what is not in our control. Epictetus, Enchiridion 1: "Things not in our control are body, property, reputation, command, and, in one word, whatever are not our own actions."
  • Being unable to be still. Pascal: unhappiness comes from the fact "that they cannot stay quietly in their own chamber."
