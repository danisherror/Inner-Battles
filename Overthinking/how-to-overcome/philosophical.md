The philosophers' remedies are to test the thought, change the judgment, and focus on what is in your control.

- **Change the judgment.** Marcus Aurelius: "it is in thy power to wipe out this judgement now."
- **Sort what is yours.** Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 1: opinion, pursuit, desire and aversion are in our control; body, property and reputation are not. Spend effort on the first.
- **Cross-examine the fear.** Seneca, Letter 13: "Put the question voluntarily to yourself: 'Am I tormented without sufficient reason, am I morose, and do I convert what is not an evil into what is an evil?'"
- **Test the evidence.** Seneca's rule is to ask whether the trouble is present or only to come: "As to things present, the decision is easy... To-day there is nothing wrong with it." And for the future: "consider whether your proofs of future trouble are sure."
- **Look for the thought behind the feeling.** Epictetus, section 5: "When therefore we are hindered, or disturbed, or grieved, let us never attribute it to others, but to ourselves; that is, to our own principles."
