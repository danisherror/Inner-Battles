The Buddhist remedies are to settle the mind, to sort thoughts, and to feel one arrow and not two.

- **Starve what fuels it.** SN 46.51: "There is the settled mind. Frequent rational application of mind to that starves the arising of restlessness and remorse, or, when they have arisen, starves their increase and growth."
- **Sort your thoughts.** In MN 19 the Buddha describes sorting thoughts into two classes: sensual, malicious and cruel thoughts in one, and thoughts of renunciation, good will and harmlessness in the other. When a harmful thought arose he reflected that it leads to hurting himself, others and both, and that it blocks wisdom, and "it went away".
- **Guard the mind.** Dhp 33: "The discerning straighten it out, like a fletcher an arrow." Dhp 36: "the discerning protect the mind, a guarded mind leads to bliss."
- **Settle and still it.** In MN 19 he says: "I stilled, settled, unified, and immersed my mind internally... So that my mind would not be stressed."
- **Feel one arrow.** SN 36.6: the learned disciple, touched by painful feeling, does not sorrow or wail, and "experiences one feeling: physical, not mental."
