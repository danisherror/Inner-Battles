The Upanishadic answer is to still the mind, hold the reins, and become the witness.

- **Still the mind.** Katha 2.3.10-11: "When the five instruments of knowledge stand still together with the mind, and when the intellect does not move, that is called the highest state. This, the firm holding back of the senses, is what is called Yoga. He must be free from thoughtlessness then, for Yoga comes and goes."
- **Hold the reins.** Katha 1.3.6: "he who has understanding and whose mind is always firmly held, his senses are under control, like good horses of a charioteer."
- **Be the onlooker.** In the image of the two birds, grief passes when the man on the tree sees "the other lord contented and knows his glory" (Mundaka 3.1.2). The second bird "looks on without eating" (3.1.1).
- **Wake up.** Katha 1.3.14: "Rise, awake! having obtained your boons, understand them!"

The last words of Katha 2.3.11, that "Yoga comes and goes", are an honest note that stillness is not permanent.
