Overthinking

Going round the same thoughts about the past or the future, unable to settle or to act.

What it is

The Upanishads have no word for overthinking. They speak of the unsteady mind and of grief that does not let go, and they set against these a still mind and a witness.

In the Katha Upanishad, the self is compared to a rider in a chariot: the body is the chariot, the intellect the charioteer and the mind the reins (1.3.3-4). "He who has no understanding and whose mind (the reins) is never firmly held, his senses (horses) are unmanageable, like vicious horses of a charioteer" (1.3.5).

The Mundaka Upanishad gives an image of two birds on one tree. "On the same tree man sits grieving, immersed, bewildered by his own impotence. But when he sees the other lord contented and knows his glory, then his grief passes away" (3.1.2).

This is related to overthinking and not identical to it: the texts speak of the restless mind and of grief, within a path toward knowledge of the Self, and not of everyday loops of thought.
