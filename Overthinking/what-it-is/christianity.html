Overthinking

Going round the same thoughts about the past or the future, unable to settle or to act.

What it is

The Bible has no word for overthinking, but it names its relatives: worry, anxiety and a mind that is "disquieted within me".

Jesus says: "do not worry about your life" (Matthew 6:25). To Martha he says: "you are worried and distracted by many things" (Luke 10:41). Proverbs 12:25 says: "Anxiety weighs down the human heart, but a good word cheers it up." The psalmist asks himself: "Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why are you disquieted within me?" (Psalm 42:5).

The desert tradition calls tempting inner thoughts logismoi, "thoughts". Evagrius of Pontus writes: "Whether these thoughts are able to disturb the soul or not is not up to us; but whether they linger or not, and whether they arouse passions or not; that is up to us."

This is related to overthinking and not identical to it. Scripture speaks of worry about life and provision, and Evagrius of the thoughts that tempt a monk, and neither describes a general habit of looping over the past and future.
