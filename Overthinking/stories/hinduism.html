A story

Arjuna and the wind

In chapter 6 of the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna has been teaching Arjuna how to steady the mind in meditation. Arjuna interrupts with an honest objection. In 6.33 he says: "The system of Yog that you have described, O Madhusudan, appears impractical and unattainable to me, due to the restless mind."

He goes on in 6.34: "The mind is very restless, turbulent, strong and obstinate, O Krishna. It appears to me that it is more difficult to control than the wind."

Krishna does not tell him he is wrong. In 6.35 he answers: "what you say is correct; the mind is indeed very difficult to restrain. But by practice and detachment, it can be controlled."

And in 6.26 he adds what to do in practice: "Whenever and wherever the restless and unsteady mind wanders, one should bring it back".

What it shows: The restless mind is treated as ordinary, even for the best warrior in the story. The remedy offered is not a one-time victory but steady returning, each time it wanders.
