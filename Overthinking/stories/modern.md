**Anil's story**

In a team meeting, Anil disagreed with his manager's plan. His manager said, "Let's take that offline," and moved on.

By midnight, Anil was lying awake replaying it. Had he sounded rude? Had the tone changed? Why does this always happen to me? What if it counted against him in the next review? Each pass through produced no new fact. It only produced more dread.

The next day he was tired, short with a colleague, and could not focus on his own work.

What helped:

1. **He named it.** "This is rumination. I'm going round, not solving."
2. **He asked a concrete question.** Instead of "why does this always happen?", he asked "what is one thing I can do?" The answer was to send his manager a two-line note: "Thanks for hearing me out. Happy to talk through the plan whenever suits."
3. **He changed the way he talked to himself.** He tried, "Anil, you made a reasonable point. What would you tell a friend?"
4. **He gave the worry a limit.** Ten minutes in the evening to think it through on paper, then a walk.
5. **He noticed that it was not getting better.** After two weeks of poor sleep, he booked a visit with his doctor.

His manager replied the next morning with a thumbs-up and a time for a chat. The loop did not vanish at once, but it got shorter each time.

**What it shows:** Going round a problem feels like work and rarely is. A concrete next step, a kinder voice and a time limit moved Anil from replaying to doing.
