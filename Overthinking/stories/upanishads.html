A story

The two birds on one tree

The Mundaka Upanishad tells a short parable. "Two birds, inseparable friends, cling to the same tree. One of them eats the sweet fruit, the other looks on without eating" (3.1.1).

The first bird stands for the person caught up in life, tasting each fruit and being moved by it. The Upanishad says of this one: "On the same tree man sits grieving, immersed, bewildered by his own impotence" (3.1.2).

The second bird only watches. And the verse turns on what happens when the first bird looks at it: "But when he sees the other lord contented and knows his glory, then his grief passes away."

What it shows: The image says that the same person has two positions, one inside the thoughts and one watching them, and that grief eases when attention shifts from the first to the second. The teaching is that the watcher is already there.
