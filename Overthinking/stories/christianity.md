**Martha and Mary**

In Luke's Gospel, Jesus comes to a village where a woman named Martha welcomes him. Her sister Mary "sat at Jesus's feet and listened to what he was saying" (Luke 10:39).

"But Martha was distracted by her many tasks, so she came to him and asked, 'Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to do all the work by myself? Tell her, then, to help me.'"

Jesus answers: "Martha, Martha, you are worried and distracted by many things, but few things are needed—indeed only one. Mary has chosen the better part, which will not be taken away from her" (10:40-42).

Martha is not described as overthinking in so many words. The story shows a related state: a mind pulled in many directions, a rising resentment, and a sense that everything depends on her.

**What it shows:** Jesus does not scold her for caring. He names the state ("worried and distracted by many things") and points to the one thing that matters. The way out is shown as choosing, not as thinking harder.
