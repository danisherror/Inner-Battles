Burnout is exhaustion from long strain that has left you drained, distant and doubting your own use. Apathy is the flatness that can come with it: little interest in what once mattered. Those are standard descriptions and not taken from the sources below, and apathy is not defined in them.

Maslach, Schaufeli and Leiter write that "Burnout is a prolonged response to chronic emotional and interpersonal stressors on the job, and is defined by the three dimensions of exhaustion, cynicism, and inefficacy." The World Health Organization's ICD-11 describes it as "a syndrome conceptualized as resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed", with three dimensions: "feelings of energy depletion or exhaustion; increased mental distance from one's job, or feelings of negativism or cynicism related to one's job; and reduced professional efficacy." The WHO says burn-out "is not classified as a medical condition", and "should not be applied to describe experiences in other areas of life."

Everyday signs, as people commonly describe them and not as findings from these sources: waking tired, dreading what you used to manage, snapping, and feeling nothing about things that used to matter. It is a cousin of [sloth](../../Sloth/what-it-is/modern.html), and it is not laziness.

Burnout is not laziness, and it is nothing to be ashamed of. If exhaustion or flatness lasts, or comes with low mood, hopelessness or thoughts of harming yourself, please talk to a doctor, therapist or someone you trust; a doctor can also check whether something else, such as illness or depression, is involved (a standard statement, not drawn from the sources below). If you may harm yourself, contact your local emergency services now; [the International Association for Suicide Prevention points to a directory of verified helplines in over 150 countries](https://www.iasp.info/crisis-centres-helplines/).
