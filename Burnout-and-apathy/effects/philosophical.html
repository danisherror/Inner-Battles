Effects on life

Seneca names the effect of unbroken work: "continuous toil produces a sort of numbness and sluggishness." And: "the liveliness of our minds will be destroyed by unceasing labour, but they will recover their strength after a short period of rest and relief".

Aristotle names the cost of making amusement the aim: "it would, indeed, be strange if the end were amusement, and one were to take trouble and suffer hardship all one's life in order to amuse oneself."

This is related to burnout and not identical to it. Seneca is writing about the mind and Aristotle about the purpose of amusement, and neither describes a syndrome. Reading them as effects of burnout is ours. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and exhaustion that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.
