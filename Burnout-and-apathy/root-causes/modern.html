Root causes

The sources read here place the cause in work and its setting, as well as in the person.

  • Chronic strain. The WHO's description: "chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed."
  • The organisation. Maslach and colleagues say research "places the individual stress experience within a larger organizational context of people's relation to their work." Panagioti and colleagues conclude: "This finding provides support for the view that burnout is a problem of the whole health care organization, rather than individuals."
  • Long strain without recovery. Common description, not from these sources: overload, little control, little reward, unfair treatment, a team that has broken apart, and work that clashes with your values.
  • Other causes of flatness. Also common description: apathy can come from illness, grief or depression, and not only from work.

Burnout is not a character flaw. Burnout is not laziness, and it is nothing to be ashamed of. If exhaustion or flatness lasts, or comes with low mood, hopelessness or thoughts of harming yourself, please talk to a doctor, therapist or someone you trust; a doctor can also check whether something else, such as illness or depression, is involved (a standard statement, not drawn from the sources below). If you may harm yourself, contact your local emergency services now; the International Association for Suicide Prevention points to a directory of verified helplines in over 150 countries (external link).

Sources

  • Maslach, Schaufeli and Leiter (2001), "Job burnout," Annual Review of Psychology 52, 397-422. doi:10.1146/annurev.psych.52.1.397 (external link)
  • Panagioti and colleagues (2017), "Controlled interventions to reduce burnout in physicians: a systematic review and meta-analysis," JAMA Internal Medicine 177(2), 195. doi:10.1001/jamainternmed.2016.7674 (external link)
  • World Health Organization (2019), "Burn-out an 'occupational phenomenon': International Classification of Diseases" (external link) (28 May 2019), read in full on 2026-11-15.
  • International Association for Suicide Prevention, Crisis Centres & Helplines (external link): read on 2026-11-15; the page says the directory it points to lists verified helplines in over 150 countries.
