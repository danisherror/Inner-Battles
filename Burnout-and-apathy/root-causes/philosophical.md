Seneca places the cause in unbroken labour. *Of Peace of Mind*: "Neither ought we always to keep the mind strained to the same pitch, but it ought sometimes to be relaxed by amusement."

And: "the liveliness of our minds will be destroyed by unceasing labour".

Aristotle gives the reason: "we need relaxation because we cannot work continuously."

This is related to burnout and not identical to it. Both are arguments for rest and not accounts of a syndrome, and neither speaks of unfair or overloaded work. Reading them as roots of burnout is ours. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and exhaustion that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.
