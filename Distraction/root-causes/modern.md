The research read here does not establish what causes chronic distraction, and it points away from blaming devices alone.

- **The mind wanders on its own.** Killingsworth and Gilbert found "that people are thinking about what is not happening almost as often as they are thinking about what is". That was measured in everyday life, with no device needed.
- **Heavy multitasking goes with more interference.** Ophir and colleagues found that "heavy media multitaskers are more susceptible to interference from irrelevant environmental stimuli and from irrelevant representations in memory". But Uncapher and Wagner, reviewing the field, say further research is needed "to determine the direction of causality": it is not clear whether multitasking makes people easier to distract or distractible people multitask more.
- **Devices and cognition.** Wilmer and colleagues find the research on smartphone habits "still limited" and say there are domains where "the scientific literature is not yet mature enough to endorse any firm conclusions".

Common explanations, not findings from these papers: apps and feeds are built to hold attention, a hard task invites escape into an easy one, and tiredness and stress lower the threshold for giving in.
