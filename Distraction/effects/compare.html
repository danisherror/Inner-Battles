Distraction

Lenses to show

Modern

The evidence on what distraction does is mixed, and the reviews say so.

  • Poorer task-switching. Ophir and colleagues found that "heavy media multitaskers performed worse on a test of task-switching ability, likely due to reduced ability to filter out interference from the irrelevant task set".
  • A cautious summary. Uncapher and Wagner conclude that "the balance of evidence suggests that heavier media multitaskers exhibit poorer performance in a number of cognitive domains, relative to lighter media multitaskers (although many studies find no performance differences between groups)". Where there is a relationship it is "often on tasks that require or are influenced by fluctuations in sustained goal-directed attention".
  • Unhappiness. Killingsworth and Gilbert found that a mind not on the task "typically makes them unhappy".

These are associations in particular groups and tasks. They do not show that distraction harms everyone, or that it causes the differences found.

Commonly described effects, not findings from these papers: work that takes twice as long, the feeling of a day gone, and shallower conversations. If your attention problems are affecting work, study or relationships, a doctor can help look for causes.

Sources

  • Ophir, Nass and Wagner (2009), Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 106(37), 15583-15587. doi:10.1073/pnas.0903620106 (external link)
  • Uncapher and Wagner (2018), Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 115(40), 9889-9896. doi:10.1073/pnas.1611612115 (external link)
  • Killingsworth and Gilbert (2010), Science 330(6006), 932. doi:10.1126/science.1192439 (external link)

All three were read in the abstracts (Europe PMC, 2026-11-14), not in full. The last paragraph cites no study.

Hinduism

The Gita names what an uncontrolled mind lacks. Gita 2.66: "For one who never unites the mind with God there is no peace; and how can one who lacks peace be happy?"

It follows on from the earlier half of the verse: "an undisciplined person, who has not controlled the mind and senses, can neither have a resolute intellect nor steady contemplation on God."

This is related to distraction and not identical to it. The verse is about a mind not united with God and the lack of peace that follows, and it is not a description of everyday distraction. Reading it as the toll of scattered attention is ours.

Upanishads

The Katha Upanishad says what following outward things leads to. Katha 2.1.2: "Children follow after outward pleasures, and fall into the snare of wide-spread death. Wise men only, knowing the nature of what is immortal, do not look for anything stable here among things unstable."

This is related to distraction and not identical to it. The verse is about outward pleasures and death, and does not describe attention. Reading it as the toll of constant outward pulling is ours.

Christianity

The Bible shows what a divided mind costs.

  • Instability. James 1:8: "double-minded and unstable in every way".
  • Worry. Luke 10:41: "you are worried and distracted by many things".
  • Chasing wind. Ecclesiastes 4:6: "Better is a handful with quiet than two handfuls with toil and a chasing after wind."

This is related to distraction and not identical to it. These verses speak of doubt, worry and toil in general. Reading them as the toll of scattered attention is ours.

Buddhism

The Buddhist texts say what an untrained mind brings. AN 1.23: "I do not see a single thing that is so very harmful as this mind that is not developed. An undeveloped mind is very harmful."

And AN 1.29: "I do not see a single thing that brings such suffering as this mind that is not developed and cultivated. An undeveloped and uncultivated mind brings suffering."

This is related to distraction and not identical to it. The texts are about the untrained mind in general. Reading them as the toll of scattered attention is ours.

Philosophical

Seneca describes what scattering does to what we take in. Letters 2.2: "Everywhere means nowhere. When a person spends all his time in foreign travel, he ends by having many acquaintances, but no friends."

And 2.3: "a plant which is often moved can never grow strong. There is nothing so efficacious that it can be helpful while it is being shifted about. And in reading of many books is distraction."

This is related to distraction and not identical to it. Seneca is speaking of restless travel and reading, and not of scattered attention in a modern day. Reading him as describing its effects is ours.
