Distraction

A mind pulled in many directions at once, unable to stay with what matters.

How to overcome it

The Buddhist texts teach redirecting the mind, settling it, and cultivating it.

  1. Swap one thought for another. MN 20: "That mendicant should focus on some other subject connected with the skillful." The sutta compares it to "a deft mason or their apprentice who'd knock out or extract a large peg with a finer peg."
  2. Settle the mind. SN 46.51: "There is the settled mind. Frequent rational application of mind to that starves the arising of restlessness and remorse".
  3. Restrain it. Dhp 37: "Those who will restrain the mind are freed from Māra's bonds."
  4. Cultivate it. AN 1.30: "A developed and cultivated mind brings happiness."

This is related to distraction and not identical to it. MN 20 is about unskillful thoughts of desire, hate and delusion, and the other passages are about the mind in general. Nothing here replaces a doctor if you cannot focus even when you want to, and applying MN 20 to ordinary distraction is ours.
