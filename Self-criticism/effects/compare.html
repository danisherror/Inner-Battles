Self-criticism

Effects on life, read through each lens side by side.

Lenses to show

Modern

The research read here is on the caring opposite of self-criticism, and it is mostly correlational.

  • Mental health. MacBeth and Gumley pooled 20 samples from 14 studies, all using Neff's Self-Compassion Scale, and found a large association between self-compassion and psychopathology, r = -0.54: people higher in self-compassion reported fewer symptoms.
  • Well-being. Zessin and colleagues combined 79 samples (N = 16,416) and found a relationship of r = .47 between self-compassion and well-being, "a subsample of studies indicated a causal effect".
  • What this does not show. Correlations do not show that harsh self-criticism causes symptoms, and neither abstract measures self-criticism as such.

Commonly described effects, not findings from these papers: dread of mistakes, avoiding tasks you might do badly, poor sleep and low mood. If these are constant, a doctor or therapist can help.

Sources

  • MacBeth and Gumley (2012), "Exploring compassion: a meta-analysis of the association between self-compassion and psychopathology," Clinical Psychology Review 32(6), 545-552. doi:10.1016/j.cpr.2012.06.003 (external link)
  • Zessin, Dickhäuser and Garbade (2015), Applied Psychology: Health and Well-Being 7(3), 340-364. doi:10.1111/aphw.12051 (external link)

The findings were read in the abstracts (Europe PMC, 2026-11-14), not the full papers. That the Self-Compassion Scale's items include self-judgement is from memory and was not checked. The last paragraph cites no study.

Hinduism

The Gita names the cost of a mind that turns on itself. 6.5 warns against the mind that makes us "degrade yourself", and 6.6 says that for those who have not conquered it "the mind works like an enemy". The cost is that the thing we have to work with is working against us.

The Gita also names what freedom looks like. After the teaching, Arjuna says: "by Your grace my illusion has been dispelled, and I am situated in knowledge. I am now free from doubts, and I shall act according to Your instructions" (18.73).

This is related to self-criticism and not identical to it. The verses are about the untrained mind and about doubt, and the effects of a harsh inner voice are not described.

Sources

  • Bhagavad Gita 6.5 (external link), 6.6 (external link), 18.73 (external link)

Quotations (Swami Mukundananda's translation) were read on those pages on 2026-11-14. Reading these verses as describing the effects of self-criticism is ours.

Upanishads

The Mundaka names the effect as grief: the person "sits grieving, immersed, bewildered by his own impotence" (3.1.2).

The Katha adds a caution, so that the point is not read as ignoring wrongdoing. Verse 1.2.24 says that he "who has not first turned away from his wickedness, who is not tranquil, and subdued, or whose mind is not at rest" cannot obtain the Self even by knowledge. Turning away from what is wrong is part of the path, while grief that goes round and round is not.

This is related to self-criticism and not identical to it. Both passages are about the seeker and the Self, and the effects of a harsh inner voice are not described. The balance drawn here between the two is ours.

Sources

  • Mundaka Upanishad (external link) 3.1.2 (Max Müller's translation)
  • Katha Upanishad (external link) 1.2.24 (Müller)

Quotations were read in Müller's translation on Wikisource on 2026-11-14. The Katha sentence continues "he can never obtain the Self (even) by knowledge"; only its first clauses are quoted.

Christianity

The Bible shows both the weight of self-condemnation and what lifts it.

  • The condemning heart. 1 John 3:20 names it: "whenever our hearts condemn us".
  • Removal. Psalm 103:10-12: "He does not deal with us according to our sins nor repay us according to our iniquities... as far as the east is from the west, so far he removes our transgressions from us."
  • Welcome. The father in the parable runs to his son, "filled with compassion", and says "this son of mine was dead and is alive again; he was lost and is found!" (Luke 15:20, 24). The son's own sentence ("I am no longer worthy") is not what the father carries out.

This is related to self-criticism and not identical to it. The texts speak of sin and forgiveness, and do not describe the toll of a harsh inner voice.

Buddhism

SN 3.4 says what treating oneself badly leads to: "happiness is not easy to find by someone who does bad deeds". The sutta is about harmful action, not self-talk.

Dhp 161 describes the weight of one's own wrongdoing: "For the evil that is done by oneself, born and produced in oneself, grinds down a simpleton, as diamond grinds a lesser gem."

SN 36.6 shows the added suffering of the second arrow. It says the learned disciple, "they don't sorrow or wail or lament": the pain is the same, and the second arrow is missing.

This is related to self-criticism and not identical to it. The texts speak of wrongdoing, pain and the response to it, and not of a harsh inner voice.

Philosophical

The Stoics see the cost in lost time and in a mind at war with itself.

Marcus Aurelius names the reaction to failing: "Be not disgusted, nor discouraged, nor dissatisfied, if thou dost not succeed in doing everything according to right principles" (Meditations 5). Disgust at oneself, discouragement and dissatisfaction are the effects he wants to avoid, and he tells himself instead to "return back again".

Aristotle (Nicomachean Ethics 9.4) says that people who are at odds with themselves are "laden with repentance" and "their soul is rent by faction". He means bad men, so the application to people who are simply hard on themselves is ours.

This is related to self-criticism and not identical to it.

Sources

  • Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 5 (external link) (George Long's translation)
  • Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics 9.4 (external link) (W. D. Ross's translation)

The quotations were read at MIT Classics on 2026-11-14. The page has no paragraph numbers for Marcus. Aristotle's phrase is "bad men are laden with repentance"; only "laden with repentance" is quoted.
