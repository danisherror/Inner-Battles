The Upanishads do not give a root for self-criticism. The Mundaka's image of the grieving bird points to one: identification with the experiencer.

- **Immersion.** "On the same tree man sits grieving, immersed, bewildered by his own impotence" (Mundaka 3.1.2). The person who is fully taken up in what happens to them feels every blow.
- **Not seeing the other.** The verse goes on: "But when he sees the other lord contented and knows his glory, then his grief passes away." The root is that the watching self is overlooked.

This is related to self-criticism and not identical to it. The verse is about grief, and its use here for the inner critic is ours.
