Philosophical lens

Root causes

The philosophers find the root in our own principles and judgements, not in the event. Epictetus, Enchiridion 5: "Men are disturbed, not by things, but by the principles and notions which they form concerning things." The disturbance of self-blame, on this view, comes from the rule we apply: that every mistake should be punished.

Aristotle describes how a person's relation to themselves breaks down. In Nicomachean Ethics 9.4, bad men, "having nothing lovable in them", and "their soul is rent by faction", and "bad men are laden with repentance". For Aristotle this follows from wickedness. Applying it to people who are only hard on themselves would go beyond his text.

This is related to self-criticism and not identical to it.
