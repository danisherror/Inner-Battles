The Bible shows the root in a person who judges himself by his own record and by what he has lost. In the parable of the prodigal son (Luke 15:17-24), the young man who has wasted his inheritance plans what to say: "Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you; I am no longer worthy to be called your son; treat me like one of your hired hands."

His own verdict is that he has forfeited his place. The father has not said so.

Matthew 7:3-5 shows a related habit, a standard held up to others and missed in oneself: "Why do you see the speck in your neighbor's eye but do not notice the log in your own eye?"

This is related to self-criticism and not identical to it. The parable is about repentance and the second passage about judging others, and reading them for the roots of the inner critic is ours.
