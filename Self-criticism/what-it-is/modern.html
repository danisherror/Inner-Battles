Self-criticism

A harsh inner voice that punishes mistakes instead of helping you improve.

What it is

Self-criticism is a harsh inner voice that judges and punishes you for mistakes and shortcomings, when it could be helping you improve. It is not the same as honest self-assessment: noticing an error and asking how to fix it is one thing, and attacking yourself for it is another.

Researchers often study its opposite. Zessin and colleagues describe self-compassion as "a positive and caring attitude of a person toward her- or himself in the face of failures and individual shortcomings". Neff's 2003 paper presents self-compassion as "a healthy attitude toward oneself", and Gilbert and Procter's 2006 pilot study of a group therapy approach was designed for "people with high shame and self-criticism".

Self-criticism is close to perfectionism and often comes with shame. It is not a diagnosis, but it can go with low mood and anxiety. If the voice is relentless, a doctor or therapist can help.
