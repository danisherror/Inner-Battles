Buddhist texts have no term for the inner critic, but SN 3.4 speaks of treating oneself as an enemy. King Pasenadi says: "Those who do bad things by way of body, speech, and mind don't love themselves... It's because they treat themselves like an enemy." And those who do good "treat themselves like a loved one". The Buddha agrees with him.

Here the point is about conduct, not self-talk. Dhp 159 turns to how we treat ourselves: "If one were to treat oneself as one instructs another, the well-tamed indeed would tame: for the self, it seems, is hard to tame."

This is related to self-criticism and not identical to it. Pasenadi means that harmful action is how a person treats themselves badly, and the texts do not describe a harsh inner voice. Reading Dhp 159 as speaking to it is ours.
