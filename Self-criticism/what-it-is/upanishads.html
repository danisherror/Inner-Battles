What it is

The Upanishads do not name an inner critic. They speak of grief, and of a self that is not touched by what the mind says about it.

The Mundaka Upanishad (3.1.2) describes a person who "sits grieving, immersed, bewildered by his own impotence", and says that when he sees the other, "then his grief passes away". Katha 1.2.22 says: "The wise who knows the Self as bodiless within the bodies, as unchanging among changing things, as great and omnipresent, does never grieve."

This is related to self-criticism and not identical to it. The passages are about grief and the nature of the Self, and not about harsh self-judgement. Reading them as speaking to the inner critic is ours.
