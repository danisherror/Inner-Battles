The Buddhist texts point to treating oneself as one would treat someone dear, and to not adding the second arrow.

1. **Know yourself as beloved.** Dhp 157: "If you knew your self as beloved, you'd look after it so well."
2. **Treat yourself as you instruct another.** Dhp 159: "If one were to treat oneself as one instructs another, the well-tamed indeed would tame: for the self, it seems, is hard to tame."
3. **Do not treat yourself as an enemy.** SN 3.4: those who treat themselves "like a loved one" are those who love themselves.
4. **Feel the first arrow only.** SN 36.6: the learned disciple feels the pain but they "don't sorrow or wail or lament". The practice is to notice the second arrow and put it down.

This is related to self-criticism and not identical to it. SN 3.4 and SN 36.6 are not about harsh self-talk, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for relentless self-attack.
