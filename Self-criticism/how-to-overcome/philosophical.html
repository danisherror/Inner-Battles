Self-criticism

A harsh inner voice that punishes mistakes instead of helping you improve.

How to overcome it

The philosophers advise moving from blame to understanding, and returning without disgust.

  1. Look to your principles. Epictetus, Enchiridion 5: "Men are disturbed, not by things, but by the principles and notions which they form concerning things." Ask which rule you are punishing yourself under.
  2. Move up the ladder of blame. In the same chapter the uninstructed blame others, the beginner blames himself, and the "perfectly instructed" blame "neither on others nor on himself".
  3. Return. Marcus Aurelius: "when thou hast failed, return back again, and be content if the greater part of what thou doest is consistent with man's nature, and love this to which thou returnest."
  4. Treat the practice as a remedy. "Do not return to philosophy as if she were a master", but as one who applies "a bit of sponge and egg" to sore eyes.

This is related to self-criticism and not identical to it. These are rules for living, and applying them to a harsh inner voice is a reading.
