The Buddhist texts name sorrow, fear and conflict as what comes from holding things dear.

- **Sorrow and fear.** Dhp 212: "Sorrow springs from what we hold dear, fear springs from what we hold dear; one free from holding anything dear has no sorrow, let alone fear."
- **Quarrels.** Snp 4.11: "Quarrels and disputes come from what we hold dear, as do lamentation and sorrow, stinginess, conceit and arrogance."
- **Enmity.** DN 21: beings bound by jealousy and stinginess "still have enmity, violence, hostility, and hate", though they wish to be free of them.

This is related to jealousy and not identical to it. The verses are about clinging in general, and do not describe a jealous person in particular. Reading them as the toll of jealousy is ours.
