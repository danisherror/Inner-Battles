Jealousy

Effects on life

The philosophers describe the disturbance jealousy causes in the mind and in the relationship.

  • Lament and disturbance. Epictetus, Enchiridion 1: if you take what belongs to others as your own, "You will lament, you will be disturbed, and you will find fault both with gods and men."
  • A divided mind. Spinoza calls jealousy "a wavering of the disposition arising from combined love and hatred".
  • Pain on both sides. Spinoza adds that "a jealous man is not greeted by his beloved with the same joyful countenance as before, and this also gives him pain as a lover". The jealous person's behaviour changes how they are met.

This is related to jealousy and not identical to it. Epictetus is speaking of disturbance in general, and Spinoza describes the emotion as he understood it. Neither is a study of what jealousy does to a particular person or relationship.
