Jealousy

The fear of losing someone or something to a rival, and the hurt and suspicion that follow.

Root causes

Epictetus puts the root in treating things that belong to others as our own. Enchiridion 1: "Remember, then, that if you suppose that things which are slavish by nature are also free, and that what belongs to others is your own, then you will be hindered."

And in 14: "If you wish your children, and your wife, and your friends to live for ever, you are stupid; for you wish to be in control of things which you cannot, you wish for things that belong to others to be your own."

Spinoza adds the other half. The lover will "endeavour, as far as he can, to imagine the loved object as most closely bound to him", and this endeavour is "checked by the image of the loved object in conjunction with the image of him whom the loved object has joined to itself" (Ethics III.35, proof).

This is related to jealousy and not identical to it. Epictetus is speaking of things outside our control in general, and Spinoza of the structure of the emotion. Reading Epictetus's point as the root of jealousy is ours.
