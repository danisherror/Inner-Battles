Jealousy

The fear of losing someone or something to a rival, and the hurt and suspicion that follow.

Root causes

DN 21 traces jealousy back through a chain. Sakka asks about the source of jealousy and stinginess, and the Buddha says: "The liked and the disliked, lord of gods, are the source of jealousy and stinginess."

Then, question by question: "Desire is the source of what is liked and disliked." "Thought is the source of desire." "Judgments driven by the proliferation of perceptions are the source of thoughts."

The Sutta Nipata (4.11) gives a similar line: "Quarrels and disputes come from what we hold dear". Asked where what we hold dear comes from, it answers: "What we hold dear in the world spring from desire".

This is related to jealousy and not identical to it. The suttas speak of jealousy, stinginess and quarrels together, and not of the fear of losing a person to a rival. Reading the chain as the roots of that fear is ours.
