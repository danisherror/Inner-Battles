> **Note:** This is an invented composite story, not a real case. It is built from patterns people commonly describe, so you can see how jealousy works and how people loosen its grip.

**Tomas's story**

Tomas's partner, Priya, moved to a new team. She came home talking about a colleague who had made her laugh. Tomas smiled, then spent the evening running the sentence over in his head.

Within a week he was reading her messages when she was in the shower. He asked about her lunches. When she stopped answering, he said she was hiding something. Priya, who had hidden nothing, began to stay quiet.

What helped:

1. **He named the fear.** A friend asked what he was afraid of. "That she will find someone better and I will not see it coming."
2. **He sorted what he knew from what he imagined.** He knew she had a new job and a colleague who was funny. The rest was a story.
3. **He said the feeling.** "I have felt insecure since you changed teams. I do not think you have done anything. I wanted to tell you instead of checking."
4. **He stopped checking.** The first fortnight was the hardest, and he went for a walk when the urge came.
5. **He noticed it was not lifting.** After weeks of poor sleep and a mind that would not rest, he spoke to his doctor and was referred to a counsellor.

The fear did not vanish. It got smaller, and it stopped giving the orders.

**What it shows:** The jealousy was about a fear of loss, not about anything Priya did. Naming it and saying it aloud did what checking could not.
