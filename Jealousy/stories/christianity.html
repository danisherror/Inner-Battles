A story

The elder son at the door

In Luke 15 the younger son has come home and his father has held a feast. The elder son was in the field: "as he came and approached the house, he heard music and dancing." A slave tells him: "Your brother has come, and your father has killed the fatted calf because he has got him back safe and sound" (15:25-27).

"Then he became angry and refused to go in. His father came out and began to plead with him" (15:28). The elder son answers with his record of service and a complaint: "when this son of yours came back, who has devoured your assets with prostitutes, you killed the fatted calf for him!" (15:30).

The father replies: "Son, you are always with me, and all that is mine is yours. But we had to celebrate and rejoice, because this brother of yours was dead and has come to life; he was lost and has been found" (15:31-32).

What it shows: The elder son stands outside his own father's house because he thinks his place has been taken. The parable is about welcome and repentance and not about jealousy as such; it shows the father going out to him and telling him that all the father has is his. The parable ends there, and the text does not say whether he went in.
