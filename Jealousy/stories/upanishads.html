A story

The bees and the honey

In the Chandogya Upanishad a father, Uddalaka, teaches his son Svetaketu in parables. The ninth section begins: "As the bees, my son, make honey by collecting the juices of distant trees, and reduce the juice into one form, And as these juices have no discrimination, so that they might say, I am the juice of this tree or that, in the same manner, my son, all these creatures, when they have become merged in the True (either in deep sleep or in death), know not that they are merged in the True."

The next section gives a second picture, the rivers: "as those rivers, when they are in the sea, do not know, I am this or that river". After each, the father says: "It is the Self, and thou, O Svetaketu, art it."

What it shows: The parable pictures separate things that are one in the end. It is not about jealousy, and the link is ours: jealousy depends on telling "mine" from "theirs", and the parable shows juices that cannot say whose tree they came from.
