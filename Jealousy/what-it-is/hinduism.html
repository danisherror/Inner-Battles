Jealousy

The fear of losing someone or something to a rival, and the hurt and suspicion that follow.

Hinduism lens

What it is

Hinduism has no single word in the Bhagavad Gita for jealousy as the fear of losing someone to a rival, but the Gita describes the person who is free of its roots. Gita 12.13-14: "Those devotees are very dear to Me who are free from malice toward all living beings, who are friendly, and compassionate. They are free from attachment to possessions and egotism, equipoised in happiness and distress, and ever-forgiving."

The translator's commentary on these verses says of such devotees: "If they harbor envy toward others, it is tantamount to harboring envy toward God Himself."

This is related to jealousy and not identical to it. The verses speak of malice, envy and attachment to possessions, and do not describe the fear of losing someone to a rival. Reading them as speaking to jealousy is ours.
