In the Buddhist texts jealousy is joined with stinginess, the clinging to what is one's own. In the Dhammapada (209) the Buddha describes a person who forgets their goal and "cling[s] to what you hold dear, jealous of those devoted to their heart's goal".

In the Sakka sutta (DN 21), the god Sakka asks what binds beings who wish to be at peace, and the Buddha answers: "the fetters of jealousy and stinginess bind the gods, humans, titans, dragons, centaurs—and any of the other diverse creatures—so that, though they wish to be free of enmity, violence, hostility, and hate, they still have enmity, violence, hostility, and hate."

This is related to jealousy and not identical to it. The translation renders the Pali as "jealousy", but the word is generally described as envy, and the fear of losing a loved one to a rival is not the sutta's topic.
