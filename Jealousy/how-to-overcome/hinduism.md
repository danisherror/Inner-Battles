The Gita points to a loosening of possessiveness, and to goodwill toward others, held together by a steady mind.

1. **Loosen the sense of "mine".** Gita 2.71: "That person, who gives up all material desires and lives free from a sense of greed, proprietorship, and egoism, attains perfect peace."
2. **Practise goodwill toward the rival too.** Gita 12.13-14 describes the devotees who are "free from malice toward all living beings, who are friendly, and compassionate", and "free from attachment to possessions and egotism".
3. **Stay level in loss and gain.** The same verses say "equipoised in happiness and distress".
4. **Make the mind a friend.** Gita 6.5: "Elevate yourself through the power of your mind, and not degrade yourself, for the mind can be the friend and also the enemy of the self."

This is related to jealousy and not identical to it. The verses are about detachment and goodwill in general, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for constant jealousy, or for jealousy that leads to controlling someone. Taking "proprietorship" to mean the sense of owning a person is ours.
