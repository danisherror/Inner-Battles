Jealousy

The fear of losing someone or something to a rival, and the hurt and suspicion that follow.

How to overcome it

The Buddhist texts point to loosening the clinging that jealousy grows from.

  1. See the chain. Dhp 213-216 trace sorrow and fear back through attachment, relishing, desire and craving. Dhp 215: "Sorrow springs from desire, fear springs from desire; one free from desire has no sorrow, let alone fear."
  2. Ask what a feeling grows. DN 21 says of happiness: "should you know of a happiness: 'When I cultivate this kind of happiness, unskillful qualities grow, and skillful qualities decline.' You should not cultivate that kind of happiness." The sutta says the same of sadness and equanimity. Applying the test to jealous thoughts is ours.
  3. Understand what the states depend on. Snp 4.11: "Knowing that these states are dependent, and knowing what they depend on, the inquiring sage, having understood, is freed, and enters no dispute."

This is related to jealousy and not identical to it. The passages are about desire and clinging in general, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist for constant jealousy, or for jealousy that leads to controlling someone. The Dhammapada's counsel is read here as about clinging, which is a reading and not a claim about loving less.
