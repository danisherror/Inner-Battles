Hopelessness and despair

Believing that nothing will change, and that nothing you do will matter.

Effects on life

The Gita shows what hopelessness does to action. Arjuna "cast aside his bow and arrows, and sank into the seat of his chariot" (1.47). The commentary says: "Arjun's reluctance to fight the war had now reached its climax. He had now surrendered to his grief and slumped into deep dejection."

The other side is the end of the teaching, in 18.73: "I am now free from doubts, and I shall act according to Your instructions."

This is related to hopelessness and not identical to it. The effect shown is stopping, and the recovery comes through a long teaching, not a quick fix. The text shows that dejection can end, and does not say how long it takes. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, please tell someone today and contact your local emergency services if you may act on it.
