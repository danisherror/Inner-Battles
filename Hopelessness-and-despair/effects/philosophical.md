Boethius describes what grief did to him: "Old? Ah yes; swift, ere I knew it, / By these sorrows on me pressed / Age hath come; lo, Grief hath bid me / Wear the garb that fits her best." And: "And the skin hangs loose and shrivelled / On this sorrow-shrunken frame."

Philosophy names a further effect: "thou also for awhile hast been parted from thy mind's tranquillity."

This is related to hopelessness and not identical to it. The song is a poet's description of grief, and the lines that follow it, which long for death, are not quoted. Reading the passage as an effect of hopelessness is ours. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist.
