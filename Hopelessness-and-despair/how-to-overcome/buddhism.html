Hopelessness and despair

Believing that nothing will change, and that nothing you do will matter.

How to overcome it

The Buddhist texts counsel good company, a path, and good done again and again.

  1. Seek good friends. Kisāgotamī's verses begin: "Associating with good friends, even a fool becomes astute."
  2. Take the path. SN 56.11 names "the noble truth of the practice that leads to the cessation of suffering", and says it "should be developed".
  3. Do good again and again. Dhp 118: "If you do something good, do it again and again, set your heart on it, for piling up goodness is joyful."

This is related to hopelessness and not identical to it. The first is from a nun's verses on grief, the second names the path without describing it here, and Dhp 118 is advice on conduct. Reading them as a way through hopelessness is ours, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, please tell someone today and contact your local emergency services if you may act on it.
