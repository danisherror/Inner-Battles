The research read here describes the problem and mostly tests no remedy. Maier and Seligman report, in animals, that passivity "can be overcome by learning control", and their abstract only speculates about "how to treat depression". The rest is common advice.

1. **Say what it is.** "I feel hopeless" is a report about now. "Nothing will ever change" is a prediction, and low mood makes poor predictions. That is common advice.
2. **Tell someone.** A friend, family member, doctor or therapist. Hopelessness tends to want silence, and saying it aloud often loosens it. That is common advice.
3. **Get treatment.** Doctors and therapists treat low mood and hopelessness, and persistent low mood may be depression. That is a standard statement, not tied to a study read here.
4. **Make one small thing.** A short walk, a message, a meal. Maier and Seligman's animal work is about control, and linking it to small acts is the site's own idea and is untested here.
5. **Look after the basics.** Sleep, food, daylight, company. That is common advice.
6. **Keep safe.** Hopelessness can be a sign of depression, and it is nothing to be ashamed of. If it lasts or keeps coming back, please talk to a doctor, therapist or someone you trust. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, tell someone today, and if you may act on those thoughts contact your local emergency services now. [The International Association for Suicide Prevention points to a directory of verified helplines in over 150 countries](https://www.iasp.info/crisis-centres-helplines/).
