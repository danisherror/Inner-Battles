What it is

Boethius wrote the Consolation of Philosophy in prison, and it opens with his complaint: "Now perforce in tears and sadness / Learn a mournful strain to raise." Then Philosophy appears and names what she sees: "thou art pining with regretful longing for thy former fortune."

She adds: "all sudden changes of circumstances bring inevitably a certain commotion of spirit. Thus it hath come to pass that thou also for awhile hast been parted from thy mind's tranquillity."

This is related to hopelessness and not identical to it. Boethius' grief follows a loss of fortune, and Philosophy treats it as a sickness of the mind that she can address. Later lines of his opening song long for death and are not quoted here; the pages of the work were read only in part. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, please tell someone today and contact your local emergency services if you may act on it.
