Hinduism has no single word in the Bhagavad Gita for hopelessness, but it shows the collapse. In Gita 1.47 Arjuna, "his mind in distress and overwhelmed with grief", "cast aside his bow and arrows, and sank into the seat of his chariot."

Krishna answers: "how has this delusion overcome you in this hour of peril?" (2.2) and "it does not befit you to yield to this unmanliness. Give up such petty weakness of heart and arise" (2.3).

This is related to hopelessness and not identical to it. Arjuna's despondency is about a battle and a duty, and Krishna's first words are a rebuke. They should not be read as "snap out of it" for someone whose low mood is an illness. The long teaching that follows was not read here. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, please tell someone today and contact your local emergency services if you may act on it.
