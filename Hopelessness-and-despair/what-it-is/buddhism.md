The Buddhist texts begin with suffering and hold out an end to it. In SN 56.11, the first teaching, the Buddha says: "Rebirth is suffering; old age is suffering; illness is suffering; death is suffering; being coupled with the disliked is suffering; separation from the liked is suffering; not getting what you wish for is suffering."

Then: "Now this is the noble truth of the cessation of suffering. It's the fading away and cessation of that very same craving no residue left behind; giving it away, letting it go, releasing it, and not clinging to it."

This is related to hopelessness and not identical to it. The sutta does not name hopelessness, and its subject is suffering in general. What it shows is the claim that suffering has an end, and the claim is the opposite of the belief that nothing can change. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, please tell someone today and contact your local emergency services if you may act on it.
