Hopelessness and despair

Believing that nothing will change, and that nothing you do will matter.

Upanishads lens

What it is

The Upanishads describe hope as a force, which shows by contrast what is missing in hopelessness. In Chandogya 7.14 Sanatkumara tells Narada: "Hope (āśā) is better than memory. Fired by hope does memory read the sacred hymns, perform sacrifices, desire sons and cattle, desire this world and the other."

He adds that for one who meditates on hope as Brahman, "all his desires are fulfilled by hope, his prayers are not in vain". Narada asks: "Sir, is there something better than hope?" and the answer is: "Yes, there is something better than hope."

This is related to hopelessness and not identical to it. The passage does not name despair, and reading hope's absence as despair is ours. It also ranks hope below something higher, which the next section names as breath. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, please tell someone today and contact your local emergency services if you may act on it.
