Inner battle

Hopelessness and despair

What it is, read through each lens side by side.

TopicWhat it isRoot causesEffects on lifeHow to overcome itA story▦ All sections
Lenses to show

Modern

Hopelessness is the belief that things will not get better and that nothing you do will change that. Despair is the deeper, heavier form. That is a standard definition and not taken from the papers below.

Researchers have studied it as part of depression. Abela's study of third- and seventh-grade children tests "the hopelessness theory of depression", which names cognitive styles as "vulnerability factors". Maier and Seligman describe the older idea of learned helplessness: "Seligman and Maier (1967) theorized that animals learned that outcomes were independent of their responses-that nothing they did mattered", and then report that "the original theory got it backward". That work is on animals given shocks, and the abstract only speculates about what it means for people.

Everyday signs, as people commonly describe them and not as findings from these papers: the future looks closed, effort feels pointless, and the thought "nothing will change" feels like a plain fact and not a thought. It overlaps with procrastination and fear and anxiety.

Hopelessness can be a sign of depression, and it is nothing to be ashamed of. If it lasts or keeps coming back, please talk to a doctor, therapist or someone you trust. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, tell someone today, and if you may act on those thoughts contact your local emergency services now. The International Association for Suicide Prevention points to a directory of verified helplines in over 150 countries (external link).

  • Maier and Seligman (2016), "Learned helplessness at fifty: Insights from neuroscience," Psychological Review 123(4), 349-367. doi:10.1037/rev0000033 (external link)

Hinduism

Hinduism has no single word in the Bhagavad Gita for hopelessness, but it shows the collapse. In Gita 1.47 Arjuna, "his mind in distress and overwhelmed with grief", "cast aside his bow and arrows, and sank into the seat of his chariot."

Krishna answers: "how has this delusion overcome you in this hour of peril?" (2.2) and "it does not befit you to yield to this unmanliness. Give up such petty weakness of heart and arise" (2.3).

This is related to hopelessness and not identical to it. Arjuna's despondency is about a battle and a duty, and Krishna's first words are a rebuke. They should not be read as "snap out of it" for someone whose low mood is an illness. The long teaching that follows was not read here. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. Upanishads

The Upanishads describe hope as a force, which shows by contrast what is missing in hopelessness. In Chandogya 7.14 Sanatkumara tells Narada: "Hope (āśā) is better than memory. Fired by hope does memory read the sacred hymns, perform sacrifices, desire sons and cattle, desire this world and the other."

He adds that for one who meditates on hope as Brahman, "all his desires are fulfilled by hope, his prayers are not in vain". Narada asks: "Sir, is there something better than hope?" and the answer is: "Yes, there is something better than hope."

This is related to hopelessness and not identical to it. The passage does not name despair, and reading hope's absence as despair is ours. It also ranks hope below something higher, which the next section names as breath. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, please tell someone today and contact your local emergency services if you may act on it.

Christianity

Christianity names despair openly. In Lamentations 3 the speaker says: "my soul is bereft of peace; I have forgotten what happiness is; so I say, 'Gone is my glory and all that I had hoped for from the Lord.'"

Paul's letter to the Corinthians says: "afflicted in every way but not crushed, perplexed but not driven to despair" (2 Corinthians 4:8).

The Psalms give the question: "How long, O Lord? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me? How long must I bear pain in my soul and have sorrow in my heart all day long?" (13:1-2).

This is related to hopelessness and not identical to it. These passages describe the state and do not offer a definition. Reading them together as an account of hopelessness is ours, and how the tradition treats despair as a doctrine was not checked. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, please tell someone today and contact your local emergency services if you may act on it.

Buddhism

The Buddhist texts begin with suffering and hold out an end to it. In SN 56.11, the first teaching, the Buddha says: "Rebirth is suffering; old age is suffering; illness is suffering; death is suffering; being coupled with the disliked is suffering; separation from the liked is suffering; not getting what you wish for is suffering."

Then: "Now this is the noble truth of the cessation of suffering. It's the fading away and cessation of that very same craving no residue left behind; giving it away, letting it go, releasing it, and not clinging to it."

This is related to hopelessness and not identical to it. The sutta does not name hopelessness, and its subject is suffering in general. What it shows is the claim that suffering has an end, and the claim is the opposite of the belief that nothing can change. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. Philosophical

Boethius wrote the Consolation of Philosophy in prison, and it opens with his complaint: "Now perforce in tears and sadness / Learn a mournful strain to raise." Then Philosophy appears and names what she sees: "thou art pining with regretful longing for thy former fortune."

She adds: "all sudden changes of circumstances bring inevitably a certain commotion of spirit. Thus it hath come to pass that thou also for awhile hast been parted from thy mind's tranquillity."

This is related to hopelessness and not identical to it. Boethius' grief follows a loss of fortune, and Philosophy treats it as a sickness of the mind that she can address. Later lines of his opening song long for death and are not quoted here; the pages of the work were read only in part. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, please tell someone today and contact your local emergency services if you may act on it.
