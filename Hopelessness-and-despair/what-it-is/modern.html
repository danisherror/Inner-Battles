Hopelessness and despair

Believing that nothing will change, and that nothing you do will matter.

What it is

Hopelessness is the belief that things will not get better and that nothing you do will change that. Despair is the deeper, heavier form. That is a standard definition and not taken from the papers below.

Researchers have studied it as part of depression. Abela's study of third- and seventh-grade children tests "the hopelessness theory of depression", which names cognitive styles as "vulnerability factors". Maier and Seligman describe the older idea of learned helplessness: "Seligman and Maier (1967) theorized that animals learned that outcomes were independent of their responses-that nothing they did mattered", and then report that "the original theory got it backward". That work is on animals given shocks, and the abstract only speculates about what it means for people.

Everyday signs, as people commonly describe them and not as findings from these papers: the future looks closed, effort feels pointless, and the thought "nothing will change" feels like a plain fact and not a thought. It overlaps with procrastination and fear and anxiety.

Hopelessness can be a sign of depression, and it is nothing to be ashamed of. If it lasts or keeps coming back, please talk to a doctor, therapist or someone you trust. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, tell someone today, and if you may act on those thoughts contact your local emergency services now. The International Association for Suicide Prevention points to a directory of verified helplines in over 150 countries (external link).
