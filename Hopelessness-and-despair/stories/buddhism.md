> **Note:** This story is about grief after the deaths of a husband and children, and may be hard to read.

**Kisāgotamī**

In Thig 10.1 the nun Kisāgotamī remembers: "I was on the road, near to giving birth, when I saw my husband dead. I gave birth there on the road before I'd reached my own house. My two children have died, and on the road my husband lies dead".

Her verses turn at the end: "I've developed the noble eightfold path leading to freedom from death. I've realized extinguishment, as seen in the mirror of the Dhamma. I've plucked out the dart, laid down the burden, and done what needed to be done."

The poem includes lines about women harming themselves, which are left out here, and the story of how she met the Buddha is told in later commentary and was not read.

**What it shows:** The poem moves from loss without measure to a burden laid down, in a nun's own words. It is a poem of grief and not a tale of hopelessness, and linking them is ours. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist. If you are thinking of ending your life or harming yourself, please tell someone today and contact your local emergency services if you may act on it.
