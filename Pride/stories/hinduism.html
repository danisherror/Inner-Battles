A story

Indra and Govardhan

The Bhagavata Purana (Book 10, chapters 24-27) tells of Indra, king of the gods and giver of rain. The cowherds of Vrindavan used to hold a yearly offering to him. Krishna, then a boy, asked why: the cows and the hill Govardhan fed them, he said, so honour should go to the work and the land that sustain them.

The villagers agreed and worshipped Govardhan instead. Indra, proud of his rank, was furious. He sent a devastating storm of rain and wind to flood Vrindavan.

Krishna lifted Govardhan hill on one finger and held it like an umbrella, and everyone sheltered beneath it for seven days. Indra's power was spent. Realising whom he had challenged, he came, bowed and begged forgiveness.

What it shows: Pride grows from forgetting that your position depends on something larger. Indra's anger was wounded status. His recovery began when he stopped defending it.
