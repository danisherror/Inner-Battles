A story

Socrates and the oracle

In Plato's Apology (20e-23b), Socrates, on trial, explains how he got his reputation. His friend Chaerephon asked the oracle at Delphi whether anyone was wiser than Socrates, and the oracle said no one was.

Socrates was puzzled. He thought himself not wise at all. So he set out to test the oracle by finding someone wiser. He went to a politician with a high reputation for wisdom, and found that the man thought himself wise and was not. Socrates tried to show him, and made an enemy. He reflected: "I am wiser than this man; neither of us knows anything fine and good, but he thinks he knows when he doesn't, whereas I, as I don't know, don't think I do either."

He then went to poets and craftsmen. The craftsmen did know their crafts, but because of this each thought himself wise about everything else too.

What it shows: The worst ignorance is the kind you don't know you have. Socrates' "wisdom" was a refusal to be proud of knowledge he lacked, and it cost him the goodwill of the powerful.
