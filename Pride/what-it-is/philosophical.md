Greek and later European philosophers treat pride as a failure of self-knowledge and of proportion.

**Aristotle.** In the *Nicomachean Ethics* (Book 4, chapters 3 and 7) the mean is *magnanimity*, claiming honour that you actually deserve. One extreme is the vain person (*chaunos*), who claims honour beyond their worth. The other is the small-souled person, who claims less than they deserve. Pride as vice is therefore a mismatch between self-image and reality.

**Socrates.** In Plato's *Apology* (21a-23b), Socrates tests people who think they are wise and finds that he is wiser only in knowing that he does not know.

**The Stoics.** Epictetus (*Enchiridion* 13) advises: "If you want to improve, be content to be thought foolish." Marcus Aurelius (*Meditations* 12.4) notes the oddity that we love ourselves more than others yet care more about their opinion than our own.

**Kant.** In the *Metaphysics of Morals* (Doctrine of Virtue), arrogance (*Hochmut*) is the demand that others think little of themselves in comparison with us. It breaks the duty of respect owed to every person.
