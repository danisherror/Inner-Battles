Pride

Valuing yourself above truth, and above other people.

Buddhism lens

What it is

The Buddhist word is mana, usually translated "conceit". It is the habit of measuring yourself against others, whether as better, equal or worse, and it is one of the traditional mental fetters that bind a person to suffering. It is subtler than boasting. Feeling inferior is also mana, because it still takes the comparison seriously.

The deepest layer is asmi-mana, the "I am" conceit. In the Khemaka Sutta (SN 22.89), monks who have already let go of the five lower fetters say they still have a residual sense of "I am". Khemaka compares it to the smell left in a cloth that has been washed but not yet scented: it fades only with deeper insight into the five aggregates.

The suttas also show crude pride. In the Sukhumala Sutta (AN 3.39), the Buddha describes how he once lived in luxury, and how the intoxication with youth, health and life drops away when you see they are not secure. The Dhammapada (verse 74) describes the fool who wants everyone to think "this was done by me."

Buddhism does not rank itself above other traditions here. It simply asks you to see that the "I" being defended is a changing process, not a fixed possession.
