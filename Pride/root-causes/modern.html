Pride

Valuing yourself above truth, and above other people.

Root causes

Pride is rarely confidence gone too far. More often it is insecurity wearing armour. Common roots:

  • Fragile self-worth. If your value feels like it depends on being impressive, every mistake is a threat, so you deny it or blame someone else.
  • Self-serving bias. The mind credits you for wins and blames circumstances for losses, which quietly inflates your self-image.
  • Comparison. Status is relative, so feeling "above" others becomes a cheap way to feel fine.
  • Praise and power. Success, flattery and a lack of honest feedback make people more certain they are right. Researchers describe a "hubris syndrome" in some leaders after long spells in power.
  • Upbringing. Being praised only for results, or for being "better" than others, teaches that worth must be earned by winning.
