Philosophers locate pride in **ignorance of oneself, love of honour, and mistaking luck for merit**.

- **Not knowing what you don't know.** In the *Apology* (21c-22e), Socrates finds that the politicians, poets and craftsmen he questions believe they are wise about everything because they are skilled in one thing.
- **Love of honour.** Aristotle (*Nicomachean Ethics* 4.3) says the vain person overrates himself and is easily taken in by the outward goods of wealth and power, which he takes for the real worth that deserves honour.
- **Opinion over reality.** Epictetus (*Enchiridion* 5) says people are troubled not by things but by their judgments of things, and our judgment of ourselves is no exception. Marcus Aurelius (*Meditations* 12.4) notes how much we value others' opinions despite loving ourselves best.
- **Self-conceit.** Kant, in the *Critique of Practical Reason*, calls the claim to be the standard of worth "self-conceit," and says the moral law "strikes down" that claim and so produces humility.
