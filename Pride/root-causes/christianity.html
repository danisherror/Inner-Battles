Deadly sin

Pride

Valuing yourself above truth, and above other people.

Christianity lens

Root causes

Christian writers see pride's roots in a distorted love of self and a forgetting of dependence on God.

  • The first fall. Isaiah 14:12-15 (the boast "I will make myself like the Most High") has long been read as the pattern of pride, and the Genesis temptation "you will be like God" (Genesis 3:5) repeats it for humans.
  • Self-love turned inward. Augustine, in City of God XIV.13, says pride is a perverse desire for exaltation: the soul abandons the one it should be rooted in and makes itself its own centre.
  • Success and comfort. Deuteronomy 8:11-17 warns that once people are fed and prosperous their heart will be lifted up and they will say "My power and the might of my own hand have gotten me this wealth."
  • Comparing and being praised. Luke 18:11 shows the Pharisee building his self-image by comparison with the tax collector. Gregory the Great also lists vainglory, a hunger for praise, as pride's child.
