Pride

How to overcome it

Philosophy offers training in accuracy about yourself.

  • Examine yourself. Socrates (Apology 38a) said the unexamined life is not worth living for a human being. Ask honestly what you know and what you only assume.
  • Aim for the mean. Aristotle (Nicomachean Ethics 4.3) wants you to claim what you actually deserve, no more and no less, and builds that habit through practice.
  • Stoic exercises. Epictetus (Enchiridion 13) says to accept being thought foolish. Marcus Aurelius opens the Meditations (Book 1) by thanking each person he learned from, a daily practice of credit-sharing. Seneca recommends an evening review of the day's faults, a habit he credits to Sextius.
  • Look from above. Marcus (Meditations 9.30, 12.24) pictures the earth as a point and fame as short-lived, which shrinks the importance of rank.
  • Respect persons. Kant's duty of respect asks you to treat every person as having dignity equal to yours.
