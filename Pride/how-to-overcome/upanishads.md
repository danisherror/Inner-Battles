The Upanishadic path is to question the "I" and to learn from a teacher with an open mind.

- **Approach a teacher humbly.** Mundaka 1.2.12 says to seek a guru who is learned in the scriptures and established in Brahman, coming "with fuel in hand", a sign of readiness to serve.
- **Drop the idea that you already know.** Kena 2.3 gives the posture: knowing that you do not know it is the beginning of knowing it.
- **Ask "who am I?"** The Self is not the body, mind or intellect. Katha 1.2.23 says it is gained by the one the Self chooses, which is why grasping does not work.
- **See the same Self in all.** Isha 6 says the one who sees all beings in the Self does not shrink from anyone. Seeing equality weakens the habit of ranking.
- **Study, reflect, meditate.** This is *shravana, manana, nididhyasana*, the sequence taught in later Vedanta, built on passages such as Brihadaranyaka 2.4.5.
