Pride

Valuing yourself above truth, and above other people.

How to overcome it

Buddhism treats conceit as something to be seen through, not fought with force.

  • Contemplate not-self. Looking closely at body, feeling, perception, habits and awareness (SN 22.59) shows there is nothing fixed to be proud of or ashamed of.
  • Notice comparing. When you catch the mind ranking itself as better, equal or worse, label it "measuring" and let it go without acting on it.
  • Remember impermanence. AN 3.39 teaches reflecting that youth, health and life do not last, which takes the air out of intoxication with them.
  • Practise respect and gratitude. Showing reverence to teachers and elders, and to those who have helped you, is a standard practice. The Mangala Sutta (Sn 2.4) lists respect, contentment and gratitude among the highest blessings.
  • Share merit. Dedicating good deeds to others weakens the claim "I did this."
  • Cultivate metta (loving-kindness), which makes it harder to look down on anyone.
