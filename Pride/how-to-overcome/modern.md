The aim is not to think badly of yourself. It is to see yourself accurately and keep your worth from depending on being right or better.

1. **Separate worth from performance.** You can value yourself and still be wrong. Say it out loud: "I was wrong about that."
2. **Ask for honest feedback**, from someone who is not afraid of you, and respond with "thank you" before you defend.
3. **Practise intellectual humility.** Add "I might be wrong" or "what would change my mind?" to your disagreements.
4. **Share credit, own blame.** In a team, name who else contributed. After a failure, say what you would do differently.
5. **Prefer authentic pride.** Take pride in effort and growth ("I improved") instead of rank ("I'm better than them").
6. **Be around people better than you** at something, and learn from them.
7. **Practise gratitude.** Listing what luck, others and timing gave you weakens the story that you did it all.

**Sources**

- Leary et al. (2017), *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* 43(6). [doi:10.1177/0146167217697695](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167217697695)
- Tracy & Robins (2007), JPSP 92(3). [doi:10.1037/0022-3514.92.3.506](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.92.3.506)
