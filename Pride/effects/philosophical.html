Pride

Valuing yourself above truth, and above other people.

Philosophical lens

Effects on life

Philosophers describe pride's costs as lost friendship, lost wisdom and lost freedom.

  • Ridicule and isolation. Aristotle (Nicomachean Ethics 4.3) says vain people make themselves look foolish by dressing up and boasting, and so are laughed at.
  • Poor judgment. Socrates in the Apology argues that thinking you know what you don't is the worst ignorance because it prevents inquiry (29a-b).
  • Slavery to opinion. The Stoics held that someone who needs praise gives control of their peace to others. Epictetus (Enchiridion 13, 33) advises not telling everyone about your achievements and being content to be thought foolish.
  • Disrespect for persons. For Kant, arrogance treats others as of little worth, which violates the respect owed to them as ends in themselves.
