How to overcome it

The research read here describes shame and guilt and tests no remedy. The rest is common advice.

  1. Say which it is. Try the sentence "I did something I regret" before "I am bad". The first points at what can be repaired. That is common advice.
  2. Repair what you can. If you hurt someone, say plainly what you did and apologise without excuses, where it is safe to. That is common advice.
  3. Tell someone you trust. Shame tends to want hiding, and saying it aloud to a kind person often loosens it. That is common advice.
  4. Speak to yourself as to a friend. A harsh verdict on yourself repairs nothing; see self-criticism. That is common advice.
  5. Let guilt do its job, then put it down. Once you have understood and repaired what you can, the feeling has done its work. That is common advice.
  6. Get help. If shame is heavy or long-lasting, is tied to abuse, or comes with thoughts of harming yourself, please talk to a doctor or therapist, and in an emergency contact your local emergency services.
