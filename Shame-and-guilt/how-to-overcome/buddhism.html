Shame and guilt

Feeling bad about what you did, or about who you are, and what to do with it.

How to overcome it

The Buddhist texts counsel recognising a mistake, and turning to good.

  1. Recognise it. AN 2.21: "There are two who are astute. What two? One who recognizes when they've made a mistake. And one who properly accepts the confession of someone who's made a mistake."
  2. Turn to heedfulness. Dhp 173: "Someone whose bad deed is supplanted by the good, shines upon this world, like the moon freed from clouds."
  3. Build conduct that leaves no regret. AN 10.1: "having no regrets is the goal and benefit of skillful ethics."

This is related to shame and guilt and not identical to them. AN 2.21 is about the wise and the foolish and says nothing of making amends, and Dhp 173 is about good done after a bad deed and does not describe a feeling. Reading them as a way through shame and guilt is ours, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist.
