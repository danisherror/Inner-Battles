The philosophers' counsel is to look at your judgments, and to be accurate about blame.

1. **Check the judgment.** Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 5: "Men are disturbed, not by things, but by the principles and notions which they form concerning things."
2. **Be accurate about blame.** Same section: "An uninstructed person will lay the fault of his own bad condition upon others. Someone just starting instruction will lay the fault on himself. Some who is perfectly instructed will place blame neither on others nor on himself."
3. **Aim at not needing the feeling.** Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* 4.9: "the good man will never voluntarily do bad actions."

This is related to shame and guilt and not identical to them. Epictetus is writing about being disturbed in general, and Aristotle is saying shame is not a virtue, so neither gives a method for the feeling. Step 3 is a standard that does not help once something has gone wrong, and it should be read beside [Self-criticism](../../Self-criticism/what-it-is/modern.html). Reading these as a way through shame and guilt is ours, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist.
