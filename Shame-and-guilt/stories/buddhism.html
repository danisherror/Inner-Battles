A story

Aṅgulimāla

In MN 86 Aṅgulimāla, a former bandit who has become a monk, says of himself: "I have intentionally killed many living creatures." He is sent to a woman in a difficult labour with words that begin "Ever since I was born in the noble birth" and end: "By this truth, may both you and your baby be safe." She and the baby are safe.

Later someone throws a stone, a stick and gravel at him. he goes to the Buddha "with cracked head, bleeding, his bowl broken, and his outer robe torn". The Buddha says: "Endure it, brahmin! Endure it, brahmin! You're experiencing in this very life the result of deeds that might have caused you to be tormented in hell for many years".

He then speaks verses: "I used to be a bandit, the notorious Aṅgulimāla." "My name is 'Harmless', though I used to be harmful." And: "Someone whose bad deed is supplanted by the good, shines upon this world, like the moon freed from clouds."

What it shows: Aṅgulimāla does not deny what he did, and does not stay defined by it. The sutta is about a murderer who becomes a monk and is not a tale of shame as such; the link is ours. It involves killing and harm to a body, and may be hard to read. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and shame that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.
