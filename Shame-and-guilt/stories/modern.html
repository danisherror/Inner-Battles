A story

Note: This is an invented composite story, not a real case. It is built from patterns people commonly describe, so you can see how shame and guilt work and how people ease them.

Rohan's story

At a dinner, Rohan made a joke about his friend Leela's failed business, and the table laughed. Leela went quiet. For the next week he rehearsed it each night and did not answer her messages. The sentence he kept telling himself was "I am a terrible friend".

What helped:

  1. He split the sentence. "I said something cruel" was about what he did, and could be dealt with. "I am terrible" was a verdict, and it kept him hiding.
  2. He repaired what he could. He rang Leela, said what he had said and that it was unkind, and did not ask her to say it was fine.
  3. He told someone. His brother listened and did not rush to excuse him.
  4. He spoke to himself as to a friend. He asked what he would say to a friend who had done the same.
  5. He let it do its work. The guilt had pointed him to Leela. Once he had called, he let it go.
  6. He asked for more help. The "terrible person" thought stayed for weeks, and he spoke to a counsellor.

Leela took time to answer, and the friendship did not snap back at once.

What it shows: Rohan did not need to feel nothing. Separating the deed from the verdict let him act, and the repair did more than the hiding had.
