The Buddhist texts treat a sense of wrongdoing as a protection. AN 2.9: "These two bright things, mendicants, protect the world. What two? Conscience and prudence." Without them, "the world would become dissolute, like goats and sheep, chickens and pigs, and hounds and jackals."

The sutta says these two qualities are bright, and that without them there would be no recognition of the status of mother, aunts, or wives and partners of tutors and respected people.

This is related to shame and guilt and not identical to them. Bhikkhu Sujato renders the pair as "conscience and prudence", and the root text reads "Hirī ca ottappañca". They are commonly explained as a sense of moral shame and a wariness of doing wrong, but that explanation was not checked here. Nothing in the passage read speaks of shame as a verdict on oneself.
