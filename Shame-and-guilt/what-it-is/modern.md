Guilt is the painful sense of having done something wrong. Shame is the painful sense of being wrong, of being bad or small as a person. A common way to put it is that guilt says "I did something bad" and shame says "I am bad". That is a standard summary and not a quotation from the papers below.

Researchers treat them as two "moral emotions". Tangney, Stuewig and Mashek's review covers "the distinction between shame and guilt, and the relative advantages and disadvantages of these two moral emotions." In two studies of undergraduates, Tangney, Wagner and Gramzow found that "Shame-proneness was strongly related to psychological maladjustment in general", while "Guilt-proneness was only moderately related to psychopathology." Those are correlations from questionnaires and do not show that shame causes distress.

Everyday signs, as people commonly describe them and not as findings from these papers: wanting to hide or disappear, replaying a moment, going quiet around the person involved. Guilt often points toward repair, and it can be a fitting response to real harm. It overlaps with [self-criticism](../../Self-criticism/what-it-is/modern.html), where the harsh voice turns a deed into a verdict on the person.

Neither is a diagnosis. If shame is heavy or long-lasting, is tied to abuse, or comes with thoughts of harming yourself, please talk to a doctor or therapist, and in an emergency contact your local emergency services.

**Sources**

- Tangney, Stuewig and Mashek (2007), "Moral emotions and moral behavior," *Annual Review of Psychology* 58, 345-372. [doi:10.1146/annurev.psych.56.091103.070145](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.56.091103.070145)
- Tangney, Wagner and Gramzow (1992), "Proneness to shame, proneness to guilt, and psychopathology," *Journal of Abnormal Psychology* 101(3), 469-478. [doi:10.1037/0021-843X.101.3.469](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-843X.101.3.469)

Both were read in the abstracts (Europe PMC, 2026-11-15), not in full, and publication details were checked in Crossref. The "I did / I am" summary and the list of everyday signs are a standard summary and not drawn from a source; the abstract of the review says it covers the distinction but does not state it in those words.
