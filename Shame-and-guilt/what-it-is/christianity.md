Christianity has words for both. Guilt is the awareness of a wrong done, and the Bible also shows the urge to hide. In Genesis 3 the man and woman "knew that they were naked", and "hid themselves from the presence of the Lord God among the trees of the garden." Asked where he is, the man says: "I heard the sound of you in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked, and I hid myself."

Psalm 51:3 shows the other side: "For I know my transgressions, and my sin is ever before me." In verse 4 the speaker says: "Against you, you alone, have I sinned and done what is evil in your sight."

This is related to shame and guilt and not identical to it. Reading Genesis 3:7-10 as an account of shame, and Psalm 51 as guilt, is ours. The verses read do not use the words "shame" or "guilt" of the man and woman, and the Psalm is about sin before God and not a general feeling. What both show is a person who knows something is wrong and is aware of being seen. Nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist, and shame that is heavy or lasting is worth telling someone about.
