# Inner Battles: Topics Where the Fight Is Inside Us

The seven deadly sins (Pride, Greed, Lust, Envy, Gluttony, Wrath, Sloth) are only one list. Many traditions and modern psychology describe other struggles where the opponent is our own mind. This file collects them with basic info.

---

## 1. Other Religious / Spiritual Lists

### Shadripu: the six inner enemies (Hinduism)
| # | Name | Meaning |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | Kama | Craving, desire, lust |
| 2 | Krodha | Anger |
| 3 | Lobha | Greed |
| 4 | Moha | Delusion, attachment, confusion |
| 5 | Mada | Pride, arrogance |
| 6 | Matsarya | Jealousy, envy |

Taught as the six "enemies" that live within and cloud clear judgment. The Bhagavad Gita calls desire, anger and greed the "three gates to hell".

### The Five Hindrances / Kleshas (Buddhism)
1. **Sensual desire**: chasing pleasure.
2. **Ill will**: hatred, aversion.
3. **Sloth and torpor**: heaviness, dullness.
4. **Restlessness and worry**: an agitated mind.
5. **Doubt**: paralysing uncertainty.

Beyond these, the **Three Poisons** are root causes: **greed (raga), hatred (dvesha), delusion (moha)**. Other kleshas include conceit and wrong views.

### Kleshas (Yoga Sutras of Patanjali)
Five afflictions that cause suffering: **avidya** (ignorance), **asmita** (ego), **raga** (attachment), **dvesha** (aversion), **abhinivesha** (fear of death or loss).

### Eight Thoughts / Evagrian vices (Christian desert tradition)
The original list the seven sins came from: gluttony, lust, avarice, sadness, anger, **acedia** (spiritual weariness), vainglory, pride. Note **acedia** (a restless "I can't be bothered with anything") and **sadness** (despair).

### Nafs (Islam / Sufism)
The struggle against the lower self, called **jihad al-nafs**. Stages run from the commanding self (*nafs al-ammara*), to the self-blaming (*lawwama*), to the peaceful self (*mutma'inna*).

### Yetzer hara (Judaism)
The "evil inclination" versus the good inclination (*yetzer tov*). The idea is that the drive itself is not evil, but it needs to be directed.

## 2. Philosophical Struggles

- **Akrasia (weakness of will)**: knowing what is best and doing the opposite anyway (Aristotle, Socrates).
- **Stoic passions**: fear, craving, distress and pleasure-seeking that distort judgment. The Stoic task is to separate what we control from what we don't.
- **Ego versus true self**: Eastern and Western thought both describe a false, defended self against a deeper one.
- **Duty versus desire**: Kant's moral law against inclination.
- **Freedom versus fear**: existentialists describe the anxiety of choice and "bad faith" (self-deception).

## 3. Modern Psychological Battles

| Battle | Basic idea |
|--------|-----------|
| **Procrastination** | Delaying what matters because the present mood wins over the future self. |
| **Self-doubt / Impostor syndrome** | Feeling like a fraud despite evidence of competence. |
| **Perfectionism** | Standards so high that starting or finishing feels impossible. |
| **Fear and anxiety** | The mind predicting threats that haven't happened. |
| **Overthinking / rumination** | Looping on the past or future, unable to act. |
| **Self-criticism / inner critic** | A harsh internal voice that punishes instead of guiding. |
| **Shame and guilt** | Shame says "I am bad"; guilt says "I did something bad". Shame is the more corrosive. |
| **Addiction and compulsion** | Phone, porn, substances, gambling: the pull against the decision to stop. |
| **Comparison** | Measuring your inside against others' outsides (close to envy). |
| **Resentment and grudges** | Holding on to old hurt, so the past keeps paying rent in your head. |
| **Jealousy** | Fear of losing someone or something to another (differs from envy). |
| **Ego and need for validation** | Needing approval and praise to feel worthy. |
| **Loneliness and isolation** | The urge to withdraw even while wanting connection. |
| **Fear of failure / fear of success** | Self-sabotage to avoid risk or change. |
| **Cognitive dissonance** | Discomfort from holding conflicting beliefs, leading to rationalising. |
| **Denial and self-deception** | Not letting yourself see the truth. |
| **Burnout and apathy** | Exhaustion that flattens motivation (modern cousin of sloth/acedia). |
| **Impulsivity vs delayed gratification** | The "marshmallow" conflict: now versus later. |
| **Hopelessness / despair** | Believing nothing will change. (Persistent low mood may be depression; seek professional help.) |
| **Distraction / attention fragmentation** | Inability to hold focus against constant stimulation. |

## 5. Common Threads

- The opponent is usually a **thought, urge, or story**, not an outside person.
- Nearly every tradition says the first step is **awareness**: noticing the urge before acting on it.
- Common tools: meditation or prayer, journaling, reframing thoughts, habits and environment design, accountability, and compassion toward yourself.
- Most of these overlap. Anger feeds shame, envy feeds comparison, sloth feeds procrastination.
