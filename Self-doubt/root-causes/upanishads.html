Self-doubt

Doubting your ability or worth, and feeling like a fraud despite what you have done.

Upanishads lens

Root causes

The Upanishads do not describe where self-doubt comes from. Two passages suggest a root: measuring ourselves by learning, and forgetting what the Self is.

  • Measuring by learning. Mundaka 3.2.3: "That Self cannot be gained by the Veda, nor by understanding, nor by much learning." If we treat knowledge or cleverness as the measure of a person, we will always find ourselves short.
  • Lacking strength and earnestness. 3.2.4 names "destitute of strength, or without earnestness, or without right meditation" as the causes of failure to reach the Self. Doubt is not named, but a person who gives up through feeling unfit lacks strength in this sense. That is a reading.
  • Standing on achievement. In the Chandogya (6.1.2), Svetaketu comes home "conceited, considering himself well-read", and his father shows him what he lacks. Standing built on attainment is exposed when someone asks a question we cannot answer.

This is related to self-doubt and not identical to it. These passages are read for the roots they point to and are not about impostor feelings.
