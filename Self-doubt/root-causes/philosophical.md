The philosophers find the root in a mistaken judgement of one's own worth, and in resting that worth on things outside us.

- **A false estimate.** Aristotle's "unduly humble" person thinks himself "worthy of less than he is really worthy of" (*Nicomachean Ethics* 4.3). The fault is a wrong measurement.
- **Worth in what is not ours.** Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 1: "Things not in our control are body, property, reputation, command, and, in one word, whatever are not our own actions." If our sense of worth rests on reputation or on others' approval, any slight shakes it.
- **Reluctance at the start.** Marcus Aurelius notes the pull of the bed in the morning ("when thou risest unwillingly", *Meditations* 5). He treats it as a reluctance to be answered, and not as a verdict on himself.

This is related to self-doubt and not identical to it. These are accounts of character and judgement; the link to impostor feelings is ours.
