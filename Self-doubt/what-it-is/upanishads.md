The Upanishads do not discuss self-doubt, but the Katha Upanishad begins with a boy who is unsure of what lies ahead and goes on anyway. Nachiketas, "still a boy", asks his father three times, "to whom wilt thou give me?" and is told "I shall give thee unto Death" (Katha 1.1.2-4).

The Mundaka Upanishad speaks to those who feel unequal to the search: "That Self cannot be gained by the Veda, nor by understanding, nor by much learning. He whom the Self chooses, by him the Self can be gained" (3.2.3). And: "Nor is that Self to be gained by one who is destitute of strength, or without earnestness, or without right meditation" (3.2.4).

This is related to self-doubt and not identical to it. The texts speak of strength, earnestness and the nature of the Self, and not of feeling like a fraud. Taking the first verse as a comfort to the doubtful (that learning is not the measure) is a reading and not stated in the text.
