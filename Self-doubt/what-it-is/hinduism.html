Self-doubt

Doubting your ability or worth, and feeling like a fraud despite what you have done.

What it is

Hinduism has no single word for self-doubt, but the Bhagavad Gita opens with a man in its grip. Arjuna says in 2.7: "I am confused about my duty, and am besieged with anxiety and faintheartedness. I am Your disciple, and am surrendered to You. Please instruct me for certain what is best for me."

Krishna's first answer in 2.3 is: "O Parth, it does not befit you to yield to this unmanliness. Give up such petty weakness of heart and arise, O vanquisher of enemies."

Later the Gita speaks of the mind as both ally and obstacle. 6.5: "Elevate yourself through the power of your mind, and not degrade yourself, for the mind can be the friend and also the enemy of the self."

This is related to self-doubt and not identical to it. Arjuna's crisis is about duty and its cost, and the verses do not speak of competence or feeling like a fraud. The wording "unmanliness" in 2.3 is the translator's.
