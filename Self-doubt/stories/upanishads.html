A story

Nachiketas and the third boon

The Katha Upanishad begins with a boy. His father has promised to give away all he owns, and "faith entered into the heart of Nachiketas, who was still a boy" (1.1.2). He asks, "Dear father, to whom wilt thou give me?" three times, and the angry father says, "I shall give thee unto Death" (1.1.4).

Nachiketas goes to the house of Death, waits, and is granted three boons. For the third he asks what happens after death: "There is that doubt, when a man is dead, - some saying, he is; others, he is not. This I should like to know, taught by thee" (1.1.20).

Death tries to put him off: "On this point even the gods have doubted formerly; it is not easy to understand. ... do not press me" (1.1.21). He offers sons, cattle, gold and long life. Nachiketas answers: "These things last till tomorrow, O Death, for they wear out this vigour of all the senses" (1.1.26).

What it shows: A boy with no standing, facing the one who has the power to refuse him, keeps to his question. The story is about the seeker's resolve, and not about self-doubt as such; it shows steadiness against being told that the question is too hard.
