**Marcus Aurelius gets out of bed**

In the *Meditations*, the emperor Marcus Aurelius writes notes to himself. In one of them he describes a morning when he does not want to rise.

"In the morning when thou risest unwillingly, let this thought be present: I am rising to the work of a human being. Why then am I dissatisfied if I am going to do the things for which I exist and for which I was brought into the world? Or have I been made for this, to lie in the bed-clothes and keep myself warm?"

An objector answers: "But this is more pleasant." He replies: "Dost thou exist then to take thy pleasure, and not at all for action or exertion?" He goes on to point to the little plants, the little birds, the ants, the spiders and the bees, each doing its own work.

**What it shows:** Marcus does not wait for the feeling to pass. He reminds himself what he is for and gets up. The passage is about reluctance, and not about self-doubt as such; it shows acting before the mood is right.
