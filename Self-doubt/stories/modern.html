A story

Note: This is an invented composite story, not a real case. It is built from patterns people commonly describe, so you can see how self-doubt works and how people loosen its grip.

Rohan's story

Rohan was promoted to team lead after two years as an engineer. His manager said he had earned it. Rohan thought they had made a mistake.

He stayed late to double-check everything, avoided asking questions in case they showed what he did not know, and put off giving feedback to a colleague because he felt he had no right to. When the team praised the release, he told himself that the others had done the hard parts.

What helped:

  1. He named it. A colleague mentioned "impostor feelings" in passing, and Rohan recognised the pattern.
  2. He kept a list. Each Friday he wrote down three things he had done that week and one piece of feedback he had received. After a month the list was hard to argue with.
  3. He asked a friend. The friend, a manager at another company, said she had felt the same for years.
  4. He asked his questions. The first two turned out to be questions others had been afraid to ask.
  5. He noticed it was not lifting. After a few months of poor sleep and dread on Sunday evenings, he spoke to his doctor.

The doubt did not disappear. It got quieter, and he stopped letting it make decisions.

What it shows: Rohan did not argue himself out of the feeling. He put evidence beside it, spoke about it and acted before he felt ready.
