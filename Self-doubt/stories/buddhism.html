A story

The chapter on the self

The Dhammapada is a collection of verses and has no single story of self-doubt. The twelfth chapter, on the self, comes closest to speaking to it.

It begins: "If you knew your self as beloved, you'd look after it so well" (Dhp 157). And then: "One is indeed the lord of oneself, for who else would be one's lord? By means of a well-tamed self, one gains a lord that's rare indeed" (Dhp 160).

Traditional commentaries attach narratives to these verses. They were not read for this article, so none is retold here.

What it shows: The verses do not say that doubt is wrong. They say that the person is the one who has to look after and govern their own mind, and that doing it well is rare and worth doing. This is not about self-doubt directly, and the reading is ours.
