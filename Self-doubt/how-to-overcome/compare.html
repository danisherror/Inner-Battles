Inner battle

Self-doubt

How to overcome it, read through each lens side by side.

TopicWhat it isRoot causesEffects on lifeHow to overcome itA story▦ All sections
Lenses to show

Modern

The review of impostor syndrome found no published studies of treatment, so what follows is common advice and not evidence of what works.

  1. Name it. "This is the impostor feeling" separates the feeling from the facts.
  2. Keep a record of what you did. Write down tasks completed, feedback received and problems solved, and read it when the doubt is loud. Doubt that does not respond to evidence needs evidence in front of it.
  3. Check the story against the facts. Ask what you would say to a friend with the same record.
  4. Talk to someone. A mentor, colleague or friend often says they feel the same, which matters because the review found impostor feelings common across ages and groups.
  5. Learn the skill you doubt. Self-doubt about a real gap is useful information; fill the gap.
  6. Act before you feel ready. Confidence often follows action. This is common advice, not a finding here.
  7. Get help if it takes over. The review reports that impostor syndrome often goes with depression and anxiety. If doubt is constant or your mood is low, a doctor or therapist can help.

Sources

  • Bravata et al. (2020), Journal of General Internal Medicine 35(4), 1252-1275. doi:10.1007/s11606-019-05364-1 (external link)

Only the points about the lack of treatment studies, common prevalence and the link with depression and anxiety rest on the abstract read (Europe PMC, 2026-11-14). Steps 1 to 3, 5 and 6 are common advice and not drawn from a study read here, and no claim is made about how well they work.

Hinduism

The Gita's answer to Arjuna runs from a firm word to a steadying view of the mind.

  1. Rise. 2.3: "Give up such petty weakness of heart and arise." The translator's commentary says the path requires "high spirits and morale".
  2. Ask for guidance. Arjuna's own step in 2.7: "I am Your disciple... Please instruct me for certain what is best for me." Admitting the confusion is where the teaching begins.
  3. Make the mind a friend. 6.5: "Elevate yourself through the power of your mind, and not degrade yourself, for the mind can be the friend and also the enemy of the self."
  4. Train it. 6.6: "For those who have conquered the mind, it is their friend."

This is related to self-doubt and not identical to it. A firm word like 2.3 suits some people and not others, and nothing here replaces help from a doctor or therapist where doubt is severe.

Sources

Quotations (Swami Mukundananda's translation) were read on those pages on 2026-11-14. The "high spirits and morale" phrase is from the commentary on 2.3, and not the verse.

Upanishads

The Upanishads point to strength, earnestness and steadiness.

  1. Do not rest worth on learning. Mundaka 3.2.3: "That Self cannot be gained by the Veda, nor by understanding, nor by much learning." Not knowing enough is not the measure.
  2. Bring strength and earnestness. 3.2.4: the Self is not gained by one "destitute of strength, or without earnestness, or without right meditation. But if a wise man strives after it by those means... then his Self enters the home of Brahman."
  3. Stay with the question. Nachiketas keeps asking when offered wealth and long life instead: "These things last till tomorrow, O Death" (Katha 1.1.26).

This is related to self-doubt and not identical to it. The Upanishads give no method for doubt of one's competence, and these passages are read for the steadiness they show.

Sources

  • Mundaka Upanishad (external link) 3.2.3-4 (Max Müller's translation)
  • Katha Upanishad (external link) 1.1.26 (Müller)

Quotations were read in Müller's translation on Wikisource on 2026-11-14. The ellipsis in step 2 marks the words "(by strength, earnestness, and right meditation)," which Müller adds in brackets.

Christianity

The Bible's answer to the doubters is presence and a different measure.

  1. Remember you are not alone. To Moses and Gideon: "I will be with you" (Exodus 3:12; Judges 6:16). To Jeremiah: "Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you" (1:8).
  2. Change the measure. 2 Corinthians 3:5: "our qualification is from God".
  3. Say what you are. 1 Corinthians 15:10: "by the grace of God I am what I am".
  4. Ask for strength. Philippians 4:13: "I can do all things through him who strengthens me."
  5. Take it step by step. In Exodus 4:12 the instruction is only "now go, and I will be with your mouth and teach you what you are to speak".

This is related to self-doubt and not identical to it. These verses address calling and faith, and pastoral and medical help is also part of Christian care for constant doubt.

Buddhism

The Dhammapada's chapter on the self gives the Buddhist direction.

  1. Treat yourself as someone dear. Dhp 157: "If you knew your self as beloved, you'd look after it so well."
  2. Be your own lord. Dhp 160: "One is indeed the lord of oneself, for who else would be one's lord? By means of a well-tamed self, one gains a lord that's rare indeed."
  3. Do not feed the doubt. SN 46.51 says that doubt arises, grows and is starved by what is given to it. The sutta says there are "things that are grounds for doubt" and, separately, what "starves" its growth. Seeing which is which is the practice, though what the sutta lists was not read here.

This is related to self-doubt and not identical to it. The suttas are about the path, and nothing here replaces a doctor or therapist where doubt is severe.

Philosophical

The philosophers advise measuring accurately and then acting.

  1. Measure yourself correctly. Aristotle's fault is "worthy of less than he is really worthy of" (Nicomachean Ethics 4.3). The remedy is an honest estimate, neither above nor below the facts.
  2. Act for what you are for. Marcus Aurelius: "I am rising to the work of a human being. Why then am I dissatisfied if I am going to do the things for which I exist and for which I was brought into the world?"
  3. Rest worth on what is yours. Epictetus, Enchiridion 1: opinion, pursuit, desire and aversion are in our control, "reputation" is not. Judging your own conduct is yours; others' approval is not.

This is related to self-doubt and not identical to it. These are rules for living and judging, and applying them to impostor feelings is a reading.
