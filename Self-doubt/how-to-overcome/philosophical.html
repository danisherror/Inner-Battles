Self-doubt

Doubting your ability or worth, and feeling like a fraud despite what you have done.

How to overcome it

The philosophers advise measuring accurately and then acting.

  1. Measure yourself correctly. Aristotle's fault is "worthy of less than he is really worthy of" (Nicomachean Ethics 4.3). The remedy is an honest estimate, neither above nor below the facts.
  2. Act for what you are for. Marcus Aurelius: "I am rising to the work of a human being. Why then am I dissatisfied if I am going to do the things for which I exist and for which I was brought into the world?"
  3. Rest worth on what is yours. Epictetus, Enchiridion 1: opinion, pursuit, desire and aversion are in our control, "reputation" is not. Judging your own conduct is yours; others' approval is not.

This is related to self-doubt and not identical to it. These are rules for living and judging, and applying them to impostor feelings is a reading.
